# Audit: Q-18, Stage 2 D-663's stress checks on P-14 (`results/p14_validation.csv`) (2026-11-17)

**Audit verdict: clean with caveats.** The file is what its manifest says. Every headline line I re-derived from the
CSV agrees with what the file reports. All eight identity lines in the run log held, and I rechecked the two engine
identities against P-14's registered file myself. The variant count matches the ledger. I found no choice made after
an outcome. The caveats (section 5) are omissions and unstated assumptions, not wrong numbers.

**What it shows, in plain words (section 6):** P-14 has **no positive trading edge to survive**. Its average trade
loses money at every risk size (`b1`, column `truth`, all twelve values negative). What the stresses test is the
other thing P-14's verdict rests on: whether the **money measure** (growth on the required bankroll, the bound arm)
stays above zero. It does, under every stress declared:
- reshuffling the days in blocks;
- missing trades and sessions;
- up to five extra ticks of slippage;
- pushing the assumed edge 0.16 R further down.

The money comes from the shape of the prop-firm contract: many cheap Combines, payouts from the few that pass. It
holds only under the assumptions the file leaves unstated (section 5.3), above all **0 % payout denial**.

Method: my own pandas and scipy code on the CSV, its manifest, P-14's registered file
`vendor/stage2/results/a3_brk30_cb20_gate.csv`, the run's resume file and the run log. Nothing was imported from
`extended/`, `scripts/` or `vendor/`. Nothing was rerun. No bar, no atlas file and no confirmation-window data was
read. The scripts and their output are in `docs/audits/scripts/q18/` (`rederive.py`, `resume_identity_check.py`,

## 6. What the stress checks show, in plain words

Each figure is the bound arm unless named. The point arm moves the same way in every part, by less.

- **First, what "edge" means here.** P-14's average trade loses a little at every risk size: `b1`/`truth` runs from
  -0.035838 R (`rr` 0.25) to -0.002807 R (`rr` 3.0, the funded leg), all negative. So there is no trading edge for a
  stress to remove. P-14 passed its gate because of the prop-firm contract. Over 504 sessions a typical path buys
  about 428 Combines (`a1`, registered row, `mean_combine_purchases` 428.438) and takes a median 76 payouts
  (`v1_payouts_per_path_p50`). Its median net cash is positive (`v1_net_p50` 51713.48), against a required bankroll
  whose 90th percentile is 3503.64 (`pf_required_p90`). The stresses ask whether **that** survives.
- **Days reshuffled in blocks, keeping streaks of good and bad days (A1): survives, almost unchanged.**
  - Growth stays between 1.360790 and 1.382039 at mean blocks 5, 21 and 63 (`a1`/`pf_growth_on_base_p50`), against
    the registered 1.378736.
  - The 90th-percentile bankroll moves by -3.55 to +152.84 dollars (`a1`/`diff_pf_required_p90`).
  - All these moves sit within about one Monte-Carlo error. P-14's result does not depend on the order of days, and
    A0 agrees: the days look like white noise (`a0`/`ljung_box_p_*` all above 0.2).
- **How trustworthy the registered confidence bound is (B1): if anything, far too cautious.**
  - In 200,000 reshuffled histories at each block length, P-14's registered bound **never once** claimed more edge
    than the history really had at its registered level (`b1`, era_rule, 0.05/370, `exceed` 0 in every cell). At
    0.05 it erred at a small fraction of the nominal rate (`ratio_to_nominal` 0.0033-0.0506).
  - The simpler pooled bound beside it is about right at 0.05. Under month-long blocks at the strict level it errs up
    to 2.9 times too often (`b1`, pooled, 0.05/370, `mean_block` 21, `rr` 3.0). But that is not the rule P-14 was
    judged by.
  - The verdict does not rest on the bound being above zero, so this part says little about the money.
- **How much worse the edge could be before the money stops (B2): more than the range tested.**
  - Pushing every trade's assumed edge down by 0.16 R more (8 steps) never brings growth to zero, under either day
    scheme (`b2`/`bracketed` False, `margin_at_least` 0.16).
  - But the user's experience gets much worse. At the last step:
    - growth falls to 0.135770 (`b2_curve`, independent, step 8, `pf_growth_on_base_p50`), against 1.378736 at step 0;
    - the 90th-percentile bankroll rises to 10778.91 (`pf_required_p90`);
    - 0.348 of the paths end below where they started (`v1_share_below_start`), against 0 at step 0;
    - the 10th-percentile path loses money (`v1_net_p10` -7848.76).
  - "Survives" here means the median path still grows. It does not mean every user is safe.
- **Missed trades and missed sessions (C1): survives.**
  - Dropping a tenth or a quarter of the trades, or missing a tenth of the sessions, keeps growth above zero in all
    nine realisations (`c1_summary`/`share_realisations_growth_above_zero` 1.0).
  - The lowest is 1.299415 (`c1`, drop25, realisation 1).
  - Dropping trades sometimes **raises** growth (`c1`, drop25, realisation 0, `diff_pf_growth_on_base_p50`
    +0.065022). That is what one expects when the trades lose on average.
- **Worse fills (SLIP): survives every declared level, at a cost.**
  - Growth falls at every added tick but stays above zero up to stop slip 12 ticks (`slip`, `A_stop12`, 1.015964)
    and entry and stop slip 5 ticks (`slip`, `B_slip5`, 0.870756).
  - At `B_slip5` the 90th-percentile bankroll is 5189.08 (`pf_required_p90`) and 0.012 of paths end below the start
    (`v1_share_below_start`).
  - There is no break-even level in the declared range (`slip_reading`, "none", both families).
  - The twin and slice lines D-583 also reads were not run (finding 1).
- **What none of this tests:**
  - whether the firm pays: every figure is at 0 % denial with the placement off;
  - the real order of days against the resampled paths (A2);
  - any part of the fill model other than slippage.

  P-14's money is the contract's shape. These checks show the shape is robust to the order of days, missed trades and
  worse fills, and that the money measure does not need a trading edge at all.
