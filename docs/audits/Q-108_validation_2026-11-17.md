## 3. Is the failure real? Construction and bookkeeping ruled out, as far as the files allow

**Its count is consistent across the k columns.**
- The counts are non-increasing in k.
- They are also non-increasing over all twelve level columns, sorted by level value. Its `count_a0.025`, `a0.01`,
  `a0.005` and `a0.001` are 2,850, 1,114, 679 and 230, all above `count_k13`.
- I checked the same twelve-level monotonicity on all 9,508 null rows of the file: 0 violations. **A mislabelled k
  column would break this ordering, and none does.**
- Its ratio rises from k = 13 to k = 14 because the level falls faster than the count there (97/110 against the ratio
  of the two levels). That is arithmetic, not an anomaly.

**It is in the rule.** The row has `in_rule` True, `group` subset, `kind` exact and `part` sub4. `exact_index` is 992
and `sample_entropy` is [20262127, 992], the plan's position for (subset z = 4, j = 5, `win1`, flip63).

**The denominator is right.**
- Every flip63 null has `denominator` 258,048, which is 63 x 2^12.
- My own count of the blocks touched at L = 63 on 657 sessions gives 11 blocks at 37 phases and 12 at 26, as the plan's
  section 1 table says. Its exact cells are therefore weighed in units of 1/(63 x 2^12).
- The flip42 and flip55 denominators (42 x 2^17, 55 x 2^13) and the alternating ones (2L) are also the plan's.
- **The direction matters.** A wrong weighting of the eleven-block phases, counting them once instead of twice, would
  understate a size, not overstate it.

**No duplication.**
- The 2,293 rule nulls have 2,293 distinct names and 115 distinct subset entropies.
- Each null's `entropy`, `subset_entropy` and `sample_entropy` are identical across the four candidate-scope rows, so
  the draws are shared.
- The log has exactly one per-null line for each of the 2,377 nulls, with no repeat. Every line's "Q x D y of N" equals
  the CSV's `count_k13` for `cons_m1.25` in both scopes and its `denominator`: 0 mismatches. The failing null's line
  reads "Q 110 D 0 of 258048".

**The orderings that shared draws force all hold** (every null, every one of the twelve levels):
- `cons` >= `cons_m1.25`: 0 violations;
- scope D <= scope Q: 0 violations.

On the failing null, `cons` has `ratio_k13..k20` from 3.85091 up to 6.38021, above `cons_m1.25` at every k, as the
margin requires.

**It is a property of that subset's trades, not of one cell.**
- The same subset (`subset_entropy` [20262122, 4, 5]) is the worst exact null of the validation under flip42 (0.25589)
  and under flip55 (0.46875), as well as under flip63.
- Its `win1` markov80 null holds the largest Monte Carlo count of the whole run.
- On `net3` the same subset is at 0 under flip55 and flip63.
- So one subset's `win1` values are bad under every persistent side, and the badness rises with the persistence. A
  bookkeeping error would not be organised by persistence like this.

**It is the plan's own mechanism.**
- By the plan's section 1 formula, the aligned patterns at L = 63 have a weighted count of 100 in these units (37 x 2
  + 26). That gives A(63)/level from 1.411 at k = 13 to 3.255 at k = 20, which matches the plan's table.
- The failing null's `count_k13` (110) exceeds that aligned mass. So on this subset the margin of 1.25 no longer keeps
  the aligned and near-aligned patterns out.
- This is the plan's 4.2 warning: a candidate that only just clears the calibration subsets may carry an unseen
  bad-subset rate.

**Why the calibration could pass it (a reading, my arithmetic).**
- In the calibration, 0 of 115 flip63 subsets were bad for `cons_m1.25`. Its worst ratio for any exact model is no
  higher than 0.197483 (`q108_cal_size.csv`, `cons_m1.25`, Q). In the validation, 1 of 115 is bad.
- At a true bad-subset rate of 1 %, seeing none in 115 has probability 0.315, (0.99)^115. At 2 % it is 0.098.
- So the two runs are consistent with a small bad-subset rate that the calibration's draws happened to miss. The plan's
  3.2 states its detection requirement at 2 %, and that requirement does not exclude a rate near 1 %.
- That diagnosis is Q-125's. Nothing here changes the verdict.

**Conclusion: the FAILS stands.** The failure is an exact enumeration, so it carries no Monte Carlo noise at the level
of the null. It sits on a null the pinned rule names, at levels the plan fixes. I found no denominator, duplication,
labelling or ordering defect that could produce it. Under the plan's 4.3 one null suffices, and under its 7 (items 2
and 3) nothing is re-chosen.

## 5. Findings, most serious first

**F1. E-159's description of the failing null is contradicted by the file (a typed characterisation, not a result
error).**
- E-159 says the failing null's size row "carries few non-tie sessions for its trade count, a heavily clustered
  configuration". `nontie_sessions_mean` on that row is 573.
- For an exact null the column is the number of sessions the subset trades in: `ds.traded.sum()` in
  `scripts/q108_calibrate.py`, read only for its meaning. On flips, all of a session's trades share one sign, so a tie
  comes only from an empty session.
- Across the 23 validation subsets of 5,000 trades, that column runs from 551 to 573, with a median of 560. **The
  failing subset has the most traded sessions of any of them**, and so the fewest trades a session.
- It also exceeds every 5,000-trade subset of the calibration (`q108_cal_size.csv`, the largest there is 569).
- Whatever makes this subset bad, it is not a shortage of traded sessions, by this column.
- **Why it matters.** Q-125's brief asks about "its sessions, ties and clustering", and a new plan might declare an
  admission or a weighting from that description. The sentence should be withdrawn or corrected before it is cited.
  The verdict is unaffected.

**F2. Choices that would come after the outcome: none made, three exposed.** I found no parameter, null, threshold or
range in the run that the plan does not contain. Nothing was re-chosen. The code and the plan are unchanged since
11c2082. But the files invite three post-hoc readings, and each would be "exploratory, not labelled" if adopted:
- **(a) A narrowed range.** The criterion holds at k = 19 and 20. Adopting `cons_m1.25` for k >= 19 only would be a
  range chosen after the look. The plan's 4.3 requires every k in 13..20.
- **(b) Scope D.** On these same validation draws, `cons_m1.25` in scope D has no failing null under the plan's 4.3. On
  the failing null `admitted_share` is 0, because flip63 is never admitted. But scope D was eligible only if scope Q had
  no candidate at the calibration (4.2). Switching now is a re-choice on validation seeds, which the plan's 7 item 2
  forbids. E-159 rightly makes no such switch.
- **(c) Dropping a value or a model.** The failure is on `win1` under flip63. Excluding either after this would fall
  under the plan's 7 item 4: nothing is dropped after it failed here unless argued from something else and labelled so.

**F3. Nothing chosen after the outcome in the run's own record.** The one change to Q-108 files between the calibration
and the validation, the two-line test fix in 11c2082, was committed before the run's start. It touches no run code.
