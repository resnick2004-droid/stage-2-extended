## 2. Independent re-derivation

**Method.** I wrote a scratch script that imports nothing from `extended/` or `vendor/` and reads only the CSV and its
manifest. The caps were taken by hand from the vendored config:
- the floor is `stand_aside.combine/funded.floor_ticks` = 45 in `vendor/stage2/config/fv3_corrected_portfolio.yaml`;
- the cap is the start room over the MNQ tick value. The room is `combine_mll` 2000.0 (= |`xfa_mll_start`|) in
  `vendor/stage2/config/rules.yaml`, the tick value is $0.50, so the cap is 4000 ticks.

Both caps equal the manifest's `parameters.caps`. A separate check (section 3) shows `lever_space.stop_caps()` returns
the same today.

**What the file allows, exactly.** The file has no per-day T(d), only the 11 `k_floor` order statistics per session.
These are the inverted-CDF quantiles of kmin(d) = F·TICK/T(d). Under the declared rule, kmax(d) = (C/F)·kmin(d) on
every day, and inside(k) = #{d: kmin(d) <= k} - #{d: kmax(d) < k}. Let m = ceil(q·n), and let kmin_(i) be the 0-based
order statistics. Then:

- **Contiguity is provable from the file.** In every session the least kmax, (C/F)·`k_floor`(p=0), is at least the
  greatest kmin, `k_floor`(p=1). The table below gives both. Below the least kmax no day has left the range yet, so
  inside(k) only rises. Above the greatest kmin every day has entered, so inside(k) only falls. The two regions
  overlap, so {k : inside(k) >= m} is one interval at every share.
- **k_lo = kmin_(m-1).** Under the same condition this is exactly `k_floor` at p = q, because the quantile index
  ceil(p·n) - 1 equals m - 1. So it can be re-derived **exactly at all 24 rows**.
- **k_hi = kmax_(n-m) = (C/F)·kmin_(n-m).** This is in the file only when some p in `PROBS` has ceil(p·n) - 1 = n - m.
  That holds when q·n is not an integer, and at q = 1 (index 0). So it can be re-derived **exactly at 16 rows**: shares
  0.9, 0.95, 0.99 and 1.0, through p = 0.1, 0.05, 0.01 and 0.0. At q = 0.5 and q = 0.75, q·n is an integer (n = 936
  and 944 are both multiples of 4). There the index needed (468 / 234 for n = 936; 472 / 236 for n = 944) falls between
  two written quantiles. Those **8 rows can only be bracketed.**
- **inside_at_lo / inside_at_hi:** the file proves each is at least m/n. The reported values equal m/n exactly, which
  means no tie at either end. That cannot be checked without the per-day values.

## 4. Findings, most serious first

No defect. Caveats:

1. **C1 -- 8 of the 24 `range_k` rows cannot be re-derived exactly from the file.** These are k_hi at shares 0.5 and
   0.75, all four sessions. q·n is an integer there, and the order statistic needed is not one of the 11 written
   quantiles. They are bracketed and lie on the T grid, but they are not proven. The declared row (0.95) is unaffected:
   all of it is exact. The fix is a CSV holding more quantiles, or per-day k. Per-day k would raise the licence
   question in C4.
2. **C2 -- `inside_at_lo` / `inside_at_hi` are only shown to be at least m/n.** Equality, meaning no tie of kmin(d) at
   k_lo or of kmax(d) at k_hi, cannot be checked without per-day T. Ties are possible in principle, since T lies on a
   1/80-point grid. Only the exact share at the end depends on this, not k_lo or k_hi.
3. **C3 -- `bounds("S1").lo` / `.hi` is an envelope across sessions in incommensurable units.** For each session k
   multiplies that session's own trailing range. ny's is the walker day's range (17:01 to 15:10 CT) and each overnight
   session's is its own window's. `_stop_width` returns lo = the least k_lo over sessions (ny's) and hi = the greatest
   k_hi (evening's). A consumer that searched one k over [lo, hi] for every session would put ny's stop far outside the
   caps. Today nothing outside the tests reads `.lo` / `.hi`. The design search must read `stop_width(session)` or
   `detail["by_session"]`. This is a property of the loader's whole-lever Range, not of the CSV.
4. **C4 -- licence.** The docstring says the file holds "no range in points". No column does, but each `k_floor` and
   each range end inverts exactly to a pooled order statistic of the trailing range in points: T = 11.25 / k_floor for
   kmin, T = 1000 / k_hi for kmax. No date, no percent and no per-day value is present, so I find no bar price
   recoverable, and E-179's rule ("a bar price can be recovered") does not reach it as written. It is still a statistic
   of the licensed bars. Q-121's equivalent rows are kept out of git for a different reason (the price recovery through
   range in points and in percent). Whether pooled range quantiles may be committed is the lead's call under E-179 /
   Q-130. This note deliberately types no value in points.
5. **C5 -- assumptions every use of the range must state.**
   - The caps are contract arithmetic before costs (`stop_caps`).
   - The cap assumes one MNQ micro's stop-out fills the larger start room ($2,000).
   - The floor is Stage 2's registered stand-aside (45 ticks).
   - The stop k·T(d) is unrounded. An executed stop on the tick grid may fall just outside a cap at either end on a
     boundary day.
   - ny's k is relative to the whole walker day's range and the overnight sessions' to their own windows. A k is not
     comparable across sessions.
   - At share 0.95, on up to about 1 day in 20 of the search years a design at either end stands aside, or its stop
     lies beyond the cap. The journey prices this itself (docstring).
6. **C6 -- small documentation and provenance gaps, carried from E-179's pattern.**
   - The run log has no provenance header (command, commit). That is the convention of every log in the runs folder.
   - The manifest's `data_manifest` hashes are copied from `vendor/DATA_MANIFEST_stage2.sha256`, not measured. They
     equal that file's entries.
   - No `results/variants.jsonl` line records the run (that file holds exploratory variants).
   - The docstring's "The file" paragraph says `k_quantile` holds "the quantiles of kmin and kmax", while the paragraph
     above it, and the file, hold kmin only (kmax = k_floor·C/F).
7. **Observation, outside this file.** `docs/plans/LEVER_TABLE_topstep50k.md` section 6 says "Q-121's `block` rows ...
   span the whole window and are not admitted". Q-121's file has only `block` rows labelled `2020-01-02..2023-12-29`
   (`band` 120, `band_mark` 17280, `range` 4). Either the sentence means RD-04 (a2) only, or it is inaccurate. I used
   the `range` block row only as a consistency check (section 2).

No check failed, so the failure protocol was not invoked.

## 5. What I could not verify, and why

  20-day trailing means, and the exact order statistics behind C1 and C2. The CSV holds no per-day T(d), and by design
  no date. Re-deriving them means reading the full search-year bars through `extended/reader`. CLAUDE.md classes any
  full-bar read as heavy (under `run.lock`, one at a time), and the auditor's brief is to read one CSV and never the
  bars. **Skipped.** If the lead wants it, it is a light-memory heavy job: about 360 MB peak in the producing run.
- That the builder did not look at real bars before the declared share was fixed. Choice-block reads are not ledgered,
  so no record could show it. Nothing suggests it: the build commit says "Not run: heavy", and no earlier result exists
  in any commit.
- The data files' sha256 (not re-hashed: `python scripts/vendor_stage2.py --verify` covers them).
- The `q121` and `a1_days` identities themselves. I checked their output (the log) and the pinned Q-121 file. ny's
  count also ties to Q-121 (947 = 944 + 3), but I did not rerun either identity.
