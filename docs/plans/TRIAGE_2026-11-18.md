## 1. The tests applied to each item

**Keep:** the item serves the new method unchanged.

**Recast:** a part of the item becomes one of the plan's parts.
- A **trigger or signal source**: when trades happen, with the direction a fair coin.
- A **lever**: a setting the user controls.
- A **component**: one session's trigger with its trade shape.
- **Test machinery or a measure.**

**Park:** the item's only purpose is a claim that a side beats the coin, which is no longer the pass line.

Under the plan (section 2, "zero-edge assumption") a signal's side carries no value. So:
- a draft whose question is "does this side beat the coin" is parked;
- a draft whose machinery decides *when* to trade, *how* to shape a trade or *how* to run an account is recast.

## 3. Q-126, Q-108 and Q-16: what serves the three-way test

### 3.1 What carries over and what does not

**Q-108 and Q-126 calibrate a different statistic from the one the plan needs.**
- *Q-108 and Q-126:* the lower bound of a mean of per-trade pair differences `x_i`, with session sums clustered in
  calendar blocks of 21, 42 and 63 sessions, sign-flipped under side-persistence models on P-16's judged offers.
- *The plan's three-way test (4.5 and 4.6):* a paired difference between two designs' journey measures (or the
  composite), across coin seeds, across years and regimes, and on held-out years. Its null is "two identical designs
  differ no more often than the level".

Three differences matter:
- **The unit.** The plan's unit is a start day's journey, which overlaps its neighbours over the measure's whole
  horizon: 63 sessions for P, 252 for month-12 measures. Q-126's unit is a trade, summed by session.
- **The source of the null's randomness.** In the plan it is the coin seeds and the market's history. In Q-126 it is the
  persistence of a side.
- **What carries the side.** Under zero edge every side is a fair coin, so Q-126's band check (2.5) and scope-D
  admission (4.4), which classify how persistent a side is, have nothing to classify.

### 3.3 The null calibration and the power check: proposed constructions from existing parts

**The null: two identical designs.** With paired seeds, a design run twice is identical path for path, so its
difference is zero and calibrates nothing. Two constructions give designs identical in distribution and still paired
on days:
- **(a) The antithetic coin twin.** The same design with every coin side reversed. Under a fair coin it has the same
  expected journey exactly, whatever the market's drift: reversing a fair coin's draws gives another fair coin's
  draws. For market entries the walked pair gives both sides from one walk (F-04 5.2-5.3). For resting entries the
  pair is exact only where RL3 5.5's same-bar mirror holds (F-04 NB-1).
- **(b) Disjoint seed sets.** The same design on two disjoint blocks of coin seeds: paired on days, independent on
  coins.

Both are run through the full three-way test at the declared level. The pass rule is E-052's criterion.

**The power check: a planted improvement.** F-04 7.3's value arms shift every executed trade's dollar P&L by a declared
amount: Stage 2's zero-edge-twin convention, with a full engine run, so floors, passes and payouts move. That is a
ready generator for "a planted improvement of the size that matters" (plan 4.6). The size that matters comes from the
composite's weights (Wave 4.2).

`family_stats.conjoint_mdl_grid` (`extended/family_stats.py:523`) gives the smallest planted shift at which a
declared share of draws passes **every** leg together. That matches the plan's "a difference counts only if it holds"
in all three legs. The pieces are `need_from_lowers` (`:487`), `mdl_from_need` (`:501`) and `conjoint_mdl_linear`
(`:542`).

### 3.4 Two feasibility warnings from these calibrations' history (for H3's design and its red team)

**1. Overlapping start-day windows bias variance low.**
- Q-16 found every batch-type variance biased low on P's start-day series, because each start's month-3 window
  overlaps its neighbours' for 63 starts (`docs/research/calibration_2026-11-15.md` 7.2, 7.4 item 3). `pcons` held
  but could detect almost nothing; `bm63c` was not adopted (E-085).
- Month-6 and month-12 measures overlap over 126 and 252 starts, which is worse.
- The years leg must be calibrated per horizon. A measure's horizon also bounds how many independent windows any span
  holds: a 252-session horizon gives few on the whole development window and very few on two and a half held-out years.

**2. Few independent time units cannot reach the family level by an exact test.** This is Q-108's section 1 and
Q-125's lesson, as arithmetic:
- An exact sign-flip test over B independent blocks cannot give a p-value below 2^-B.
- At k = 13 the level 0.05 / (13 x 14) = 0.05 / 182 lies between 2^-12 and 2^-11.
- So a time leg needs at least 12 independent units before an exact test can reject at that level.
- Q-126 1 and E-161 record that the judged block holds only 11 or 12 sign units at 63-session blocks.

**What follows, for the lead and the red team** (not decided here): the coin-seed leg can carry the level, since seeds
are unlimited. The years-and-regimes leg and the held-out leg can be **consistency conditions** (the sign holds in most
years, in both regimes, and on the held-out years), each with its own stated size. They cannot be tests at
0.05 / (k (k + 1)) on their own. The plan's 4.6 and 4.7 need that structure stated before H3 is built. My standing
memory note applies: the strict rule must be shown to have power, not only size.
