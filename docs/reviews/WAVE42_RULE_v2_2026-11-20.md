## 2. Blocking findings (MATERIAL; fix before the pin)

### V-1. The binding is only as good as the manifest the caller passes, and the arm is not bound

**Where:**
- `extended/wave42.py` 74-75 (`H4_MANIFEST_KEYS`), 226-246 and 309-310 (the binding checks);
- declaration 1.1, lines 56-83 ("an auditor checks one against the other", line 77; "Applied once", 79-83);
- 9, lines 436-439.

**Evidence.** The rule is pure: it never opens H4's file or its sidecar, which is right for a pure function. Every
check therefore compares the readings against a manifest that the caller hands in. The probes:

- **P2, a forged subset.** I built a manifest for the first half of the start days and all 62 seeds. Its `sha256`
  field is the real file's, and its `readings_sha256` is the subset's own digest. **It is accepted.** The output's
  `h4.result_sha256` equals the real file's sha256, which is the check section 1.1 tells the auditor to make. Only
  `h4.readings_sha256`, and the start and seed counts, differ from the real sidecar. The declaration asks no one to
  compare those.
- **P2b.** The same forgery on 10 seeds is accepted, falls below `H4_MIN_SEEDS`, and gives delta\* = 0.05 (the cap).
  At 62 seeds it is 0.030. That is a 1.66x change in the size the gate is sized for, chosen by the input.
- **P1, two genuine outputs.** Two outputs, an `off` reading and a `worse_1`-like reading (every spending +1 fee unit),
  each carry their own manifest, with an extra `arm` field. **Both are accepted.** B\* is 200 and 250. `arm` is not one
  of `H4_MANIFEST_KEYS`, so the rule cannot tell H4's deciding arm from a stress arm. H4's draft runs four arm-runs a
  baseline (`off` deciding, `off` as-walked, `worse_1`, `missed_0.1`; Appendix A).
- **P5, a second application.** A second application with `prior_applications=[]` is accepted. The output then states
  `"prior_applications": 0` (line 480), a constant.
- **The audit chain.** The mutant "input_sha256 ignores H4's manifest" survives the tests (section 4). The one digest
  that would expose a forged manifest is untested.

**What the declaration relies on.**
- It names no application script: no loader that reads H4's file and sidecar from disk, and no adapter from H4's units
  files to `BaselineReading`.
- The pin covers the declaration and `extended/wave42.py` only.
- The step where B-1's attack happens (assembling the input) is therefore unpinned, unbuilt and unnamed.
- H4's own red team (its B-4) asks H4 to name that adapter. E-254 ruled "one collection result for W42-R1". W42-R1's
  1.1 still says "the result file H4's clean run wrote".

**The failure it allows.** After H4 is read, someone runs the rule on a start or seed subset, or on another arm's
matrices, under a manifest they assembled. Each run is accepted and "applied once". The pinning row's cited result
sha256 matches. The selection happens in the manifest, exactly as B-1 described it in the input.

**Remedy** (all mechanical):
1. **An application script, pinned with the rule** (its sha256 in the same row), e.g. `scripts/wave42_apply.py`:
   - it takes the path of H4's collection result (E-254) and nothing else;
   - it computes that file's sha256 itself, and reads the sidecar from disk;
   - it builds the `BaselineReading`s by a named adapter whose round trip is tested bit for bit (float64 through CSV,
     NaN canonical);
   - it calls `setting_rule`;
   - it writes to one fixed result name through `extended.results.write_result`. That function refuses outside a clean
     run and never overwrites (`extended/results.py` 169-188), so a second application is refused by the file system,
     not by the caller's list.
   - `prior_applications` then becomes the script's own glob of `results/*.manifest.json`, or is dropped.
2. **Bind the arm.** Add `arm` (= `off`) and the verdict settings (0 % denial, placement off, Focused Trader Program
   off) to `H4_MANIFEST_KEYS`, and refuse anything else.
3. **Record the whole binding.** The output records the sidecar's own sha256 (its canonical JSON). The pinning row
   cites both H4's collection-result sha256 and its sidecar sha256. 1.1's audit sentence lists every equality the
   auditor checks: file sha256, sidecar sha256, `readings_sha256`, the start and seed counts.
4. **A test** that kills the "input_sha256 ignores the manifest" mutant.
5. **Optional hardening, cheap, independent of provenance.** H4 declares every search session a start and seed
   indices 0 to J - 1 (draft section 4 and section 7). The rule can then refuse:
   - start days that are not the whole calendar;
   - seeds that are not `range(nJ)`.

   A forged subset is then refused whoever builds the manifest.

### V-4. Choices 5 and 8: the composite gives a design that never buys exactly one third, and only a limit and a floor outside the composite stop it

**Where:** declaration 4.2, lines 244-249 (ruin's censored state "acceptable because the limit `not_paid_within_B`
reads run out plus censored"; bankroll's "money, speed, cost and steady oppose inactivity"); 4.3; 11 items 2-3.

**Evidence (arithmetic; P7 confirms it).** Take a journey that buys nothing and trades nothing: net 0, spend 0,
never passed, never paid. Under the composite form it scores exactly **2/6 = 0.3333 whatever H4 reads**:
- **bankroll z = 1:** lo is clamped at 0 or above, so a spend of 0 scores at the top;
- **ruin z = 1:** spend 0 is at or below B\*;
- **zero on the rest:**
  - money: not ahead;
  - speed: W + 1 is at or above hi;
  - cost: unpassed;
  - steady: no positive month.

A journey that buys one evaluation and quits at its first failure is close to the same, about 0.32: one fee spent, so
bankroll z near 1, and ruin 1.

On a synthetic set with rare payouts (pay odds 0.06-0.12), the baselines' composites are 0.323 (coin), 0.413, 0.467
and 0.482. **The do-nothing journey outranks the naive coin.** If all four baselines are weak under zero edge, it could
outrank them all.

**What stops it:**
- **The `not_paid_within_B` limit (required).** A do-nothing journey scores 1.0 there, and fails if the worst
  baseline's share is below 1. In P7 the limit sat at 0.972, so any design paid within B\* by W on 2.8 % of its units
  passes it.
- **When that limit is vacuous.** If the worst baseline is never paid within B\* by W, the limit is 1, flagged vacuous
  (line 384), and nothing in W42-R1 stops inactivity.
- **CHARTER 3.8 (c)'s long-run gate** (median log growth above zero) stops a do-nothing or quit-early design, but only
  at the held-out test. A low-activity finalist failing there stops the fixed sequence and spends k = 13.

**Answer to choice 8.**
- **Holding an evaluation is not rewarded.** A design that never trades but keeps its evaluation pays the subscription
  each month, and bankroll and ruin see it: P7's "idle subscriber" scores 0.
- **Buying nothing, or quitting at once, is rewarded.** Such a design gets a floor of about one third. Bankroll and ruin
  reward it, and cost no longer does (B-5 fixed that).
- **A guard is owed.** It is light, and it is mostly a reading.

**Remedy:**
1. **Print the null composites.** The output prints, beside the baselines' composites, the composite of the null
   journey (exactly 2/6 in composite form, 0 in the fallback) and of the one-evaluation quitter. Both are computable
   from the caps with no further data.
2. **Name the guards.** The declaration names the guards that stop inactivity: the `not_paid_within_B` limit, and
   3.8 (c)'s long-run gate. It states that if `not_paid_within_B` comes out vacuous, the pinning row must say that only
   the long-run gate stops a quitter.
3. **An obligation on Wave 5.** The finalist rule screens the floors' search-year readings, including the long-run
   gate's history-block reading, before it ranks by the composite.
4. **The stronger option, the lead's** (a rule change, not required): fall back to P_B alone if the null journey's
   composite is at or above the best baseline's.

### n2-1 (NOTE). Choice 1: `H4_MIN_SEEDS` = 62 is right for what it claims, and controls the safe error

- **The arithmetic holds.** 2 P(t_61 > 2) = 0.04996 and 2 P(t_60 > 2) = 0.05003.
- **Two-sided is right.** For one ranked pair of equal baselines, g = |mean difference|.
- **With four equal baselines it is conservative.** Ranked, the adjacent gaps are order-statistic spacings. In a
  simulation (20,000 replicates, `probes.py` P8) the false-resolution rate is about 0.007 per gap and 0.016 for any gap,
  at 62 and at 128 seeds.
- **The error it bounds is the safe one.** A false resolution makes delta\* smaller (a stricter gate). The harmful
  direction is a missed resolution with a larger fallback. `DELTA_MAX` bounds that, not this count.
- **The cliff at 62 matters only if the seeds can be chosen.** P2b: 10 seeds give delta\* 0.05 against 0.030. Under
  V-1's binding they cannot be chosen.

### n2-2 (NOTE). Choice 3: the joint (Bonferroni) z is correct, and costs almost nothing at J = 1000

- **It is conservative.** z_L = Phi^-1(1 - 0.2 / L) gives a design equal to each limit's best baseline a joint pass of
  at least 0.8 under any dependence. That is CHARTER 3.7's "every limit check" read jointly.
- **The loosening is small.** Against a per-limit z of 0.84 it loosens a headroom-bound limit by (z_L - 0.84) se_test,
  about one coin standard error (z_6 = 1.83). With J = 1000, se_test is of order 1e-3 on a share (P4).
- **The alternative fails 3.7.** A per-limit z with the joint power reported would not meet 3.7 when it is read
  jointly.
- **It stands.**
- **The history leg, carried from the first read's m-3 (b).** Because the coin headroom at J = 1000 is so small, a
  headroom-bound limit sits within about two coin standard errors of the best baseline. A design equal to the best
  fails it on any held-out history that moves the measure by more than that. The per-year estimates printed beside
  (5) show the scale. H3-D's synthetic histories will show whether the gate's limit clause survives. This is the lead's
  ruled position, and I record it, not re-raise it.
