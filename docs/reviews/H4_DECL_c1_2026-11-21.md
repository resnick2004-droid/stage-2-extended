## 3. Blocking findings

### RB-1. The coin's declared source cannot carry P-16's account policy, so I-5 fails by construction; its schedule side is unseeded

**Where:**
- 112: the coin goes "through the same registered account policy and the mix router (Q-169)".
- 164-165: the coin comes "from the trigger library (`triggers.CoinMarks`, `components.coin_component`,
  `components.schedule_day`, the mix router)".
- 197: the coin takes "P-16's registered job's ... target and sizing rule, its account policy (the strict rule)", and
  "I-5 holds everything else".
- 158-160: the schedules are "built once, before any outcome".
- 419-422 (I-5): "equals P-16's schedule path for path ... times, brackets, sides and the strict rule's routing to
  accounts".
- 582: the coin source's interface needs only an admission restricted to a mark list.

**Evidence:**
- **P-16's trades are unchained offers.**
  - `resolved_coin.py` 205-212 (D-525): "a coin entry is OFFERED at every `MARK_STEP` minutes ... and the engine's
    strict one-at-a-time router ... takes the next offer at least five minutes after the last trade resolves --
    re-entry when flat, no schedule".
  - Each trade carries every R of the grid: `cell_trades` 168-200 writes `r_R`, `exit_t_R` and `o_R` for each R.
- **P-16's strict rule is applied in the vendored routing, at each account's own R, with an account-state check.**
  - `shared_days.py` 357-359: a trade is skipped while any account's `open_until + gap > t`.
  - 378: `drw.can_take(h, j, *states[k])`.
  - 388: `open_until[k] = exit_for(k, j)`, the exit "at the R the account trades" (D-349; D-429).
  - P-16's R differs by account state: `prereg/P-16_rc-s150-all-nr.md` 33 and 39 (1.5 at the fresh Combine, 3 at the
    fresh funded account), and 159-162 (the no-closure table, need-aware Combine exits, one trade an account a
    session).
- **The coin's source chains at one R before any account is known.**
  - `components.schedule_day` 366-389 skips every offer whose live minute falls before `free_at`. `free_at` is the
    walked exit at the component's single `rr` plus five minutes.
  - `triggers.execute` 857-858 walks one bracket (`stop_ticks`, `rr`).
  - Q-169's `Router` (`router.py` 1-30) assigns accounts "longest waiting" by busy time. It reads no account state
    (no `can_take`, no account R).
- **The chain's side is drawn per unit.** `components.py` 371 executes each offer at `T.coin_side(unit, o)`. The
  `CoinUnit` (`triggers.py` 899-911) carries `seed`, `part`, `history`, `seed_index` and `start`, and the walked exit
  time and exit reason depend on the side.
  - Section 15 seeds no H4-S role, and its part ids are only H4-0 and H4-B (354).
  - So either H4-S draws from H4-B's role seed (H4's `h4b_coins` seed) out of its part, or the coin's "schedule" is J × n
    schedules.
  - In both cases, section 5's per-baseline, per-year supply and resolution counts are not defined for the coin, and
    I-2's nested-cut comparison of the coin's day blocks is not defined either.

**The failure it allows:**
- **(a) I-5 fails on the first busy mark.** With admission restricted to P-16's marks, `schedule_day` drops every
  P-16 mark that falls while its own chained position is open. P-16's trigger-level list keeps those marks, and it has
  at least one per open stretch by D-525's design. I-5 then fails at H4-S, the failure protocol stops H4, and the
  coin's mechanism is re-chosen after the schedules are seen.
- **(b) The coin is not "P-16's policy".** If I-5 were relaxed to make it pass, the coin would still differ:
  - its supply is pruned at an R the declaration does not name (the component's `rr`), not at the account's R;
  - its accounts are chosen with no `can_take`;
  - its trades carry one R where P-16's carry the table's R.

  The coin is likely the worst baseline, the one W42-R1's limits are set from (n-3 of the first read). So each limit
  would rest on an account policy no row declares.
- **(c) An undeclared seed decides the coin's supply.** Which seed draws the H4-S schedule side is a free choice made
  at build time.

**Remedy (declare before the pin):**
- **The coin's offers.** Every clock moment of P-16's window `all` (`resolved_coin.marks()`'s five-minute grid; T7
  off; L = 5; Q-166's void calendar; E-223's news convention), **unchained**, each walked at **every R of P-16's
  table**, as P-16's trades carry them.
- **The schedule side.** Each offer's as-run side is drawn once per (session, moment) from a declared H4 role seed
  (one of the spares, given the role "the coin's schedule side", independent of the seed index). The coin's schedule
  is then one per history, as P-16's is with `coin_table`. The J seeds vary only the redraw and the side of the
  as-walked reading.
- **One routing.** The strict rule is applied once, by **the vendored routing the registered designs use** (the
  `TradeRouter` with `one_at_a_time` at the account's R, `can_take`, P-16's cap and rule), inside the firm path. Not
  `schedule_day`'s chain, and not Q-169's router.
- **Appendix C** names this interface for the coin source and the firm path. Replace the word "mix router" at 112,
  164-165 and 197.
- **I-5 becomes:** the coin's unchained offer list, at P-16's offered marks, latency 0, with P-16's `coin_table`
  sides and `_nr` news rule, equals P-16's trigger-level trades by `trades_hash` on the search years. The routing then
  needs no separate identity, because I-4 already holds the firm path's routing to the vendored replay's.
- **Alternatively,** keep `schedule_day` and declare the coin a different account policy (its R named, Q-169's
  router named) with its own reason, and drop "P-16's account policy" and I-5's routing clause.

### RB-2. The overlap count is defined on the schedules, where the registered designs overlap by construction; and the gap is not counted

**Where:**
- 166-170: the count is taken "**from the schedules alone, before any outcome** ... **the positions open at once** (on
  the run's entry and exit times, the three-way note's N-4 definition), which must be 0".
- 213: "H4-S's overlap count must be 0 (refusing)".
- 271: the floor.
- 304: H4-S kill: "so does an overlap count above 0".
- 442: `results/h4_supply.csv` holds the overlap count "per baseline and search year", a schedule-level output.

**Evidence:**
- **P-15's and P-16's trigger-level trades overlap by design.** They are an offer every five minutes of the trading
  day (RB-1's evidence), so hundreds of offers a session hold positions that overlap in time. The registration
  counts them over the window at P-16 line 33.
- **The 1 % residue.** Even D-521's older fixed-slot rule allowed overlap on up to 1 % of days ("resolved before the
  next opens on at least 99 % of days", `resolved_coin.py` 25-28).
- **P-14's trades are pruned in routing too.** D-429 (1) says the strict rule holds each position "to its exit at its
  account's R -- the actual exit, not T23's pre-filter at the slowest one, which with targets to 3:1 would drop most
  trades".
- **The exit time of a trigger-level trade is not unique.** It depends on the R of the account that takes it, which
  depends on that account's state, an outcome. So "the run's exit times" of a schedule are not defined before any
  outcome.

**The failure it allows:**
- **(a) Counted as written.** Counted on the schedules before any outcome, the count is above 0 for P-15 and P-16
  (and very likely for P-14), so H4-S's kill stops H4 on every registered baseline. The count's definition is then
  re-chosen after it has been read.
- **(b) Counted on the run.** Counted instead on the firm path's taken trades, it is computed by the code that
  enforces the rule. It can only catch a defect if it is computed independently, from the per-trade log.
- **(c) The gap.** In either reading, "positions open at once" does not test the strict rule's five minutes flat. A
  firm path that freed an account at the exit, instead of at exit + 5, would pass the count. E8 (the gap dropped from
  section 3's row) survives T-0.

**Remedy:**
- **On every unit's taken trades.** Define the refusing count per unit (start, seed) and per arm, on the firm path's
  per-trade log. It is recomputed by the auditor's step 6 from the log alone, not by the firm path. It counts any two
  taken trades of the user whose intervals [fill bar open, exit bar end + 5 min) intersect, each trade's exit being
  the exit at the R it was taken at. It must be 0.
- **On the schedules.** Keep a schedule-level count *beside*: the trigger-level offers inside another's interval at
  the largest R, which is the supply the strict rule prunes, counted and never a kill.
- **Rewrite 166-170, 271, 304 and 442 to match, and pin those sentences in T-0.**
