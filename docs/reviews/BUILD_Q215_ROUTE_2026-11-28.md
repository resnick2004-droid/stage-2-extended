# Red team: Q-215, the survey's real-bar route and the survey's W42-R2 binding (wt/wt-survey d64aced)

**Verdict: BLOCK.** I found one blocking defect. The route's trade table gives every trade an outcome at **every**
component's w, and nothing ties a row to its own w except the aux column `rr`, which no consumer reads. The evaluation
takes each trade's R from the engine's policy. So a multi-component design can be raced at another component's target
and nothing refuses it. Everything else holds or is minor: look-ahead, latency, stop-first, the lever partition, the
refusals, the seeds, the held-out guard, the frozen files and the W42-R2 rebinding. Under E-207 (2) the corrected text
gets a full re-read.

Scope: `git diff master...wt/wt-survey` (merge base 5ae351d, one commit d64aced): `extended/survey_route.py`,
`extended/survey.py`, `identity/survey_route_identity.py` and the tests. Background I read: E-290 (3), E-294, E-296 and
E-299; `prereg/DRAFT_F-13_topstep_survey.md` at df17505 (a draft, read only for what the route must serve);
`extended/design_trades.py`, `h4_coin_source._format_cell_trades`, `three_way_baseline`, `s1_range`, `search_space`,
`components`, `triggers.P14Breakout` and `playbook`. The brief lists the worker's open points without arguing for an

## 1. Blocking

### B-1 (blocking): each trade carries live outcomes at other components' w; the evaluation picks R from the engine, not from the trade's component

- **Where.** `extended/survey_route.py:428-434` (`rr_grid`: the design's grid is every component's w). Line 493 walks
  every slot at that whole grid. Line 504 writes the row's own w only into `aux["rr"]`. Lines 528-537 (`facts`) give
  each `TradeFacts` `kinds` at every R of the grid. In `extended/h4_coin_source.py:273-292`, `r_/g_/o_/exit_t_<R>` are
  filled for every R. The base `exit_t` and `flat` are taken at `fresh_rr`, the engine's combine R, not at the row's
  own w.
- **Evidence.** A synthetic check on the tests' own three-component point (London P-14 w = 1.5, NY AM RL3 w = 1.0,
  NY PM P-14 w = 2.0; `tests/survey_route_world.py`; script in my scratchpad, not committed) gave this. Every row whose
  own w is not R has a finite `r_<R>`: 537 of 537 at R = 1, 85 of 85 at R = 1.5 and 508 of 508 at R = 2. The first
  trade's facts carry kinds at [1.0, 1.5, 2.0], although its own w is 1.5.
- **Why it matters.** The race reads a trade at `R = lt["rr"]`, the vendored draw's choice from its state policy
  (`extended/three_way_baseline.py:436-443`). It refuses only when the trade "carries no kind at R". `design_trades`'
  own docstring (lines 3-5) names that consumer: the vendored `rc_campaign` job and `TB.simulate(..., outcome="race")`.
  Nothing in the route, and nothing yet in the survey, reads `aux["rr"]`.
- **The failure it allows.** Take the design above. Wherever the engine's policy asks for R = 2.0, the London trades
  are raced and booked at NY PM's target, floor(2.0 x s), not at their own floor(1.5 x s). S2 is silently not the
  design's lever. The surrogate then learns a w the design never had, and the finalist's playbook states a target the
  evaluation never used.
  - The single-component case fails the other way. The grid is (w,). `zero_edge_decomposition.pad` (vendored
    `scripts/zero_edge_decomposition.py:188-197`) pads the policy's other R as NaN, and "an account whose state asks
    for it takes no trade". Every trade can go untaken, and the design reads as doing nothing.
- **Remedy.** Do one of these, and add a test that fails on the present code:
  - (a) In the route, restrict each row to its own w. Set `r/g/c/mae/exit_t/o_<R>` to NaN for every R that is not
    the row's own w, take `exit_t` and `flat` at the row's own w, and give `facts` kinds only at that w. A read at
    another R then refuses at `TB._law`'s "carries no kind" instead of passing.
  - (b) Keep the full grid, but write the contract into the route's docstring ("a consumer reads each row at
    `aux.rr` and nowhere else"). Then add a refusing check where the runner takes the route's table: every taken
    trade's R equals its row's `aux.rr`, with a planted mismatch that fails it.

  Either way, S2's mapping onto the engine's R needs one stated rule in F-13 before its pin. The policy's R is
  account-state driven (A1, A2), and S2 is per component. Today the text says neither how they combine nor which one
  governs.

## 2. Minor

Each of these is fixed and only its diff is checked. None changes a declared rule or a number in this build. Items
m-1 and m-3 carry a condition on F-13's pin; it is noted on each.

- **m-1: S1's rounding is the worker's choice, and nothing declares it yet.**
  - **Where.** `survey_route.py:391-404` and `vol_descriptive.py:351-356`.
  - **The rule as built.** The stop is k x T(d) rounded half up to whole ticks, at least one. The caps are read on the
    unrounded value.
  - **Assessment.** The caps-on-unrounded half is right: it is `s1_range`'s declared translation (`s1_range.py`
    docstring, "k x T(d) points, unrounded", both edges inclusive; `k_bounds`, lines 300-307). Rounding cannot leave
    the caps, because they are whole ticks, so the `raise` at line 403 is unreachable, which is correct. Half up is
    sound arithmetic, and a resting stop needs a tick.
  - **What is missing.** Neither the lever table's S1 row (`docs/plans/LEVER_TABLE_topstep50k.md:68`) nor F-13's S1
    row (draft line 328) states a rounding rule.
  - **Remedy.** F-13's S1 row should name it as fixed arithmetic (half up, whole ticks, caps on the unrounded value),
    citing `band_ticks`.
  - **Condition for F-13.** The playbook writer takes one fixed `stop_ticks` per component (`playbook.py:111`, 857).
    It cannot state "k x T(d), half up, stand aside outside the caps". The finalist's instructions would then differ
    from the trades that were evaluated. This must be closed before F-13's pin; it is outside this build.
- **m-2: RL3's kind and cutoff, and the side rules, are the caller's and are not recorded.**
  - **Where.** `survey_route.py:214-289`, 322-326.
  - **Assessment.** Passing them as declared parameters, not levers, is right: F-13 fixes RL3's kind (prior-session
    levels) and the `fade` rule.
  - **What is missing.** Neither goes into `Route.provenance` or `design_id` (line 289). Two runs with different
    cutoffs give the same design id and the same provenance.
  - **Remedy.** Write `source_params` and `side_rules` into `provenance`. The runner's factory parameters, which the
    survey's part binding hashes, must carry them. F-13 must state their values.
- **m-3: T7 `admit` is refused by name, and F-13 still searches it.**
  - **Where.** `survey_route.py:245-249`; F-13 draft line 326 searches T7 over `off` and `admit`.
  - **Assessment.** Refusing by name is the correct behaviour; nothing is defaulted. But about half of F-13's space
    cannot be routed.
  - **Condition for F-13, before its pin.** Either narrow T7 to `off` by a ruling (as E-297 (2) narrowed T1), or wire
    `t7_forecast` into the route with its own identity. The runner must also never turn a `RouteRefused` into a
    recorded "refused cell" that the surrogate learns as infeasible: that would narrow the space without declaring it.
- **m-4: mixed per-row stops (and fees) have never gone through the vendored pad, bounds or gate path.**
  - **Assessment.** It is probably sound. Vendored `score_fv3_grid.gate_blocks` reads `stop_ticks` and `fee_micro_eq`
    per row (`scripts/score_fv3_grid.py:1205`, 1214), and A3's atlas tables carried several stop rungs. Still, it is
    unchecked.
  - **Remedy: a light synthetic check before the runner consumes the route.** Run `zd.pad` and `gate_blocks` on
    `SR.build`'s synthetic table (`survey_route_world.point`, 47 distinct stops). Assert that each block trade's
    `risk_per_contract` equals S x tick value, its fee equals the row's, and `early_look.check_alignment(blocks,
    SR.facts(...))` holds. This takes seconds and reads no bars.
- **m-5: the heavy identity checks less than "the route equals the early look's spec".**
  - **Where.** `identity/survey_route_identity.py:60`, 144-183; `tests/test_survey_route_identity.py:70-84`.
  - **What I-2 compares.** Fills, sides, alert sigma, tags and resting flags. It compares neither the stop per day nor
    any outcome column. The docstring says so honestly.
  - **What I-2 cannot catch.** A wrong stop, a wrong outcome or a wrong w on real bars (the light test covers only
    one stop group, on synthetic bars). The only planted disagreement in its light test leaves `fills_equal` True, so
    the fill comparison itself is never shown to fail.
  - **What I-2 rebuilds.** It rebuilds the early look's spec itself at the route's one-w grid, not through
    `early_look.build`. It ties the rebuild to the record only by `counts` and `trades`, although the record also holds
    `trades_sha256`.
  - **On I-1.** `walked_status_disagreements` is printed but not part of `holds`. Today a disagreement would break the
    trailing equality anyway.
  - **Remedy.**
    - Rebuild the reference at `A.rrs(cfg)` exactly as `early_look.build` does, and require the record's
      `trades_sha256` (`SC.trades_subhash(zd.pad(...), last=LAST)`).
    - For every stop group of the route, compare the route's whole frame with `design_trades.build_trades(rl3_spec(...,
      s))` on that group's days, column for column. This is cheap and in the same run.
    - In the light test, plant a route-side fault (side `break`, or a window that misses 09:00 on some dates) and show
      that `fills_equal` goes False.
    - Put the walked-status count into I-1's `holds`.
  - **With those changes, can it fail?** I-1 can fail now (its light test plants seven mis-kinded days, and the child
    process has a look-ahead mutant). I-2, with the changes, can fail on the stop and on the outcomes too.
- **m-6: the docstring claims `components.Design`'s convention, but the route takes only its window filter.**
  - **Where.** `survey_route.py:27-33`.
  - **What is not applied.** `components.schedule_day`'s chain (one position at a time, five minutes flat between
    trades, busy until the last working stretch ends, and on a same-bar tie the trade worse for the user, the M-6
    ruling).
  - **What the route does instead.** It is an unchained offer list, as H4-B's naive coin is. Within one P-14 slot every
    breakout event is its own one-cancels-other group, and all of them fill. RL3's same-bar tie is `design_trades`'
    lower `sub`, not the worse trade.
  - **Assessment.** This matches H4-B and F-13 section 5 (the router applies D-429; the book check refuses overlap), so
    it is not a defect. But the text claims otherwise.
  - **Remedy.** Say that the chain is the router's, and that the one-cancels-other tie rule is `design_trades`'.
- **m-7: two survey docstrings still name W42-R1's table.**
  - **Where.** `survey.py:61` and `survey.py:225` say "`wave42.REQUIRED`".
  - **Why it matters.** That is W42-R1's table, which lacks `net_month_12`. The code reads W42-R2's `REQUIRED` through
    the rule object, so a reader of the text would expect the wrong keys.
  - **Remedy.** "W42-R2's `REQUIRED` (`wave42_r2.REQUIRED`)".
- **m-8: small refusals and one source for the tick.**
  - **The tick.** `build` calls `stops(rt, trail, caps)` (line 470), which has no `tick`. `stop_on` defaults to
    `W.TICK`, and `band_ticks` uses `V.TICK`, while the walk uses the `tick` argument. NQ's 0.25 makes them agree, but
    there should be one source.
  - **The overrides.** The `trail` and `caps` overrides (line 461) are unchecked: nothing checks the length against
    the history, or that the caps equal `LS.stop_caps()` outside tests.
  - **Missing S2.** A space without S2 crashes with a `TypeError` at `float(g("S2"))` (line 338), not a
    `RouteRefused`.
  - **`decisions`.** With `trail` passed, `decisions` skips `check_search_days`. The calendar still refuses a held-out
    day, so this is only belt and braces.
  - **Remedy.** Refuse each by name.
- **m-9: merging.** Master's Q-226 fixture in `tests/test_survey.py` (after 5ae351d) meets this branch's edits to the
  same file. Run the full `tests/test_survey.py` once on the merged tree.

## 3. What I tried that found nothing

- **Look-ahead.**
  - **The trailing range.** It uses earlier days only (`survey_route.py:377-388` uses `s1_range.trailing_ranges`).
    The roll-by-one mutant, which reads the day's own range, is caught by the shift test and by the child process's
    comparison with `s1_range.add_trailing` on the vendored walker arrays.
  - **The shift test's clock.** `ts_of_slot` gives the bar's open stamp (`synthetic_bars.ts_of`: 17:00 onwards belongs
    to the evening before). `replace_from` replaces stamps at or after t, and the arrays are indexed by bar end. So the
    bar closed at t (stamped t - 1) is kept, and the records compared are those with alert <= t, plus the day's stop
    record.
  - **Limit level.** The mutant that reads the limit level at its alert's bar is caught.
  - **P-14's alerts.** They are the window less L, clipped; the route's trigger equals `playbook._trigger_on` on 15
    windows, and the `p14_alerts` mutant is killed.
  - **RL3.** Alert 08:30 and live 09:00 are the library's.
  - **Sigma.** It is read from bars closed at or before the alert.
  - **Windows and contexts.** The windows come per date from calendar arithmetic (`session_spans`). The context cache
    key `(calendar_reads, i)` is everything `triggers.context_for` reads of a trigger (lines 652-661).
- **Latency, fills, stop-first.**
  - **Latency.** The test asserts that each live time is at or after decision + L, that each fill bar is after its
    live time, and that each order goes live inside its window on that date.
  - **Stop-first.** A planted two-barrier bar gives `s`, and its target-only twin gives `t`, so the test can fail.
  - **Trade-through.** The fill on a tick through the level is the library walker's (E-011), unchanged here.
- **Levers.**
  - **The partition.** `LEVER_ROUTE` is held equal to `lever_space.LEVERS`; the dropped and library classes are exact.
  - **Refusals.** Every refusal refuses by name, and each removing mutant stops the refusal.
  - **Firm-path levers.** They pass unchanged. A15 and A17 are held at their bases.
  - **Defaults.** I found no lever silently defaulted: a key with no handling, a fixed lever with no handling, a
    dropped lever and a T8 or T6 off the library all refuse.
- **Equal to `design_trades`.** The route's rows equal `design_trades.build_trades`' frame for frame on one stop group
  (exact). The facts carry each component's entry ticks (a mutant is killed). S7 is dropped (no move bar).
- **The W42-R2 rebinding.**
  - **No path back to W42-R1.** `_wave42()` returns an `ObjectiveRule` whose `W42_R2_NAMES` are `wave42_r2`'s own
    objects and never fall back (`__getattr__` raises). Its `__file__` is `wave42_r2.py`, so the sidecar's
    `rule_module_sha256` binds W42-R2's module. Nothing in `extended/survey.py` imports `wave42_apply` or names
    `w42_r1_values.csv` / `w42_r1_pin.json`.
  - **What falls back to W42-R1, and why that is safe.** The fallback names are constants, `Wave42Values`,
    `calendar_digest` and `null_journeys`. W42-R2's `OVERRIDES` change none of them. `null_journeys` lacks
    `net_month_12`, and `load_objective` fills it with zero, the do-nothing journey's net.
  - **The binding test.** It plants the old script, rule, values name, pin file and modules one at a time and refuses
    each. It also refuses a W42-R1 values file under W42-R1's name, and a sidecar naming W42-R1's script or rule id. I
    ran it with two other rows tests: 3 passed.
- **Frozen files.** `git diff --name-only 5ae351d d64aced` touches none of W42-R1's `IMPORTED_FILES`, nor
  `extended/wave42_r2.py`, `scripts/wave42_r2_apply.py` or `docs/plans/WAVE42_SETTING_RULE_R2.md`. E-299 (3)'s freeze
  holds for this commit.
- **Seeds.** The route draws no randomness. The tests' `SEED_ROUTE_BARS = 4215071` and `SEED_ROUTE_SHIFT = 4215072`
  occur as whole words only in `tests/survey_route_world.py`, on every local and remote ref and in every worktree. The
  shift seeds are `SeedSequence` children, and the bars and the shift are different roles.
- **The held-out years and the confirmation window.** `build`, `trailing` and `history_from_arrays` refuse any day
  after 2023-12-29 (the confirmation window by name). The identity reads inside `search_cut(last=2023-12-29)` after
  `authorise`, and `check_calendar` is applied.
- **Light tests.** `tests/test_survey_route.py` and `tests/test_survey_route_identity.py`: 35 passed in 30.8 s
  (synthetic only). I did not run the 21-minute `tests/test_survey.py` in full; see m-9.
- **The coin twin's lift.** Nothing in this build leans on it. `mirr_g_*` is carried beside only.
