## Findings, most serious first

### MATERIAL 1 -- The tie state (m9) lets a persistently long design through scope D's admission (4.4, "The check")

**The evidence.**
- The second text makes a tied session its own state, 0, in the run sequence. That is what my m9 asked for, and it
  was wrong.
- A design long on every odd session and taking both sides on every even one has states +1, 0, +1, 0, ...
  - R = 657 and E = 657 (H). It is admitted.
  - Yet its net side is never short.
  - With ties dropped, the first text's rule, the same design has R = 1 (H).
- More generally, any long-biased design with some balanced sessions scattered through the block gets about twice as
  many runs as it has tied sessions.
- Its trades on the long sessions carry the drift credit for the whole block (E-035's note 8). That is the failing
  class.
- No null in the rule has a persistent net side with interleaved ties, so the procedure calibration cannot see this.
  - The flip and alternating nulls never tie.
  - The coin null's ties are random.
- 4.4's "long-only and short-only designs, which hold one state throughout" is then true only for designs that never
  tie. The first text's "a side series with a single side throughout a block is not admitted" was removed with the
  same change.

**The failure it allows.** A long-biased design with balanced sessions is admitted to scope D. It is then judged by a
statistic calibrated on no null of its kind, with the drift credited.

**The change.**
- Compute R (and E, MATERIAL 2) on the traded sessions whose net side is not zero, ties dropped, as in the first
  text.
- Restore "a side series with a single side throughout is not admitted". R >= 13 implies it, but keep it explicit.
- State in "What it excludes" that designs balanced in most sessions are not admitted, because their non-tie series is
  short.
- Map the reversal of m9 in section 9.

### MATERIAL 2 -- The dominant-run case is neither measured nor enforced, and the declaration condition was dropped without a row (4.4, "R or the effective count"; section 7 item 6; section 9)

**The evidence.**
- The first text's admission had three conditions, the first being "its family declares (E-035 (6)) no side
  persistence of a quarter (63 sessions) or longer". The second text has no such condition, and section 9 does not
  record the removal.
- 4.4 says the uneven case R misses "is measured, not argued away", because "the Markov models' runs are geometric",
  and that "a side with one very long run and a dozen short ones persists past a quarter, which is outside both scopes
  (section 7, n3)".
- Neither part holds:
  - **Measured?** Among Markov series that R admits, the longest run holds at most about 0.46, 0.53 and 0.51 of the
    traded sessions at D = 30, 50 and 80 (H). The fair-coin L = 21 series reach 0.52.
    - Consider a side long for 600 sessions with twelve one-session reversals after it.
    - It has R = 25, so it is admitted, with E = 1.2 and its longest run 0.91 of the sessions (H).
    - No model in the rule produces anything like it.
  - **Enforced?** Nothing keeps it out:
    - R admits it;
    - the declaration condition is gone;
    - section 7 item 6's "cannot use the one-sample line at all" has no mechanism.
- On the choice of R itself the argument is sound.
  - As the only statistic, E is too strict: E is about 0.71 R, and E >= 12.5 admits 0.38 of L = 21 fair-coin sides
    against R's 0.93 (H, in agreement with the plan's scratch check).
  - And E <= R <= the number of blocks, so 13 keeps every quarter-constant side out under either statistic.

**The failure it allows.** A design that is long nearly throughout, with a few brief reversals, passes the judged
check. It decides C1 in scope D on the whole block's drift.

**The change.**
- **Restore the declaration condition:** the family declares, under E-035 (6), its side persistence below a quarter
  before its pin.
- **Add a second, outcome-free condition on the same tie-dropped series: E >= 4.**
  - That is no run holding more than half the non-tie traded sessions, since a run of share s gives E <= 1 / s^2.
    The threshold is fixed by that arithmetic.
  - It costs nothing measurable: L = 21 fair-coin admission moves from 0.934 to 0.933, and Markov D = 50 from 0.658
    to 0.655 (H).
  - The bound s <= 1/2 is also about the most uneven admitted series the calibrated side models themselves produce.
- **Apply both conditions per replication in the procedure.**
- **Correct 4.4's "measured, not argued away".** The Markov models cover moderately uneven runs. E >= 4 keeps out the
  dominated ones.

### MATERIAL 3 -- The derived-seed guard as specified refuses on the present tree (4.1, "The runners' guard")

**The evidence.** The guard folds NAME + constant on the AST, and with a loop index added it assumes a reach of
10,000. Emulated on master's `.py` files (E), it flags three expressions, all in
`tests/test_features_lookahead.py`:
- `SEED_REPLACE + i` (line 82);
- `SEED_REPLACE + 100 + i` (line 109);
- `SEED_REPLACE + 200 + i` (line 153).

Each base lies within 10,000 below the calibration block, so the assumed reach covers both blocks. Their actual
reach is the number of cuts (tens), four days and the fixed days.
- The file is code, not a document, and is not on the allow-list.
- It is on master and every branch.
- So the runner refuses at its start. The builder would then have to change a declared rule.
- The hand check in 4.1 is right about where the derived range actually lies: it agrees with mine. It is the
  assumed reach that is wrong.
- The fold covers only names of the same file. A seed written as an imported constant plus an offset, as
  `R.F.LADDER_SEED + i` in RD-01's runner is, is not folded.

**The change.**
- Bound a loop index by its loop's literal extent where the AST shows one: `range` with constant bounds, or
  `enumerate` over a literal sequence.
- Where it cannot be bounded, **print** the expression with its file and line for a hand check, and record that check's
  reach in the allow-list. Never refuse on an assumed reach.
- State that imported names are not folded, or fold constants imported from this repository's modules.

### MATERIAL 4 -- 4.2's "P(HOLDS)" omits the fresh-subset exact part, so it is not yet E-105 (b)'s "chance of holding", and the boundary choice is where that part bites (4.2; 4.3; 3.4)

**The evidence.**
- The DP is correct (F).
  - The failure counts are 71, 63, 56, 50, 45, 41, 38 and 35 at k = 13..20, the smallest counts whose
    Clopper-Pearson lower limit is above nominal. That is E-052's "wholly above", at E-105 (d)'s range.
  - The DP agrees with a nested-binomial simulation of the criterion count by count, to within 2e-4 on four rate
    profiles: 0.99989 against 0.99989, 0.94638 against 0.94652, 0.74898 against 0.74917, and 0.99770 against 0.99771.
- But the quantity it feeds is not the chance of holding under 4.3:
  - An exact null that meets its calibration floor "contributes 1". Yet 4.3's exact nulls are on **fresh subsets**,
    and any one of them above nominal at any k fails the criterion.
  - A Monte Carlo subset null's calibration rate is taken to "stand for its validation counterpart", which is a
    different subset.
  - 4.2 says both things itself ("the validation draws fresh subsets, so a statistic's bad-subset rate is tested
    there, not by this probability").
- The choice is the most powerful level-eligible candidate: the smallest margin that clears every calibration subset.
  That is the candidate most likely to have a small, unseen bad-subset rate.

**How it plays out on the synthetic populations (G).** Same population, 115 + 115 subsets, flips at 55 and 63:
- **Weakest drift:** m = 1.25 is clean on both halves, its worst ratio 0.46 and 0.40. The boundary choice is safe.
- **Middle drift:** m = 1.25 is bad on 3 of the 230 calibration subset-models and 0 of the 230 validation ones; m = 1.5
  is clean on both.
  - With the halves the other way round, m = 1.25 would have been chosen and would have failed validation.
  - At its rate, about 0.65 % a subset-model, a calibration of 230 is clean with probability about 0.22. Validation
    then fails with probability about 0.78.
- **Strongest drift:** m = 1.25 is bad on 7 and 2; m = 1.5 is clean on both, at worst 0.63 and 0.14.

So a candidate whose bad-subset rate lies in roughly the 0.3 to 1 % band, which the detection requirement (2 % at 0.9)
leaves open, is chosen with probability about 0.1 to 0.5. It then fails validation with probability about 0.5 to 0.9.
The probability the rule computes does not see this.

**The failure it allows.** A boundary candidate is chosen at "P(HOLDS) >= 0.9" and then fails its validation on a
fresh subset. That costs a later round under section 7 item 4: a new declaration, new seeds, two heavy runs. It is
recoverable, but it is not the 0.9 the row ruled.

**The change** (the ruling is the lead's):
- Rename the quantity "P(the Monte Carlo part holds)", and say in 4.2 that it treats calibration subsets as standing
  for fresh ones.
- Write, beside each candidate in `q108_choice.csv`, its worst calibration exact ratio per exact model.
- Then rule one of two:
  - **(i) Accept the boundary choice**, with its risk stated in 4.2, and section 7 item 4 named as the recovery. This
    keeps the most power.
  - **(ii) Choose one grid step stricter than the most powerful level-eligible candidate** whenever that candidate's
    worst calibration exact ratio on any model is above a declared fraction of nominal.
    - In G, a fraction of 1/4 would have moved the choice from m = 1.25 to 1.5 on the weakest drift: one step, about
      a fifth more lift.
    - It would have protected the swapped middle case, whose clean half's worst was 0.47.
    - It would leave the middle drift's choice at m = 1.5 (worst 0.14), and move the strongest drift's from 1.5 to
      1.75 (worst 0.63). So it moves two of the three choices by one step.

A margin floor of 1/2 alone would not protect the swapped middle case.

### Minor

**m1. The detection requirement pools sizes (3.2).**
- 115 subsets per persistent model detect a 2 % bad-subset rate *pooled over the five sizes* with probability 0.902.
  That arithmetic is right, and counting per subset rather than per subset and value is right.
- But a design has one size. At a single size, 23 subsets see a 2 % rate with probability 0.37. The pooled guarantee
  amounts to detecting a rate of about 10 % at any one size.

The change: say "pooled over sizes" and why that is the unit, or raise the count at the sizes the mechanism favours.
The non-persistent models' 25 is argued (at least 131 sign units) and its 0.928 is right.

**m2. The alternating models' granularity (3.1).**
- Each has 2L equally likely configurations, so its exact size is 0 or at least 1/(2L): 1/90 and 1/110. That is about
  33 to 93 times the levels at k = 13..20.
- So its floor means **no configuration rejects**. It is not a level comparison.
- The configurations are the flip null's two alternating patterns per phase, re-weighted from 2^-B to 1/2.

That is coherent under the fixed-V framework: a no-skill regime filter at a random phase. Say so in 3.1 and 4.3.

**m3. n_c.** "Choice block: R >= floor((n_c - 1)/63) + 3" is fixed by arithmetic from the count alone, and it gives 18
at 1,006 sessions. But a family's stage A may read a shorter span: F-03's runs from 2021-01-04. Write "the sessions of
the span the family's stage A reads".

**m4. The guard's blind spot.** Imported names are not folded (MATERIAL 3). Until they are, add a line to 4.1's hand
check covering seeds written as an imported constant plus an offset.

**m5. Section 9's map** should record the removals: the declaration condition, the single-side sentence and the
tie-dropping rule. With MATERIAL 1 and 2 they come back.

**m6. Choices a builder would still have to make (question (g)).** Each needs one line in the plan:
- **The exact nulls' scalar-re-decision sample:**
  - the pattern order's encoding (which bit is block 0, and which sign a set bit means);
  - "rejecting" for which candidate, scope and k.
- **Index orders:**
  - the index i of the fixed-data streams' Monte Carlo nulls in `default_rng([s, i])` (stream, value and model order);
  - the same for the `flat` form beside, and for the count-persistent nulls beside.
- **The alternating model's block convention:** block id (j + p) // L, as `_flip_signs`, with "starting long" for
  block 0.
- **P(c) in scope D:**
  - whether the toys' MDL is taken over admitted replications only or counts a non-admitted one as infinite;
  - that the floors and the probability are computed on the procedure's own counts.
- **C8's table generator:** `default_rng(s)`, or `default_rng([s, 0])` with batches in turn.
- **An assertion** that every candidate's calibration counts are non-increasing in k, since the DP divides consecutive
  rates.
