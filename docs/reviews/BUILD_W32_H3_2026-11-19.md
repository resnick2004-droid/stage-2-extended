## 1. Blocking findings

### B-1. The race rounded down to whole ticks strands every failing account above its floor. **Blocking.**

**Where.**
- `extended/three_way_redraw.py:47-48` sets `LATTICE: int | None = 1`.
- `extended/three_way_journey.py:230-240` (`_barrier`) applies it: `step = 1 if t.law == "race"`, then
  `step * np.floor((Lt + SNAP) / step)`.
- The draft's section 7 row "the race's lattice" (line 120) fixes it as "whole ticks".
- Section 16.2 (lines 326-328) claims the runner's race equals BinaryDraw's "held by the build's test at no cost
  already".

**Evidence.**
- **The rule.** When the cushion binds (c - x_stop < l), the loss is L = floor(c - x_stop) ticks. That leaves a residual
  cushion of (c - x_stop - L) x tick value: under one tick, and above the floor's `EPS` unless c - x_stop is a whole
  number. On the next trade L = floor(residual - costs) = 0, so the trade is not taken (`taken = act & (Lt > 0)`).
- **What follows.** The account is never breached (`_settle` breaches at `bal <= floor + EPS`) and never replaced. On
  an evaluation it keeps paying the fee every billing period (`_replace_accounts`). On a funded account it sits
  untradeable.
- **The probe** (`probe_zombie.py`). Toy rules with mll = 103, tick value $5, l = g = 30, one trade a session, every
  race forced to lose:
  - **LATTICE = 1:** one loss of 20 ticks leaves the balance $3 above the floor. No account is bought in 63 sessions,
    and the cash falls by the fee every 21 closes.
  - **LATTICE = None** (BinaryDraw's fractional form): the first loss ends on the floor, and the account is replaced at
    every close. That is what the vendored lockstep does (room = the cushion, clip at -cushion, breach at
    B <= F + EPS; `vendor/stage2/propedge/campaign/lockstep.py:452-492`, as the third read checked).
- **How often.** With any fractional cost (every real commission), c - x_stop is almost never a whole number of ticks.
  An account fails only through a cushion-bound loss, so on the deciding reading **almost every account failure becomes
  this stranded state.**
- **How large** (`probe_zombie2.py`). A seeded toy with fractional costs:
  - the setup: mll $2000, a daily limit of $1000, $5 a tick, costs of $2.12 a side, l = g = 60, three trades a session,
    400 seeds, 63 sessions, B = $500;
  - **LATTICE = 1:** 0.23 replacement accounts a unit and P_B = 0.099;
  - **LATTICE = None:** 3.98 replacement accounts a unit and P_B = 0.158.
- **Why the tests miss it.** The toy rules use whole-tick cushions and costs, so they never meet it: their mll is 100 at
  $5 a tick, 20 ticks. `tests/three_way_vendored_draw.py` holds BinaryDraw equal to `race_dollars` at lattice None,
  and the journey never calls `race_dollars` (grep: no caller in `extended/`).
- **Under the daily loss limit** the same residual only ends the day, as `stopped` would, so that case is harmless.
  The harm is at the maximum-loss floor.

**Against the declared texts.**
- CHARTER 3.2 names "stage one's `adapter.BinaryDraw` form".
- The note's 1.1 says the barrier is L = min(l, c - x), "and fractions are allowed".
- Rounding inward to a lattice is N-4's rule for the *shared path* of overlapping positions, deferred to the template
  (1.6).
- The draft's justification, "a stop is placed at a tick" (`three_way_redraw.py:17-20`), holds for the stop. It does
  not hold for a cushion-bound loss, which is the firm liquidating at the floor, not a resting stop.

**The failure it allows.** Every unit whose account fails gets a journey no engine produces:
- no replacement purchase, and no fresh evaluation with a chance to pass;
- fees with no trading;
- P_B, the replacement count, the fees and every payout after a first failure all misstated.

Designs differ in how often, and how early, they fail. So θ(H) is a different estimand from the declared one, for the
design and for each baseline, and H3-D's power and H7's verdict would be read on it. The size checks would not show
it: both arms share the code, and the exact program shares `_barrier`.

**Remedy (re-declare section 7's row).**
- The race at fractional L = min(l, c - x_stop): `LATTICE = None` for the race, which is BinaryDraw's form. A
  cushion-bound loss then ends exactly on the floor and fails the account, as the engine does.
- For R-10's walk, which must live on a lattice of step s, the template declares what happens to the residual below
  one step. For example: a last race on the residual against the next lattice point, which is exactly zero-gross; or a
  fractional last barrier. That is the template's to settle, with its red team.
- Correct section 16.2's claim.
- Add m-1's oracle on the journey itself.

### B-2. The declared key guard cannot run at the declared sizes. **Blocking.**

**Where.**
- `extended/three_way_keys.py:153-182`: `KeyGuard.add` stores `states[generate_state] = key` and calls `key_state` on
  every read.
- `extended/three_way_journey.py:288-291`: every uniform read goes through the guard.
- `extended/three_way_runner.py:111, 123, 135-136`: one guard per call, over every start, seed and arm of a plan.
- The draft's section 9 (lines 164-165): "two keys with one state refuse the run". The note's 2.3 and 7 say the same.
- The admission paragraph's item 6 (the draft, line 212) makes the start check part of 2.2 (a)'s refusals.

**Evidence** (`probe_cost.py`).
- The guard holds 338 B a key. Its `generate_state` costs 18.5 µs a key, on top of 33.7 µs for the uniform.
- **A2** (Appendix A: M = 10000, |T| = 120, h = 63, four trades a session) reads about 3.0e8 keys an arm on one
  history. At 338 B that is about 100 GB, against a cap of 1.25 GB a worker (the note's 6.2).
- **H7's Hoeffding row** has 1.06e8 lane-trades: about 36 GB.
- Every part but H3-0 is out of reach by one to two orders of magnitude. So is the held-out test whose start refusal
  the guard carries.

**The failure it allows.** The part cannot be run as declared. The obvious workaround, passing `guard=None` (which
`simulate` accepts), silently drops a declared refusal on the run that matters. Dropping it is a change to a declared
rule, made at run time.

**Remedy (re-declare section 9 and the note's 2.3 and 7 in one row).**
- **N-2 per key, unstored:** `Unit` builds every coin and enforce key with its start. Assert the start word at
  construction.
- **Distinctness by construction:** one fixed length per kind, distinct nonzero tags, every word below 2^32, and the
  moment injective in (session, slot, sub). Two keys are then distinct inputs to `SeedSequence`, and the chance that
  any two of n keys share a 128-bit state is at most n^2 / 2^129. That is below 1e-17 at n = 1e11, and it is printed
  beside.
- **A tripwire:** the stored `generate_state` comparison on a declared sample, for example every key of the first
  start of the first history of each part, across all its arms.
- The refusal of a disjoint pool sharing a key becomes a check on the two seed-index sets. That check is already exact
  (`three_way_runner.py:133`).

**Where.**

  - "the toy rules ... fixed, stylised, no firm's | `three_way_journey.ToyRules`, the values written in each part's
    manifest";
  - "S1's parameters ... stylised in H3-A", with no values.
- Section 11 (lines 248-254): "placeholder composites (`P_B` at a placeholder budget, and a clipped toy composite)".
- Never fixed anywhere: the designs (`DesignSpec`s) each part runs; J' (the note's 2.6); P4's grid of a; P2's lever
  changes; "the grid of sizes"; the subset of histories run in full for the shortcut's error (2.7); and the test behind
  the kill rule "a pool not centred".
- `ToyRules` has no defaults. `S1Params`' defaults sit in code (`three_way_s1.py:44-58`) but are not cited as the

**The failure it allows.** H3-A's kill rules (a laxity "recorded and diagnosed before H3-D", a premise "stops the part")
and its outputs ("size m and J'", "fix the generator's form", the note's 6.1) all depend on these values. Chosen at

CLAUDE.md requires every parameter to be classed and fixed before the run. The note's section 7 says "Each part's field
values are fixed in H3's declaration".

**Remedy.** Write every value into section 7 before the pin, or name the commit and the constructors, for example
`S1Params(n_sessions=...)`'s defaults at a named commit and a named `ToyRules` instance per part. Do the same for:
- each part's design and baseline specs;
- the placeholder budget and the toy composite's dimensions, clips, caps and weights;
- J', the plant grids and the shortcut's subset;
- the kill rule's centring test and its level.

## 2. Minor findings

### m-1. No test checks B-1's guard on the path that decides. **Minor** (fix with B-1).

- **Where.** `tests/test_three_way_redraw.py:179-202` checks `RD.race` and `race_net`, which the journey does not call.
  `tests/test_three_way_runner.py:126-132` compares the simulation with the exact program on a toy whose cushion never
  binds: l = 10 against 20 ticks of room, and the room never falls below 10 ticks before death.
- **Evidence (mutants on the scratch copy, every test file of the build run).**
  - **W:** `_trade_mc`'s `p = Lt / (Lt + g)` replaced by `p = l / (l + g)`, the loss still clipped at the cushion.
    This is exactly B-1's defect of the first read, a gross edge of g (l - c) / (l + g) per cushion-bound trade. It
    **survives**.
  - **A:** the race rounded down to two ticks. It **survives**.
- **The failure it allows.** A regression to the unraced redraw, the defect the first read's B-1 and the note's whole
  1.1 exist to rule out, would ship green.
- **Remedy.** An oracle on `_trade_mc` and `_trade_dp` themselves:
  - over a grid of fractional cushions and unequal costs, with forced uniforms on each side of p, the booked change
    equals `race_net`'s (in exact fractions, through a `Fraction` cushion or a tolerance of 1e-9);
  - the comparison of the simulation with the exact program on a toy where the cushion binds.

### m-2. No test checks the journey's walk branches. **Minor.**

- **Evidence.** Three mutants **survive**:
  - **B:** the simulation's time exit charged the stop's cost;
  - **B2:** the simulation's walk P&L without its step size s;
  - **C:** the exact program's walk P&L without s.
- **Why.** No test compares `simulate`'s walk with `expect`'s, or either with `walk_net`. The brute-force test has race
  trades only. The history-null test reads the exact program only, at zero costs. The S1 runner test asserts nothing
  about values.
- **Remedy.**
  - Compare the simulation with the exact program on a walk toy with s = 2 and unequal x_target, x_stop and x_flat.
  - Check that the exact program's walk step equals `walk_net`'s gross, net and worst change.

### m-3. A quantile can enter McDiarmid's bound through `stat`. **Minor.**

- **Where.** `three_way_stats.py:100, 112-118, 137-138`. `admitted()` reads `kind` only, while `functional()` uses
  `stat` whenever it is given.
- **Evidence** (`probe_holes.py`). `Dimension("m", "mean", 63, a=0, r=1, source="arithmetic", stat=np.median)` is
  admitted. On 51 starts x 4 seeds, the two arms differ by one start's row:
  - the estimate is 1.0, against the c_u of 0.0098 that McDiarmid's bound assumes;
  - LB = 0.716, which rejects.
- **The failure it allows.** A pinned composite coded this way would be decided by a bound that is invalid for it.
  N-1's "the build refuses" does not hold.
- **Remedy.** Refuse a `stat` on any dimension 2.2 (a) admits, or admit only `stat is None`.

### m-4. Coverage is shown only where the bounds are far from tight. **Minor.**

- **Where.** `tests/test_three_way_stats.py:124-146`.
  - The McDiarmid check runs at a miss rate near 1e-5 against an allowance of 5 %. A bound of half the width would also
    pass it.
  - The EB and betting checks use a mean of -0.92 with J = 40, where the range term dominates.
- **Evidence.** Mutant **K** makes the betting bound's variance estimate include the current term
  (`sq = np.cumsum(...)`), so λ_i is no longer predictable and the capital is no longer a supermartingale. It
  **survives**. Mutant K2 (the bound shifted 40 grid points) is killed.
- **The failure it allows.** A validity defect in the betting bound, one of the two candidates the smaller-J rule may
  choose, ships green.
- **Remedy.**
  - Check E[K_J(μ)] <= 1 at the true mean by simulation (a direct supermartingale test).
  - Check coverage at a configuration where the bound is near tight, for example at J = 2000 with σ comparable to the
    range.
  - Require one width-halving mutant per bound to be killed by the coverage check itself, not only by the formula
    checks.

### m-5. The generator's cost, and whether section 17's remedy is enough. **Minor** (a plan that cannot run as written).

**The builder's figure is low** (`probe_cost.py`, one thread):
- `K.uniforms(key, 1)`: 33.7 µs a key.
- `key_state`, the guard's own hash: 18.5 µs.
- `Unit.coin_key`: 3.0 µs.
- The runner: 43 µs a lane-trade with the guard off and 70 µs with it on (a race toy, 64 seeds x 2 starts x 63
  sessions x 4 trades).
- A walk trade of n = 810 steps (S1's defaults: σ = 6, T = 90, s = 2): 127 µs a lane-trade.

At 43 µs (Appendix A's lane-trades, over two workers):
- A1 takes about 108 h, D2 about 496 h, and H7's EB row about 360 h.
- Only H3-0 and H7's Hoeffding row stay under 8 h.

**Section 17's remedy is feasible** (`probe_vector_gen.py`). A numpy `SeedSequence` hash plus Philox4x64-10 is:
- bit-identical to numpy's first raw output on 4000 coin keys;
- about 0.9 to 1.0 µs a key in batches of 200000: the hash about 0.29 µs, one Philox block about 0.64 µs.

**It is not sufficient:**
1. **The guard (B-2)** costs more than the generator and is not addressed.
2. **Walks.** A walk reads n uniforms, n/4 Philox blocks, so about 130 µs a lane-trade at n = 810 even vectorised.
   Appendix A's model has one c per lane-trade and no walk term, so H3-0's toy c does not carry to H3-D. A real NQ walk
   can run to thousands of steps.
3. **Some parts are over the cap at any realistic c.** A part fits 8 h only at c <= 3.18 µs (A1), 2.38 (A3, A4),
   1.16 (D1, D3), 0.69 (D2), 0.67 (D3's full-J check) and 0.95 (H7 EB). So D1 to D3 and H7 EB must be split or cut
   whatever the generator.
4. **The admission test reads one output.** "Bit for bit equal to `triggers.coin_uniform`" compares only the first raw
   output (`extended/triggers.py:760-763`). See m-15.

**Remedy.**
- Section 17: the vectorised generator, plus B-2's guard, plus a cost model `c = c_race + share_walk x mean(n) / 4 x
  c_block` with both terms measured by H3-0.
- Declare H3-D's and H7's split sizes in the row after H3-0, as section 18 already says for H3-D, with the power cost.
- Consider drawing the walk's terminal state from one uniform by the inverse of `walk_law`'s exact distribution. That
  is O(1) a trade, and the antithetic null stays exact because 1 - U has U's law. It is a re-declaration of section 7's
  R-10 row.
- **H7 is read once.** A split of the held-out read needs a crash rule, declared before it starts (E-195's part
  ledger).

### m-6. O-5's wording and the headroom formula against E-230. **Minor.**

**The wording.**
- The draft's section 7 (line 133) says "inside each limit at the point estimate, failed only when the evidence shows
  it crosses".
- `three_way_stats.py:40-41, 449-451` says the same.
- E-230 ruled: "a design passes a limit when its point estimate is inside it and fails it when outside; the one-sided
  check ... is reported beside each limit and never a second margin".
- The code, `passes = inside and not crosses`, equals `inside` for w >= 0. That is E-230's form, so the text and the
  code disagree.
- A negative half-width fails a design that is inside its limit (`limit_check(0.9, 1.0, -0.2)` gives `passes: False`).

**The headroom formula.**
- Section 10 (line 239) and the note's 4.4 keep the headroom at (c_L + z_π) σ_M / √J.
- Under the point-estimate rule, P(pass) = Φ((L - M_best) √J / σ_M), so the headroom needed for power π\* is
  z_π σ_M / √J.
- Keeping c_L makes the power gate fail pinning rows that would pass, a strict rule without power (the operator's
  standing concern).

**Remedy.**
- Reword to E-230's form.
- Set `passes = inside`, report `crosses` beside, and refuse w < 0.
- Use z_π σ_M / √J in section 10.

- **Where.** `three_way_stats.py:477-481` (`planted_mdl`), and the draft's section 10 (P1).
- **What is wrong.**
  - lower(δ) = lower(0) + δ holds for Hoeffding and McDiarmid on unclipped terms, and for EB only while the shifted
    replicates stay inside [-1, 1].
  - It is false for the betting bound: its bet cap c / m moves with the location, so the capital is not
    translation-equivariant.
  - It is false for a clipped composite, whose scaled dimensions saturate.
  - The function accepts any `lowers0`.
- **The failure it allows.** An MDL read off P1 for the betting bound, or for a clipped composite, misstates power.
  That could feed the smaller-J rule.
- **Remedy.** Restrict P1 to the unclipped Hoeffding and EB forms by an argument naming the bound, refused otherwise.
  Run the betting form's plant through the bound itself.
