### F-1 (MATERIAL). R counts switches, not persistence: a nearly constant side with sparse one-session reversals passes every band, and 4.4's admission

**Where.**

- B-31, line 1008;
- `q126_arith.py:225` `band_interval`;
- 4.4, line 646 (Q-108's admission, carried "word for word in substance").

**The evidence** (deterministic; no seed).
- **The side.** It is long on every one of the judged block's 657 sessions except 8 evenly spaced one-session shorts.
  Its non-tie series has R = 17 and E = 9.02, and its mean side is 0.976.
- **The band check passes it:**
  - 42 to 50 needs 8 <= R <= 25;
  - 51 to 62 needs 8 <= R <= 21;
  - below 42 needs R >= 10.
- **4.4's admission passes it too:** R >= 13, E >= 4, and it is not a single side.
- **Q-108's 4.4 claim fails here.** Its text says E >= 4 "keeps the dominated ones out" (Q-108's plan, 4.4, "R alone
  misses the dominated case"). That is true only when the reversals cluster. Spread evenly, they cut the dominant run
  into many medium runs of the same sign, and E grows with R.
- **No class model looks like it.** I simulated it, with a scratch seed, as an illustration. On the class's models at
  657 sessions, the 97.5 % quantile of |mean side| is:
  - 0.58 for Markov-50, 0.65 for Markov-62 and 0.73 for Markov-80;
  - 0.55 for flip-42 and 0.65 for flip-63.
  - No class side reaches 0.95 more than about 2 times in 1,000.

**The failure it allows.**
- **The routes.** For the scope-Q statistics the band check is the only guard on persistence: the second statistic (2.5,
  first column, second row) and the scope-Q primary (second column, every row).
- **How the side passes.** A long-biased design, a few cheap opposite-side sessions added, or arising naturally, passes
  the check.
- **The size.** Its side has in effect one sign unit, so the aligned pattern carries mass near one half: section 1's
  mechanism at its extreme. In a drifting judged block, the line can pass it at many times its level. That is the
  "worse than no line" case on which E-182 rests the strict floor.
- **Gaming.** The same construction is how a family can game the check without reading any outcome: R-padding.
- **In scope D** the side is admitted, but no class null resembles it. Scope D's calibration "as a procedure" therefore
  says nothing about it either.

**Remedy (rule, before the pin).**
- **Add a balance condition to the band check.** The mean side |sum m_j| / (number of non-tie sessions) is at most b on
  the span read.
  - b is the 97.5 % quantile of that statistic under the class's models at the band's upper end (Markov at hi, and
    the flip at hi with independent block signs).
  - `q126_arith.py` computes b, the flips in closed form and the Markov side by exact recursion over the occupation
    time, so no seed is needed. T-0 holds it.
- **The lead's ruling: 4.4's admission.** Whether this plan also adds the condition to scope D's admission, as an
  after-the-look B-row. It changes the procedure calibrated, so it can be done only before this plan's run, never
  after.

  - If it is not added, 8's residual names this case for scope D.

### F-2 (MATERIAL). The intervals are Markov intervals, but a regime filter's runs are half its regimes: the check refuses the band's own lower-edge model, and "at most about 5 %" is false

**Where.** 2.5, lines 133-135 ("a regime filter's regime length, or the mean dwell of a switching side"), 144-147, and
160-167, 172-175.

**The evidence** (closed form; the class's flips carry independent block signs, which I confirmed below).
- **How a flip's runs fall.** A flip-L side touches B blocks, and its runs are 1 + Bin(B - 1, 1/2). So it has about half
  as many runs as blocks, and its mean run is about 2L.
- **The check against the class.** Its admission, R >= 13 at 657, gives 0.0305 for flip-42, which is section 5's 0.030
  (the same check reproduces Markov-50's 0.661 before E). The band check's pass rates are:

| side model | 42 to 50, judged (657) | 42 to 50, choice (1,006) | 51 to 62, judged | 51 to 62, choice | below 42, judged |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| flip 21 | 0.999 | 0.999 | 0.960 | 0.947 | 0.995 |
| **flip 42** (the 42 to 50 band's stated lower edge) | **0.744** | **0.575** | 0.744 | 0.922 | 0.364 |
| flip 50 | 0.513 | 0.260 | 0.513 | 0.754 | 0.143 |
| **flip 55** | 0.379 | 0.135 | **0.379** | **0.615** | 0.070 |
| flip 62 | 0.231 | 0.045 | 0.231 | 0.422 | 0.024 |
| flip 63 | 0.214 | 0.037 | 0.214 | 0.397 | 0.020 |

- **For Markov sides at the band's ends** (exact):
  - 42 to 50 fails 0.0209 at dwell 42 and 0.0251 at 50;
  - below 42 fails 0.0208 at dwell 41;
  - **51 to 62 fails 0.0485 at 51 and 0.0985 at 62.** At 657 the two-quarters floor (R >= 8) binds above the Markov-62
    2.5 % quantile (1 + 5 = 6), so the lower tail alone is 0.0957.
  - On the choice block, every end fails 0.015 to 0.021.
- **Line 173's figure.** "A Markov side inside its band fails the check with at most about 5 % (2.5 % in each tail)" is
  printed by no script and held by no test. My mutation of it to "1 %" passed T-0.
- **A comparison in mixed units.** Line 147 compares a Markov side's runs (14.1) with the flips' sign units (12 or 13
  at 55; 16 or 17 at 42). In runs, flip-42 has 8.8 and flip-55 7.0 on average. My own previous m1 wrote that
  comparison, so the error is partly mine.

**The failure it allows.**
- **Power.** A family whose design is a 42-session regime filter with signs like the class's is "not claimed" with
  about 0.26 at stage C. It cannot declare the band at all with about 0.43 at stage A. Taken as independent reads,
  about 0.57 of such families lose the line before any outcome. A 55-session regime family in 51 to 62 loses it about
  0.77 of the time.
- **Those are profitable models failed by a check whose cost the plan states as 5 %**: the operator's rule on strict
  lines. A trend-following regime filter, whose signs persist across regimes, fails more often still.
- **Size.** Nothing in the size direction: refusing more never raises a family's false-pass chance.

**Remedy (rule and text, before the pin).** Check each of E-035 (6)'s two declared kinds by its own model.
- **A regime filter** (declared regime length L):
  - the check reads the design's regime decision points, from sides only;
  - every change of side falls on a decision point;
  - the number of decision points on the span gives the mean regime length, which must lie in the band. Its tolerance
    is the phase arithmetic, since the regimes are declared;
  - F-1's balance condition applies.
  - R is not used. Under independent regime signs a side with runs over two quarters is an ordinary class draw (three
    equal 42-session blocks in a row), so the R floor cannot separate it.
- **A switching side** (declared mean dwell D):
  - R against the Markov interval at the band's ends, as now;
  - the two-quarters floor either dropped where it binds inside a band (51 to 62 at 657), or kept with the in-band
    failure at each end printed by `q126_arith.py` and held by T-0;
  - F-1's balance condition applies.
- **Line 173** carries the computed in-band failures instead of "at most about 5 %".
- **Line 147** compares in one unit: runs with runs, or blocks with blocks.
- **The simpler alternative, if the lead prefers one measure:**
  - keep R, with each band's lower limit the least of the 2.5 % quantiles of R under the band's class models at its
    upper end (flip-hi as well as Markov-hi; about 5 at 657 for flip-50);
  - drop the two-quarters floor and keep the balance condition;
  - restate the residual, which then widens (F-3).

### 2. The band check

- **Is it outcome-free?** Yes.
  - It reads 4.4's non-tie side series: sides, and which sessions traded.
  - Which sessions traded depends on fills, so on bars after the alert, exactly as 4.4's judged check does. The pass
    line's outcome, the lift over the coin twin, is not read.
  - The intervals are arithmetic on n and the declared band.
- **Can a family game it?**
  - **Inside the class, choosing a band is harmless for size.** At 657 the three bands' intervals share 10 <= R <= 21,
    and at 1,006 they share 16 to 30, so most families can declare any band. But each adopted statistic is validated on
    the whole class in its own scope (4.3: "on every one of the 2,293 validation nulls"). So a choice inside the
    overlap, made before the pin, moves power, not size.
  - **The real ways in.**
    - **R-padding (F-1).** A few one-session opposite trades make a dominant side pass. The family reads no outcome to
      do it.
    - **Out-of-class persistence that R admits (F-3).**
  - **A minor one.** The stage-A span is unsealed (Q-108's 4.4), so n is the family's. A span picked to land R in a band
    is a choice from sides only and gains nothing at stage C, where n = 657 is fixed.
- **Is the stated residual honest?** Not fully (F-3).
  - It is right on the choice block.
  - It is too wide for fixed regimes on the judged block (64 to 109, not 64 to 125).
  - It omits Markov sides beyond dwell 80 (0.483 pass at 100), dominated sides and edge reversals.
- **Is the overlap rule sound?** Yes, for size, for the reason under gaming.
  - "From sides only" cannot be enforced, and it is not needed: the choice-block outcomes may inform it as any stage-A
    choice does, and the judged block is untouched.
  - The reading needed is m-3's: what a choice-block mismatch allows before the pin.
  - **Conditioning on the check does not raise a family's false-pass chance.** That chance is that of the joint event,
    claimed and rejecting, and the check only removes outcomes from it. This is Q-108's scope-D standard.
