### M2 (MATERIAL). The sized floor is a tightening, and its power is not shown

**Where.** Lines 150-166 and 365-388 (B-6, B-16). E-051's ruling says "a tightening is accepted only with its level
and power at the family's level shown before the pin". The operator's rule: a strict line must show power, not only
size.

**The evidence** (my scratch check; the plan's constants). The plan shows the power of the **validation criterion**,
P(pass | profile). It does not show the power of its own new **calibration floor**, zero bad subsets among 3,300:

| pooled bad-subset rate r | P(the sized floor admits the candidate) = (1 - r)^3300 | P(the validation's exact part passes it) |
|---|---|---|
| 1e-5 | 0.968 | 0.9989 |
| 3e-5 | 0.906 | 0.9966 |
| 1e-4 | 0.719 | 0.9886 |
| 3e-4 | 0.372 | 0.9661 |
| 5e-4 | 0.192 | 0.9441 |
| 9.16e-4 (r_V) | 0.049 | 0.9000 |

Take a candidate at r = 1e-4. The validation would pass its exact part 98.9 % of the time, but the floor refuses it
28 % of the time. At r = 3e-4 the floor refuses it 63 % of the time. In a nested grid a refusal moves the choice one
step up. Section 5's table puts a step at roughly 8 to 9 % of minimum detectable lift at m = 1.125 and 1.375, and 19 %
at the step from 1.25 to 1.5. That is the profitable-model cost the operator's rule asks to see.

**The failure it allows.** A design whose true lift lies between `cons_m1.25`'s and `cons_m1.375`'s MDL could be failed
by the line. The cause would be a refusal driven by the floor's 0.95 detection target, not by a size the validation
would punish. The plan would not have said so beforehand.

**Remedy (text).**
- Add the table above to section 4.3 or 3.2, with the step costs from section 5.
- State that 0.95 detection at r_V is what buys the 0.0126 and 0.0337 bounds, and what costs this refusal rate.
- Optionally give the lead the alternative of one bad subset tolerated (c <= 1). It needs about 5,180 subsets for the
  same 0.95 detection at r_V, and admits r = 1e-4 with probability about 0.90 instead of 0.72. The cost is about 57 %
  more sizing time. If taken, that is a new declared rule.

### n1 (NOTE). After this plan, the exact part's real guarantee is the calibration's

at 95 %.

**Remedy.** Section 8's "validated on other new seeds" should say this, so that no reader takes the 115-subset
validation as the line's certificate.

**1. Selection after the look.**
- Disclosing that scope D held on Q-108's validation draws, and trying D first, does **not** bias the new validation's
  operating characteristic for the candidate it tests. The new draws are independent of every draw seen.
- What it does is concentrate the program's adaptation on one population, P-16. That is disclosure n2, and my n5 is
  the answer for families.
- The fixed-data streams are no test for any candidate Q-108 ran (disclosed at 593-595).
- **Seeds.** Across the main tree, the ten worktrees (tracked and untracked), all 22 refs and every line of
  `git log --all -p`:
  - every eight-digit number sharing the blocks' leading five digits is one of the plan's twenty;
  - they appear only in the plan and T-0;
  - they were added in one commit, ec6905a.
- **Derived seeds.** The nearest Python seed literal below the calibration block is 307 below: `tests/report_synth.py`,
  used as a string seed with no offset. The RD-04 test's block of ten is 700 below. Nothing in any `.py` on any ref lies
  above the validation block. T-0's AST guard, re-pointed at the new blocks, refused nothing. I recomputed
  `fixed_b_critical`'s `seed + 1000 G + lags` from the source: for G >= 1 and lags < 50 nothing lands in either block.
- **The old draws are unused only if M4 is fixed.**

**2. The sizing.**
- The rule of three is applied to the right rate: the per-subset union rate the validation punishes, with
  r_V = 1 - 0.9^(1/115). N = 3,270 is exact.
- 660 a size meets the plan's own target. Pooling over sizes is exact, because the 660/23 ratio is the same at every
  size.
- The union is the right event.
- The residual Monte Carlo lottery **does** leave the same failure mode open on the Monte Carlo side, on the same
  subset, in scope D at m = 1.0 (M1).

**3. The criterion's power.**
- The exact rows are (1 - r)^115. The Monte Carlo rows come from the nested-binomial program with E-052's failure
  counts (71 down to 35 at k = 13 to 20, recomputed). I reproduced both from the script.
- "A statistic hugging nominal fails it" is true. **It is not a reason to change E-052 now:** a change would be a
  weakening after the look. For every margin candidate the Monte Carlo strictness costs nothing (P_MC = 1 at
  m >= 1.25), while the exact zero tolerance is the per-subset size definition (E-105 (a)), not an artefact.
- What the operator's rule does require is the power of this plan's **own** tightening, which is missing (M2).

**4. Scope D's coverage.**
- The table is the file's, and its prose shares are honest.
- The closing claim that nothing is recoverable is not (M3).
- **What an excluded family gets** (section 7 item 4, line 576): "not claimed", unless its pinned text declared a
  two-sample line before any read. So it has no one-sample line, even where the Q branch could have served it.

**5. Interpretation 6's bound: valid.**
- **The nested branch.** Eligibility is upward closed in m on shared draws:
  - the floors and condition 3 are monotone;
  - P_MC is monotone, because lower rates at every k couple replication by replication to lower nested counts;
  - P is monotone, because the MDL m crit se - est grows with m on every replication, so the smallest-P choice is the
    smallest eligible m.
- **The integral.** Validation draws are independent of the calibration's. So
  P(choice = m_i and fails) = (P_i - P_{i-1}) (1 - (1 - r_i)^n), which is at most
  (P_i - P_{i-1}) (1 - P_i^a). Since 1 - p^a decreases, the sum is at most the integral, a/(1 + a).
- **The other two branches.** C6 adds the one-candidate bound, and the Q branch adds one more nested term. The union
  is 0.0800.
- **The numeric check:** the worst over 200,000 random nested grids was 0.0304; a 20,000-step grid gives 0.03366,
  against a/(1+a) = 0.03367; the one-candidate supremum is 0.01260.
- **Its scope.** It bounds the exact part only (M1).

**6. The cost.**
- 12 to 23 h is supported by the logs (m2).
- Split into declared parts under one lock, with a crash rule (m2).
- Memory is no constraint: the logs' compute steps peak near 223 MB.
