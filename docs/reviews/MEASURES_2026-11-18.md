## Blocking findings

### B-1. The observation window is not bounded by the block, so a search-year start reads held-out sessions [CHANGES-A-CONCLUSION]

- **Where.**
  - `MEASURES.md:17-18` ("one development session (NQ 2020-01-02 to 2026-07-16)").
  - `:25` (`obs_u = min(252, sessions left)`).
  - `:45-46` (`E_h` = the units with `obs_u >= h`).
  - `:66-69`: "the search declaration sets them (plan Wave 5), never this file". E-208 has already fixed the blocks.
- **The code it maps to.**
  - `extended/user_beside.py:633`: `L = min(U.HORIZON, n - s)`, where `n` is every development session.
  - `:618`: `replay(blocks[s:s + h], ...)`.
  - `extended/user_readings.py:118-134` and `u_select.block(None, h)`: block `all` means `observed >= h` over the whole
    window.
- **Evidence.** "Sessions left" is counted to 2026-07-16. A start on any search-year day after about the end of 2022
  therefore has `obs_u = 252`, and its replay runs on 2024 bars. Every measure on `E_252` and every 126-session
  measure then includes starts whose outcomes lie in the held-out years.
- **A concrete leak in existing code, even on the `early` block.** [B] `extra/share_spent_over_*` is computed at
  `user_readings.py:183-189` as `share_over_limit(spent[:, 2])`. The value `spent` is
  `spent_before_first_payout` from the long-replay record (`user_journey.py:163`), and it is finite whenever a first
  payout comes within `observed` sessions (up to 252), not within 63. The reading's eligibility is the 63-session
  block (`u_select.block(..., P_HORIZON)`). So an `early` start between `last - 252` and `last - 63` contributes
  spending measured up to a 2024 payout.
- **The failure it allows.**
  - H4 or a survey built from this file, with `E_h` as written, reads the held-out years. E-208 and amendments 3.6 (d)
    and (e) forbid exactly this.
  - Limits, budgets and normalisers pinned from that output then carry held-out information into the composite.
- **Remedy.**
  - Define `obs_u = min(252, sessions from s to the last session of s's block)`. The search block ends 2023-12-29 and
    the held-out block ends 2026-07-16 (E-208).
  - State that the engine run itself stops at the block's end (the runner refuses held-out sessions, 3.6 (e)), not
    only the reading.
  - State that every record field read at horizon `h` (first payout, spending before it, pay days, break-even, fell
    back) is computed from closes `0 .. h` only.
  - Because a long replay's events before `h` can depend on the run's length (D-620 (3)(f), the reason for `Views.at`),
    take each reading at `h` from a run of exactly `h` sessions, or from a long run cut at the block's end, with U1's
    identity (event days up to `h` equal) checked on the cut runs.
  - Replace `:66-69` with E-208's dates.
  - State the consequence: at the 12-month horizon the search years have no start from 2023.

### B-2. P under the bankroll stop ignores spending after the first payout [CHANGES-A-CONCLUSION]

- **Where.**
  - `MEASURES.md:102-106`, `:334` and `:490`. `P_B` is the composite's named dimension and the pinned fallback pass
    line (amendments 3.9).
  - Code: `extended/user_measures.py:58-76`; the call at `extended/user_readings.py:184` passes
    `spent_before_first_payout`.
- **Evidence (synthetic check).**
  - The path: fees take the net to -1,500 before a first payout of +600. Then more Combines take it to -2,600. Then a
    large payout leaves it above zero at close 63.
  - `p_by_month(..., limit=2000)` counts the start as ahead at month 3, although the user's own money went 2,600 below
    the start.
  - A user with a budget of 2,000 would have run out at -2,000, after the first payout.
- **The failure it allows.**
  - A design that takes a small, fast first payout and then buys aggressively scores as within budget, although the
    user cannot fund it.
  - This is the one dimension the plan built to counter that behaviour. It errs in the design's favour, and it does
    so selectively for multi-account, high-purchase policies.
- **Remedy.**
  - A unit counts as ahead at month `m` under `B` only if `max(0, -min_{0 <= j < 21m} rel[j]) <= B`, the peak out of
    pocket through the month close. Applied to (24) at the window's end, that is (25)'s quantity.
  - Keep "before the first payout" as a named sub-reading beside.
  - Note that `p_by_month`'s `spent` argument must then carry the peak through each month close, not U1's column.

### B-3. Running out (24) and the bankroll needed (S1, 6) treat unpaid users as safe or leave them out [MATERIAL]

- **Where.**
  - `MEASURES.md:477-483` and reading 6 (`:741-743`).
  - (6) at `:231-233` ("Units not paid by W are left out here").
  - `share_over_limit` (`user_measures.py:86-90`) drops non-finite spending, so it drops every unpaid start.
- **Evidence (synthetic check).** Four starts: three never paid, one paid cheaply. `share_over_limit(.., 2000)` gives
  zero, read on one start of four. The document flags this. Its own (24) repeats a milder form of it:
  - A unit unpaid by `W` whose spending through `W` is at most `B` counts as not run out.
  - It is in fact censored: still paying fees and not yet paid.
  - Its spending after `W` is unread.
- **The failure it allows.**
  - (24) is the search's declared counterweight to speed (amendments 3.5) and a hard limit.
  - A slow, cheap design (one Combine, a subscription a month, rarely passing) shows a ruin share near zero and passes
    a ruin ceiling, although most of its users are never paid.
  - S1's p90 is conditioned on being paid. A design that pays few users cheaply shows a low "bankroll needed", so a
    bankroll limit read on S1 passes it.
- **Remedy.**
  - Read (24) in three states over all of `E_W`:
    - **paid within `B`:** paid by `W`, with spending before the payout at most `B`;
    - **run out:** spending above `B` before a first payout by `W`;
    - **censored:** unpaid by `W` and still within `B`.
  - The limit reads "not paid within `B` by `W`", which is run out plus censored. Report each part beside.
  - For the bankroll needed, define the limit quantity over all of `E_W`, with an unpaid unit's spending through `W`
    as a lower bound (a censored value). Keep S1, which is conditional on being paid, as Stage 2's reading beside.
  - The words "the share paid by `W`" must sit beside every conditional percentile ((2), (3), (6), (7), (23)).

### B-4. The coin layer's "seed spread" is not the user's payout variance [MATERIAL]

- **Where.**
  - `MEASURES.md:56-64` ("This is the user's payout variance (plan 4.5)").
  - Reading 1 (`:732-734`).
  - (34) at `:630-634`.
- **Evidence.**
  - Under one seed, every start day shares the same coin draws on the same calendar moments. The amendments call for
    "one literal coin draw per moment", and U1's `COIN_SEED` is one sequence over the calendar.
  - The per-seed statistic over start days (the P of seed `c`, for example) is therefore one realisation of the
    design's population statistic. Its spread across seeds is the coin noise of that statistic, which is what the
    coin-seed leg's replicates measure (3.6 (f) (i)).
  - Averaging over thousands of start days removes almost all of what one user lives. A user lives one `(s, c)`.
  - Reading 1 is right that a per-start mean over seeds hides the variance. The option it chose hides it too.
- **The failure it allows.**
  - The verdict prints "the coin-seed spread ... the user's payout variance" (amendments 3.6 (f), 2). The operator is
    shown a narrow spread of a share across seeds and told it is how much a user's results vary. That understates the
    variance a user feels by construction.
  - If a steadiness dimension is built from it, a design is rewarded for a stable average, not for stable users.
- **Remedy.** Report three things, each labelled:
  1. The pooled distribution over `(s, c)`: "a random user".
  2. **The user's payout variance**: for each start day `s`, the p10, p50 and p90 of `x_{s,c}` across `c`, summarised
     over `s` (the median start day's within-day p10 and p90, and their spread). This is "the spread a user starting
     that day can live".
  3. The per-seed statistic's spread across seeds, labelled "the design statistic's coin noise" (the coin-seed leg's
     replicate spread), not a user quantity.

  Reading 1 should be rewritten to match.

### B-6. (21) S5b, the longest gap, rewards a late first payout [MATERIAL]

- **Where.**
  - `MEASURES.md:430-445` and reading 5 (`:739-740`).
  - Code: `user_journey.py:205-221` (`payout_rhythm`) and `:512-519`.

  | payouts at 10 and 120 | 110 |

### B-7. (31) Funded-account lifetime is read on completed accounts only, a bias that depends on the design [MATERIAL]

- **Where.** `MEASURES.md:574-588` and reading 9 (`:748-749`): the distribution is "over the lost funded accounts",
  with the open accounts censored and left out.
- **Evidence.**
  - The accounts still open at the window's end are the long-lived, payout-rich ones, and they are excluded.
  - The longer a design's policy keeps accounts alive, the more of them are excluded. Its distribution is then made of
    its quickest losses.
- **The failure it allows.** A design that preserves funded accounts reads as having worse lifetimes than one that
  burns them fast. The ranking is inverted on the measure meant to show account survival.
- **Remedy.**
  - Use a censoring-aware estimate: Kaplan-Meier in payouts or in sessions, with the open accounts censored at the
    window's end.
  - Or restrict to accounts activated at least a declared number of sessions before the window's end.
  - Report the censored count beside.
  - Pool over accounts with each unit weighted equally, not each account. Otherwise designs with many accounts
    dominate the pooled distribution.
