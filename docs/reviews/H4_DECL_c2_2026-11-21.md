## 3. Blocking findings

### RC-1. The breakeven move has no law; the coin's walk rules are unnamed; I-5 fails with the trigger library's walk

**Where:**
- 223: the deciding reading's law is "the exit map `three_way_redraw.EXIT_LAW`, any other state refused".
- 186: the time limits are "the 15:10 flatten, a news window, a max hold".
- 222: the coin takes "P-16's registered job's ... its protocol".
- 220: the coin is "the lever table's T1 `coin`".
- 461-465: I-5 names P-16's marks, latency 0, `coin_table` sides and the `_nr` news rule, and nothing about the walk.
- 632: the coin's source is "the trigger library's offers (`extended/triggers.py`, `extended/components.py`)".

**Evidence:**
- **Every registered baseline walks with the move.** `fv3_rung_walk.py` 16-21 and 140-190: at the first FV3 session
  open after the entry (17:00, 19:00, 08:30 or 13:00 CT), if that open is a tick or more in favour, the stop moves to
  the entry. A later touch exits at zero with kind 3 (`b`).
  - P-14: `atlas_a3.py` 13-14, "with FV3's breakeven move".
  - P-15 and P-16: `resolved_coin.py` 10-13 (walked by `fv3_rung_walk` through `atlas_a3.walk_minutes`); `cell_trades`
    writes `o_R` in {s, t, f, b} (56, 197).
  - P-16's own stop distribution maps "stop or breakeven -> stop" (`rc150_dev_test.py` ~119), so `b` exits occur.
- **The runner has no state for them.** `EXIT_LAW` (wt/wt-firmpath `three_way_redraw.py` 66-76) names target, stop,
  stop_gap, floor, flatten, news, max_hold, profit_level and other. `other` is "an unscheduled release's flatten, or
  another exit at a real time limit", which a breakeven exit is not.
  - `three_way_journey.Trade.__post_init__` calls `RD.law_of(exit_reason)`, which raises on any unnamed reason.
- **The coin's two possible walks.** `triggers.JOURNEY = W.Rules("journey", W.FLAT_ENTRY, "worst_less_two", False)`
  (`triggers.py` 134): the move is off. `walker.STAGE2` (192) has it on.
  - The lever table drops the move (wt/wt-firmpath `LEVER_TABLE_topstep50k.md` S7: "**dropped**: off ... P-14's
    registered walk keeps its own move as part of its source (T1)").
  - So "the lever table's T1 coin" says off, and "P-16's protocol" says on. The text names neither.

**The failure it allows:**
- **(a) H4 stops by construction.** P-14's, P-15's and P-16's schedules equal their registered trades (I-1), so they
  carry `b` exits. The first such trade handed to the firm path is refused, and H4-B stops.
  - The law is then chosen after the schedules (and H4-S's exit-kind counts) have been seen. The candidates are:
    - map `b` to `stop` and race at (l, g), ignoring the move;
    - map it to `other` and walk to the flatten;
    - the move-aware sequential law.
  - Each has zero mean but a different spread. Through the contract's convexity, each moves P_B, the pass shares
    and the payout timing of every baseline, so it moves W42-R1's limits.
- **(b) I-5 fails by construction.** The coin's source at P-16's marks, latency 0, P-16's sides and `_nr`, walked by
  the library's `JOURNEY`, gives a target or flatten (and a later exit time) wherever P-16's trade booked `b`.
  `trades_hash` then differs on the first such trade.
- **(c) The coin's comparison changes silently.** If the run's coin is walked with `JOURNEY`, it differs from P-16 in
  a fifth respect (no stop move) that section 7's list of four differences (schedule, latency, news, void calendar)
  omits. If it is walked with `STAGE2`, (a) applies to it too.

**Remedy (a lead's ruling, then text):**
1. **Name the coin's walk rules** in section 7's coin rows. I recommend `JOURNEY`, the move off, as the lever table's
   S7 requires: the coin is a new comparator, and the registered designs keep their move only as part of their
   source. Add "the breakeven move (off for the coin, P-16's on)" to the list of differences at 222.
2. **Name I-5's walk:** `walker.STAGE2`, P-16's walk, in I-5's mode only.
3. **Declare the law of a `b` exit** for P-14, P-15 and P-16, add the state to the exit map, and give the oracle's
   form for it.
   - The law that keeps "the trade as placed" is the sequential driftless race on one path, which section 7's overlap
     row already names as the exact form:
     - R-10's stopped walk from the fill to the move bar's open, between -L and +g;
     - then, if the walked position is at least one tick in favour, the race between the entry (less its exit cost)
       and +g from that position, p = x / g;
     - otherwise the race continues between -L and +g.
   - Its gross expectation is zero by optional stopping. It needs the move bar (the walk's `be_open`) in the schedule.
   - Any simpler mapping (to `stop`, or to `other`) is a declared approximation, labelled so, with the `b` share
     printed beside.
4. **Count `b` exits** per baseline and year in H4-S's supply counts (section 5).
5. **Pin each sentence in T-0.**

### RC-2. The refusing overlap span is one minute stricter than D-429, so the kill fires on the vendored router's correct output

**Where:**
- 238: "any two taken trades of the user whose spans **[fill bar open, exit bar end at the taken R + 5 min)**
  intersect; it must be 0".
- 300-301 and 344-345: the count is a floor and a kill.
- 122: the strict rule is "applied by the vendored routing (`shared_days.TradeRouter`)".
- 503-505: a toy "whose second trade fills inside the first's five minutes flat" must refuse.

**Evidence:**
- **Stage 2's clock is bar END minutes.** `collect_core.py` 6: "a slot is a bar's END time in minutes after 17:00 CT";
  `fv3_rung_walk.walk`: "index j = a bar's END minute".
  - `fast_walk.entry_bar` returns the first index after the slot, which is the fill bar's end.
  - `cell_trades` writes `entry_t = entry_slot` (the fill bar's end) and `exit_t_R = x` (the exit bar's end).
- **The router refuses a trade only while the last exit plus the gap is past its entry.** `shared_days.py` 357-359:
  refuse while `open_until[k] + one_at_a_time > t`, with `t = entry_t` and `open_until = exit_for(k, j)` (the exit's
  end).
  - So it admits entry_t2 = exit_t1 + 5, which is a fill bar opening at exit bar end + 4.
  - Q-185's firm path pins the same boundary ("C1-N1: the exact-gap boundary pinned"; `three_way_firm.hold`,
    `a0 < b1 + gap`).
- **H4's span starts one minute earlier.** It starts at the fill bar's open, entry_t - 1, so it counts that pair:
  entry_t2 - 1 < exit_t1 + 5.
- **The synthetic check.** On the vendored `TradeRouter` (`scratchpad/h4c2_chk/gap.py`) I used three trades with
  entry_t (1, 11, 26) and exit_t (6, 20, 40), gap 5.
  - The router routes all three and skips none.
  - H4's declared span counts **1** overlap. The span [fill bar end, exit bar end + 5) counts **0**.
- **How often.** P-15's, P-16's and the coin's fills are on the five-minute grid plus one: entry_t is 1 mod 5. The next
  admitted offer lands exactly on the boundary whenever an exit bar ends at a minute that is 1 mod 5, about one exit
  in five. With up to five trades a session over a unit's sessions, almost every unit has such a pair.

**The failure it allows:** with correct routing, the count is above 0 on nearly every unit of every baseline. H4-B's
kill stops H4, and the definition is re-chosen after it is read (RB-2's failure, one minute over). The declared toy
test at 503-505, built on the same span, would assert the wrong boundary.

**Remedy (mechanical; fixed by D-429):**
- At 238 (and 300, 474, 503-505), write the span **in Stage 2's clock: [the fill bar's end, the exit bar's end at the
  taken R + 5 min)**, on the absolute clock (trading day x 1440 + minute). That is: a second trade's fill bar must end
  at least five minutes after the first's exit bar ends, exactly `shared_days` 357-359.
- The toy tests become entry_t2 = exit_t1 + 5, admitted, and exit_t1 + 4, refused.
- The trade log names the clock of its times (bar END minutes with the trading day).
- Beside the count, say that D-429's five minutes are four to five minutes of real flat time: the exit falls somewhere
  in its bar, and the fill is at its bar's open (rn-a).
