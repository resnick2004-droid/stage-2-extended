## Verdict

**Pin after the listed changes.** In this program's scale: **must be re-declared before its pin.** The changes alter
declared rules (4.2, 4.4, 3.2), so the text that is pinned must be a new one.

**What is sound and should stay:**
- the architecture: a new module beside the pinned `lift_bound.py`;
- candidates and rules fixed before any draw;
- the choice coded and made on calibration seeds only;

**What is not sound:**
- **Scope D's admission and coverage argument does not hold as written.** Its protection is plausibly real, but it is

- **The level rule's strictness, the power ceiling and the subset count are unlabelled choices that carry the power**

## Findings, most serious first

### MATERIAL 1 -- Scope D's admission does not establish that an admitted cell is inside the calibrated class (4.4; 3.4; 4.2 step 2; 4.3)

**The evidence.**

1. **"R runs imply at least R sign blocks, so the aligned pattern's probability is at most 2^-R <= 2^-13, below every
   family level"** bounds only the one exactly aligned pattern. A statistic with power also rejects the near-aligned
   patterns.
   - Aligned plus every single-block flip carries (1 + B) 2^-B.
   - At B = 13 that is 14 / 8192, about 4.5 times the k = 11 level (A).
   - So R >= 13 says nothing about the level by itself.
2. **The mapping from R to "persistence" depends on the side process.**
   - Under the class's own model, a fair coin per L-session block, R is about (B + 1) / 2, because adjacent equal
     signs merge.
   - Measured (C), on the judged block's 657 sessions: R >= 13 admits L = 21 sides 93 % of the time, L = 30 40 %,
     L = 42 2.9 % and L = 50 0.2 %. The plan's scratch claim holds on this model.
   - Under other side models it admits much longer persistence:
     - **A side that alternates at every block boundary** (a regime filter switching state) has R = B. Such sides at
       42-, 45- and 50-session blocks are admitted at every phase, and at 55 sessions at 93 % of phases (C).
     - **A Markov switching side with mean dwell D** has R of about 657 / D, while its long-run variance matches a
       fair-coin block of about L = D. So R >= 13 admits D up to about 50.
     - Both are beyond the calibrated L = 42, whose flip has 16 or 17 blocks.
   - "The persist42 nulls lie inside scope D's calibrated class, so every admitted cell is covered with room" is
     asserted. It is not shown, and under these side models it is not true.
3. **R ignores uneven runs.** A side with one 300-session run and twelve short ones has R = 13.
4. **At stage C the text leaves a switch open.** "A cell not admitted cannot use the one-sample line. It may use the
   two-sample forms." Read at stage C, this lets a cell change its deciding statistic after a judged-block read of its
   sides.
   - The program's precedent for an outcome-free judged check is different. F-03's K0 at stage C withholds: "a pass is
     not claimed".
5. **The judged R is a judged-block read.** The sides come from the judged bars.
   - Under `extended/reader.py` it needs a purpose on the family's pinned `Judged-block reads:` line (family B's draft
     names `stage_C_sides`, F-03 `stage_C_states`), with no choice or fitting word in its name.
   - It runs inside the stage-C clean run, is ledgered, and is "once" per declaration and purpose.
   - The plan's "ledgered like K0's count check" is right in substance but names no mechanism.

**What the check does do** (B, C):
- A sign pattern aligned with the data's own block signs has as many runs as those signs. On this block that is at
  most 12 at L = 63, so it is never admitted.
- Under a drift, the aligned pattern is "long throughout", R = 1.
- In B, the procedure "admitted and rejects" at L = 42 stayed at or below about 0.08 times nominal. That held on the
  very synthetic subsets where `cons` alone reached 1.3 times nominal there.
- So the protection is likely real. But it is a property of the **procedure**, and the plan calibrates the
  **statistic** on persist42 unconditionally. That is too strict where admission excludes, and silent where admission
  leaks.

**The failure it allows.** The input: a family whose side follows a regime switching every 45-55 sessions (R = 12-16
on the judged block), on data whose block sums are even. It is admitted, and the scope-D statistic decides a class it
was never calibrated on. In B, `cons` on such data at L = 55 reached about 1.5 to 2.9 times nominal on 2 to 5 of 24
synthetic subsets.

**The change.** Replace 4.4's "Why these thresholds" and "What it means" and 3.4's scope-D row with:
- **(a) Calibrate and validate scope D as a procedure.**
  - In every replication of every null, compute R from that replication's own side series and count a rejection only
    when the replication is admitted:
    - for the persist-L nulls, the flip signs (x = side times v);
    - for the coin nulls, the coin times the flips;
    - Q-16's synthetic nulls reset sides daily, so they are always admitted.
  - Scope D's class is then all of scope Q's 349 nulls; the persist63 nulls contribute what the admission lets through.
  - Add, in the rule, flip nulls at L = 55, and side series with switch timing applied to the same real V:
    - an alternating block side at 45 and 55 sessions;
    - a Markov switching side with mean dwell 30, 50 and 80 sessions, a fresh series each replication;
    - x is the side times V, and R is computed on that side.
  - The cost is small: R is one pass over the signs.
- **(b) At stage C, a cell whose judged R fails is "not claimed"**, as K0 at stage C. A two-sample line may decide
  such a cell only if the family's pinned text declared it as that cell's line before any read, with its k counted.
- **(c) State the judged read's mechanism**: a purpose on the `Judged-block reads:` line, in the stage-C run, before
  any outcome is computed.
- **(d) Either keep R and let (a) measure it, or replace it by an effective count robust to uneven runs.** The
  effective count would be (sum of run lengths)^2 / (sum of squared run lengths). If it replaces R, its threshold must
  be fixed by the same arithmetic before the pin.

### MATERIAL 2 -- Section 1's "at L = 42 and below the bound is far below the levels" does not follow, and scope D's boundary rests on it (section 1; 3.1's "L = 42 is the boundary of scope D")

**The evidence.**
- A(L) is a **lower** bound on the size of a test that rejects the aligned pattern. It is not an upper bound on
  anything.
- The near-aligned patterns are what a powerful test also rejects:
  - At L = 42, aligned plus every single flip already gives 0.49, 0.57, 0.67, 0.77 and 0.88 of the levels at
    k = 11 to 15.
  - With the two-block flips added it is 4.0 times the k = 11 level (A).
- Exact enumeration on synthetic subsets with a drift (B):
  - `cons` at L = 42 is above nominal on 1 of 24 subsets in each of the two more drifted populations, up to about 1.3
    times nominal at k = 11 and 1.26 at k = 15.
  - At L = 55 it is above on 2 to 5 of 24.
- So 42-session persistence can break the one-sample line on even subsets, by the same mechanism as at 63.

**The failure it allows.** A family declares a 42-session side under scope D on the strength of section 1. Its safety
then rests on the five calibration subsets per size and the five fresh ones, not on an argument.

**The change.**
- Rewrite the last bullet group of section 1 as follows.
  - "A(L) is necessary, not sufficient: any test that rejects the aligned pattern has at least this size. The
    near-aligned patterns add up to (1 + B) 2^-B and more."
  - Then give the H1 figures at L = 42 and 63.
  - Then: "no persistence is shown safe by this arithmetic; scope D's class is safe only as far as the calibration and
    validation show it".
- Delete "the persistence that breaks the one-sample line is a side that holds for about a quarter of this block".
- With M1 (a), the boundary question becomes measured rather than argued.

### MATERIAL 3 -- "Every subset a null of its own" is an unlabelled strictness choice, and five subsets per size under-sample exactly where the failure lives (3.2; 4.2; 4.3)

**The evidence.**
- The plan counts each drawn subset as a null and requires every one to hold. That is a per-subset (supremum) reading
  of size. It is consistent with K0's per-cell logic, but it is a choice.
- The family's error rate *given this market* is the size averaged over the trade sets a no-skill design could take.
  That is exactly what `extended/count_nulls.PROFILES["flat"]` (a fresh uniform subset each replication) measures.
  - Q-83 ran `flat` only at persist1 (`results/q82_count_persistent.csv`, the null names).
- Under the per-subset reading, the number of subsets drawn sets the strictness: more subsets, more chances to find a
  bad one. Under the averaged reading, five subsets give a noisy mean.
- In B the per-subset size is heavy-tailed: a median near zero, with a few subsets at several times nominal.
  - The bad-subset rate per construction ran from none (the undrifted populations) to 4 % at L = 42 and 8 % at L = 63
    on the middle drift, and 25 % at L = 63 on the strongest.
  - A rate of 2 to 25 % is seen in ten draws per size and value with probability between about 0.18 and 0.94.
  - "Why five subsets" gives no argument for five.
- Monte Carlo is not needed at L = 42, 55 and 63.
  - Every candidate C0 to C6 is a function of the test-block sums, which are linear in the flip pattern:
    - BS_g = sum_f s_f M[f, g], where M[f, g] is V summed over (flip block f) intersected with (test block g);
    - BN_g is fixed.
  - So the exact size is a sum over 63 phases x 2^12 patterns (L = 63) or 42 x 2^17 (L = 42).
  - C7 is linear in the same way, through the session partial sums.
  - B did this in about a second or two per subset per L, single-threaded, in plain numpy.

**The failure it allows.** A candidate is chosen and validated on 25 + 25 subsets. A design whose own trade set is
from the bad tail (one in twenty, say) is then judged at several times its level, with no draw having shown that tail.
Or the reverse: a candidate is refused for one unlucky subset, when its averaged size holds with room.

**The change.**
- **(i) Declare which size decides.** Either keep the per-subset reading, and argue it, or decide on the averaged
  reading with the per-subset spread beside. If the per-subset reading stays, say plainly that section 1 makes it
  unattainable at L = 63 for any test without a margin.
- **(ii) Compute the persist42, persist55 and persist63 nulls by exact enumeration**, keeping Monte Carlo elsewhere.
  - An exact size has no interval. 4.2's and 4.3's tests read "exact size at or below the rule's bound" and "exact
    size above nominal" there.
  - The scalar re-decision is done on a declared seeded sample of patterns.
- **(iii) Set the number of subsets by a stated detection requirement.** For example, a 2 % bad-subset rate per L,
  seen with probability 0.9, needs about 114 subset-constructions per L; exact enumeration makes 100 or more subsets per
  size affordable.
- **(iv) Run the `flat` form at L = 21, 42, 63 beside** (or in the rule, if (i) chooses it).

### MATERIAL 4 -- The level rule is stricter than the level needs, and the power ceiling, not the level, decides whether a margin can be chosen (4.2; 5)

**The evidence** (A, D).
- 4.2's thresholds are right: the counts at most 58, 48, 40, 34 and 29 at k = 11 to 15. The two "what the rule
  demands" statements are right too, *for every null at the same fraction of nominal*: with all 349 at a third, it
  passes 0.97; at seven tenths, the validation fails it about twice in a thousand.
- But the binding case is a handful of nulls near the level, with the rest far below:

  | true size on one null, x nominal | P(4.2's level rule passes that null) | P(4.3's validation fails on that null) |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0.5 | 0.96 | below 1e-6 |
  | 0.6 | 0.79 | below 1e-6 |
  | 0.7 | 0.46 | 1e-5 |
  | 0.8 | 0.17 | 0.0004 |
  | 0.9 | 0.04 | 0.006 |
  | 1.0 | 0.006 | 0.046 |

  So a candidate whose worst null sits at 0.7 times nominal is refused by the level rule about half the time, and five
  such nulls refuse it 98 % of the time. It truly holds its level, and that null would almost never fail it at
  validation.
- The power ceiling P(c) <= 1.5 P(C0):
  - **The margins' MDL80 ratios to `cons`** on the plan's toy form are 1.23 (m = 1.25), 1.46 (m = 1.5) and 1.92
    (m = 2), the same at k = 11, 13 and 15 and at 200 to 5,000 trades (D).
  - **C3 therefore cannot meet the ceiling by construction**, and C2 meets it with 3 % to spare. The largest
    admissible margin is about 1.55.
  - The 1.5 is argued only as "half again". It is a chosen constant, not E-051's rule, which asks that the power be
    shown, not capped relative to a statistic that itself failed.
- **What the strictness costs, in B's terms:**
  - On the most drifted synthetic population, m = 1.25 reached 0.89 of nominal on its worst subset at k = 11, and
    0.52 at k = 15. That is level-valid, but refused by 4.2.
  - m = 1.5 sat at or below 0.02.
  - So the strict rule would choose C2 over C1, at about 1.46 / 1.23 = 1.19 times the lift needed: one grid step, about
    a fifth more lift.
  - On the less drifted populations both pass, and the rule costs nothing.

**The failure it allows.** A profitable design is failed by a statistic chosen a grid step stricter than its level
requires. Or, if neither margin passes 4.2, scope Q is closed by the ceiling and the grid's top end, which were set
together.

**The change.**
- **Tie 4.2's level rule to what it protects.** Make a candidate level-eligible when its calibration counts, read as
  its rates, give it at least a 0.9 probability of HOLDS under 4.3, computed by the script as nested binomials over
  k. Keep, as a floor, no calibration null with its point rate above nominal at any k.
- Or keep the present rule, and add beside it the power cost: the MDL of the most powerful candidate refused by the
  level rule alone.
- **Label 1.5 as a chosen constant**, and report every candidate's absolute MDL50 and MDL80 at the next family's
  level beside P.
- Drop C3 or mark it "reference, cannot be power-eligible". If a margin above 1.5 is to be possible, replace the grid
  by m = 1.25, 1.5 and 1.75 and set the ceiling to admit 1.75, both before the pin.

### MATERIAL 5 -- The calibrated range and the power level ignore the program's numbering, and the bound worsens with k (2.1; 5; 8)

**The evidence.**
- E-094 (3)(b) retired k = 11 with F-02. F-03 is k = 12 and decides by two-sample forms, so the next one-sample family
  is k = 13 or later.
- **The bound grows with k** (A):
  - A(63) is 1.02, 1.21, 1.41, 1.63 and 1.86 times the level at k = 11 to 15, and 2.11 to 3.26 at k = 16 to 20;
  - log2(1 / level) rises from 11.4 to 13.0.
- So a statistic validated to k = 15 serves three families (13, 14, 15). The fourth needs a new heavy calibration
  cycle, and section 1 says it may then fail.
- The levels cost nothing per replication. The level rule keeps resolution at 200,000 to k = 20: its largest passing
  count is 14 against a nominal 23.8.
- The power measure is read at k = 11, a level no family will use. For the margins the ratios do not move with k (D),
  but C4's and C7's may.

**The change.**
- Calibrate and validate at k = 13..20, or at a declared k_max of the lead's, in the new module's own constant; keep
  `lift_bound.K_RANGE` untouched.
- Read P at k = 13.
- Report MDLs at 13, 15 and 20 beside.
- Restate section 8's last bullet with the new range.

### Notes (readings; no rule changes)

**n1. The fixed-data nulls are fresh for no one.** `offered` and `taken` are the same trades in Q-16, Q-55, Q-83 and
both of this plan's runs. Q-83 showed they reproduce (the audit, 3.3), so on them the validation tests only Monte
Carlo noise. The fresh test is in the subsets and the synthetic draws, which the plan does redraw.

**n2. One population.** Every subset in both runs comes from P-16's judged offers, so they share that population's
quarter structure. The validation tests the subset lottery inside one market, not other markets or designs.

**n3. Persistence past a quarter is in neither scope.**
- Long-only designs, half-year regimes and any side constant past 63 sessions are in neither scope.
- Say so in 7: such cells cannot use the one-sample line at all.
- Part of the L = 63 failure is E-035 note 8's drift credit. Under a drift, the aligned pattern is "a random side
  that leaned with the drift in every quarter", which conditionally *is* a positive lift over the coin twin.
- A side-matched two-sample comparison removes that credit and is the natural route for persistent cells.

**n4. "Outcome-free" holds in the sense that matters, but the judged R is not independent of the lift.**
- Sides are functions of the judged bars. In C:
  - a slow contrarian side's mean lift is shifted up given admission, and a slow trend side's is shifted down;
  - the joint rate "admitted and rejects" stayed at or below the unconditional rate.
- P(admitted and reject) <= P(reject) on the cell's process, so the procedure's error rate is bounded wherever the
  statistic is calibrated for that process. That is why M1 (a) is the fix that matters.

**n5. C6's theorem does not apply on this null.** Bakirov and Szekely's conservatism is for independent normal group
means. On the flip null the groups are symmetric two-point mixtures, and test blocks straddle flip blocks. The plan
concedes C6's exposure to the aligned pattern; the citation should not be read as more.

**n6. The margin route looks viable on synthetic data.** In B, the size fell steeply with m:
- m = 1.25 removed nearly all of `cons`'s excess at L = 42, 55 and 63 except on the most drifted population;
- m = 1.5 removed all of it.

This is a reading of synthetic data. It is not a reason to change the grid.

**n7. Memory looks right.** Q-83's one-sample part peaked near 0.7 GB by its log, so the plan's 1.0 GB is plausible.
Exact enumeration at L = 42 adds 2^17 x 31 arrays of about 32 MB each.

## On the brief's seven questions, in short

**1. Selection after the fact.**
- The candidates and scope D are labelled post hoc (section 8).
- The margins (a generic grid), the admission thresholds (from the 63-block runs bound) and the nulls show no fitting
  to any figure in Q-83's files.
- The validation stays a fresh test on the fresh subsets and the synthetic draws.
- But scope D's boundary is drawn exactly around the one failing construction, and the argument offered for it does
  not hold (M2). In effect it rests on the observed outcome, and only the fresh draws protect it.
- The fixed-data nulls are not fresh (n1).

**2. The arithmetic.** It is exact:
