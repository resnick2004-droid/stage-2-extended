## New blocking finding

### N-1. A censored percentile is labelled exact when it is a lower bound [MATERIAL]

- **Where.**
  - `MEASURES.md:106-107` (section 1.3): "A percentile that falls on a censored unit is reported as 'at least'."
  - `:355-356` and `:359` ((6): "a percentile on a censored unit reported 'at least'"; "A limit on it reads its
    censoring").
  - The same rule governs (2) and (3).
- **Evidence (synthetic check).**
  - Fifty unpaid units, each censored at a lower bound of 300. Fifty paid units, each exact at 1,000.
  - The p90 of the all-`E_W` form is 1,000. It falls on a paid unit, so the rule reports it as exact.
  - Every censored bound lies below it. If the censored users in truth need 4,000 each, the true p90 is 4,000.
- **Why the rule is wrong.** A lower bound below the percentile can move above it once the true value is known. A
  percentile is exact only when **every** censored bound lies strictly above it. Then the true values are above it
  too, and the ranks do not change.
- **The failure it allows.** This is B-3's failure moved from (24) to (6). (6) is "the bankroll needed", the natural
  home of the operator's bankroll ceiling.
  - A slow, cheap design has many unpaid users with low spending to date.
  - Its p90 is reported as an exact, low bankroll need, and it passes a bankroll ceiling it may well cross.
  - "A limit on it reads its censoring" does not say how. A limit set on the p90 as printed is the wrong reading.
- **Remedy.**
  - Section 1.3: "A dollar percentile is exact only if every censored unit's lower bound lies strictly above it.
    Otherwise it is reported as 'at least' that value."
  - (6): a limit on the bankroll needed is read through (24)'s states, as the share of `E_W` not paid within `B` by
    `W`. It is never read on the percentile of (6).
  - The days form (censored at `W + 1`) needs no change: a censored value there is above every finite one by
    construction.
