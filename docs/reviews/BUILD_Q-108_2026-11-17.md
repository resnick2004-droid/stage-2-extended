## Verdict

**Run as is, with the readings below recorded in the lead's row before the calibration run.**

The code does what the pinned plan says, section by section, and nothing I could find besides. None of the builder's
27 items needs a new declaration:
- 16 are readings the text already implies;
- 11 are harmless implementation details;
- the one that could in principle move the outcome, item 10, is the text's own rule and cannot arise on the declared
  toys.

I found no defect that changes a declared rule. My only findings are minor, and each is a reading for the row.

**Readings for the lead's row (before the run):**
1. **R1's mechanics.** A scalar re-decision disagreement on any candidate stops the run at that null. In the
   validation this includes C0 beside. No result file is written and no choice or verdict is printed. The record is
   the run's work folder and log.
2. **The re-decision's scope, as built.**
   - On Monte Carlo nulls: every candidate's rejections at a family level, plus each null's first 3 replications, at
     all eight k.
   - On exact nulls: per candidate and scope, the first 100 cells rejecting at k = 13, plus 20 distinct cells drawn
     uniformly over the cell indices, the same 20 for every candidate.
3. **The choosing row's form.** A single row naming `results/q108_choice.csv` sha256 `<64 hex>` and
   `results/q108_sn_table.csv` sha256 `<64 hex>`, in that backticked form. The validation finds the row by that form
   and refuses without it. The row is written only after the calibration log shows all three of:
   - no FAILED CHECK;
   - no derived-seed line marked "NOT in the hand-check record";
   - every critical value in `q108_sn_table.csv` positive (minor 1).
4. **P orders, and nothing else** (plan 4.2 and 5). An infinite P ranks last, and ties are broken by exact equality,
   then fewer members, then the earlier row. A candidate is not excluded for an infinite P.
5. **The seed guard scans every `.py` on every ref.** A refusal from any ref stops the run, and no other seeds are
   picked.

## (3) Item 10: can a powerless candidate be chosen?

**The text.**
- Plan 4.2: "the choice is the most powerful candidate meeting all three conditions: the smallest P".
- Plan 5: "An infinite MDL means no power", and "P orders the level-eligible candidates (4.2), and nothing else".
- The first text's requirement that P be finite went with the power ceiling, which E-105 (c) dropped.
- So the text orders by P and excludes nothing for it. The builder's reading is the text's.
- If every eligible candidate had an infinite P, one would be chosen by the tie rule, and its MDLs would be reported.
  Under E-051 each family then declares from those MDLs whether it decides by it or by the two-sample forms. No family
  would be forced onto a powerless line.

**Whether it can arise.**
- P is infinite only if, on some `win1` toy, at least a fifth of the first 20,000 replications have a blank bound, or
  in scope D are not admitted.
- On 200-trade toys (the sparsest), check K(2) found **no** blank bound for any of the ten candidates, and **no**
  non-admitted replication, on three persist1 and three coin toys.
  - A CR member is blank only with se = 0 or a single block.
  - A BM member only with df below 1.
  - C8 only with a zero normaliser.
  - A 200-trade toy has about 170 non-tie sessions, so R is far above 13.
- **So it is not a hole:** the case is the text's rule, and it does not arise on the declared toys. Reading 4 records
  it.

## (4) Item 25: is "positive-sum patterns only" exact for every candidate?

**Yes, for all ten.** Every candidate's rejection implies a positive trade-weighted sum:
- **The CR1 and CR2 members:** the estimate is sum S / sum N, and they reject only when est > m c se, with c > 0 at
  every level below 1/2 and se > 0.
- **C7:** its equal-weight member can be positive while the sum is not, but C7 is an intersection with C0's three CR1
  members, which cannot then reject.
- **C8:** it rejects only when sum S > c_a x scale, with scale >= 0 and c_a > 0.

Check K(1) confirms it. On a subset built so that the equal-weight estimate was positive with a non-positive sum on
5,676 patterns, the shortcut's exact numerators equal brute force over every phase and pattern, for all ten candidates
in both scopes, at every level.

**Minor 1 (a reading, not a change).** Two conditions carry the exactness:
- **c_a > 0 at every level.** It holds by construction, as an upper-tail quantile of a symmetric pivot at levels at or
  below 0.025. But it is not asserted, so the choosing row should check it in `q108_sn_table.csv` (reading 3).
- **No future candidate is the equal-weight member alone.** That is a note for any later declaration that adds
  candidates.

## (5) The measured costs and the suggested locks

**How the builder measured.** `q108_measure.py`:
- runs a sample of every null kind at the runs' full replication counts, each part in its own process, as the runner
  runs them;
- runs C8's 2,000,000-draw table in its own process;
- runs the parent's aggregation on a full-size table;
- multiplies each kind's mean seconds by its count in `null_plan`.

The synthetic pairs have 141 offers a session and a drift that makes the lax candidates reject, so the re-decision has
work to do. That is the right structure.

**Its limits, and my checks on them:**
- **Only 200- and 5,000-trade subsets and the two fixed streams are sampled.** The per-null cost depends on the
  sessions, not on the trade count, except in the scalar re-decision. In check L the scalar form costs **under 1 ms a
  call**, even at 92,637 trades.
- **How much the re-decision weighs.** On an exact null it re-decides at most about 1,200 distinct (candidate, cell)
  pairs, a second or so. On L = 42 the lax candidates (C0, C5, C7, C8, C9) reach the 100-cell cap, and the margins
  hardly reject (L). So real data's rejection counts cannot move the time materially.
- **The exact part, independently (K(3)):**
  - one data set and value with every candidate in both scopes took about 0.8 s at L = 63 and 10 s at L = 42;
  - with L = 55 and the alternating models, about 13 s per data set and value;
  - for 234 of them, about 50 minutes. That is inside the plan's 1 to 1.5 h.
- **The builder's per-part figures are in its hand-back, not committed.** I could not re-read them. They are cost
  figures, not results, but the lock's hours should cite where they come from.

**The locks.**
- **7 h and 1.5 GB for the calibration** follow if the hand-back's computed total is about 5.5 h or less, leaving the
  hour the lock rule expects beyond the expected end.
- **5 h for the validation** follows if its total is about 4 h or less. Two candidates instead of ten make it cheaper
  than the calibration on the Monte Carlo nulls, with the same exact enumeration.
- My checks do not contradict either.
- **Memory.** The Monte Carlo batch arrays (657 x 10,000) and the admission's integer arrays are tens of MB each, and
  the parts run one at a time. The plan's 1.2 GB with `--mem-gb 1.5` is consistent.
