## 1. Blocking

### B-1 (c2) (blocking): the engine-side check admits a policy under which the masked trades are silently not taken; the single-component case is not shown on the engine

- **Where.**
  - `extended/survey_route.py:694-704` (`check_policy_reads(policy_rrs, rt_trades)`).
  - Its docstring, lines 59-64: "a one-component design is admitted only with a policy asking for its w and nothing
    else".
  - `tests/test_survey_route.py:483-511`.
- **Evidence (read in the code; one synthetic probe, script in my scratchpad, not committed).**
  1. Vendored `zero_edge_decomposition.policy_rrs` (`scripts/zero_edge_decomposition.py:282-286`) returns
     `{leaf.rr for leaf in leaves if leaf.rr is not None}`. A leaf that keeps "the outcome process's own R"
     (`risk_policy.Leaf(rr=None)`, the vendored docstring's own meaning) is invisible to the check.
  2. Such a leaf reads the log's own outcome. That is `TierSizedDraw._outcome` with `_rr is None`, which reads
     `pnl / risked` (`propedge/campaign/sizing.py:539-545`), or `SharedDayDraw`, which reads `pnl` on the unsized path.
     `gate_blocks` builds `pnl` from `r_1` and `mae_1` (`scripts/score_fv3_grid.py:1206-1208`).
  3. Under the masking, `r_1` is NaN on every row whose w is not 1.
     - For a design with no w = 1 component, the column does not exist at all: `gate_blocks` raises `KeyError 'r_1'`.
     - So the caller must pad it with NaN. The m-4 child test does exactly that (`tests/survey_route_child.py:95`:
       "gate_blocks reads r_1: padded where absent").
     - **Probe** (the tests' one-component RL3 point, w = 1.5): `gate_blocks` without `r_1` raised `KeyError`. With
       it padded, `block.pnl` was finite on 0 of 28 trades and `r_by_rr[1.5]` on 28 of 28.
  4. The draw reads `u = NaN`. The D-315 "no outcome" branch fires only when `_rr is not None` (`sizing.py:730-733`),
     so the draw counts the trade among its own trades and returns NaN. `RaceOver.__call__` returns that NaN at
     `three_way_baseline.py:356`, before `_hand_out` and before `_law`.
     - C2-6 ("read the trade at no R") therefore never fires.
     - Nothing reaches the race log, so `check_taken_rr` cannot see the trade.
  5. **Probe:** `RiskPolicy("probe", Phase(combine=Leaf("c", 500, 1.5), funded=Leaf("f", 400, None)))`. Then
     `policy_rrs` gives `[1.5]`, and `SR.check_policy_reads([1.5], one)` **admitted** it.
  6. Before this cycle, the round-1 three-component table carried a live `r_1` on every row. There this leaf read a
     finite `pnl`, and the race refused it at C2-6. The masking turned that refusal into silence. The new check was
     meant to catch exactly this case, and it does not.
- **The failure it allows.**
  - Take a one-component design at w = 1.5 under a policy whose combine leaf asks R = 1.5 and whose funded leaf (or a
    tier of it) keeps the log's own R.
  - The check admits it. Every funded trade then goes untaken, silently, and the vendored statistics still count it.
  - The journey's funded phase earns nothing. The design reads worse than it is, or better if those trades would have
    lost. The survey's surrogate learns the wrong value, and no check, count or log shows it.
  - The same holds for any tier or phase of a state policy that leaves `rr` unset.
- **The over-refusal on the other side** (it fails closed, so it is not the blocking part):
  - `policy_rrs` also counts **flip** leaves (`source="flip"`, D-339). Their R belongs to the flip stream, not to the
    trade table.
  - So a steady-plus-flip policy (A1 or A2 = `flip`, half of the declared `policies` cells, `lever_space.py:118`) is
    refused whenever the flip R differs from w, although the flip leaf reads nothing of the table.
- **The ruling's "single-component case shown" is not on the engine.**
  - `test_the_policy_must_read_every_trade_at_its_own_w` calls `check_policy_reads` on float lists and pads a copy of
    the frame by hand. It never builds blocks, a job or a replay.
  - `test_every_taken_trade_was_read_at_its_own_w` builds its "race log" from `SR.own_rr` itself
    (`_race_log`, lines 454-462), so its positive case cannot disagree with `own_rr`. The real log's keys
    (`i = unit.start + historical_day`, `j = trade_index`, `three_way_baseline.py:598`, 627-629) are never compared
    with `own_rr`'s.
  - So nothing shows that, under an admitted policy, the engine actually takes the one-component design's trades at
    its own w, and does not pad them away. That was round 1's second failure: "every trade can go untaken, and the
    design reads as doing nothing".
- **Remedy.**
  1. `check_policy_reads` takes the policy, or its leaves with their sources, not `policy_rrs`.
     - It refuses, by name, any trading leaf (risk > 0, not abandon, `max_trades != 0`) of source `strategy` whose
       `rr` is None. It should do the same for `collect`, whose read I did not trace.
     - It treats `flip` leaves apart: either refuse them by name until F-13 rules (see m-4 below), or exclude them
       from the table-read set with a stated reason.
     - Keep the present float-level refusals.
  2. Add a light end-to-end test on synthetic bars, using `scripts/early_look.py`'s toy job (`toy_args`, with its
     protocol's combine and funded R both set to the design's w) and `three_way_baseline.simulate(..., log=True)`. Feed
     it the route's one-component table (w = 1.5, `r_1` padded as the child does). Assert:
     - trades are taken (> 0);
     - every lane's **real** race log passes `check_taken_rr(log, own_rr(...))`;
     - the vendored `no_outcome` count is 0;
     - the planted mixed policy above is refused by the corrected check.
     - The present check, run on that planted policy, must let it through, which shows the test can fail.
  3. State in the module docstring that the block's `pnl` (the vendored `r_1`) is NaN on every row off w = 1 (see
     m-2).
