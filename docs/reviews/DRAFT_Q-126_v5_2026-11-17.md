## Findings, by severity

### V-1 (MATERIAL). The regime-filter test can be gamed with unused or crowded decision points, and the residual says it cannot

**Where.**
- 2.5, "A regime filter" bullet. The test is that every change falls on a declared decision point, plus a count of
  regimes.
- 2.5's residual, two sentences:
  - "A declared regime filter cannot hide them: its decision points fix its regime length";
  - "A regime filter's decision points exclude them" (on edge reversals).
- `q126_arith.py` `in_band_refusals`: a flip is "refused only by the balance limit".

**The evidence** (deterministic constructions; my script `spot2.py`). On the judged block (657 sessions) a regime filter
declared in 42 to 50 needs 14 to 17 regimes, by `flip_counts`.
- **Two halves.** The side is long for 328 sessions, then short for 329. It has R = 2 and a mean side of 0.0015. Declare
  15 decision points, one of them at the switch. The side passes the decision-point test, the count and the balance
  limit (b = 0.5982).
- **Three regimes of 219.** R = 3 and the mean side is 0.3333. It passes in the same way.
- **Crowded points.** The side holds for about 320 sessions, alternates daily at 13 crowded decision points, then holds
  again. It has R = 14 and a mean side near 0. Every change is on a decision point, so it passes. The test reads
  only the count of points, not their spacing.
- **The edge-reversal side of the residual.** Three regimes of 219, each with one one-session reversal: R = 9, with
  8 changes. It passes if its 8 change points are declared, plus 5 to 8 unused ones to reach 14 to 17 regimes. So "A regime filter's decision
  points exclude them" is false as written.
- **The class's own odds.** Under the flips at 42 (B = 16 blocks), a draw with at most 2 changes has chance 0.0037, and
  one with 1 change 15/32768. These are sides the scope-Q statistics were never meaningfully validated on.

**The failure it allows.**
- **Where the test binds.** The band check is the only guard on persistence in two places:
  - in the second statistic's row (first column);
  - in every row of the second column, where n-4's reading makes the loosest kind and band the effective limit. With
    regime filters that limit is the balance limit alone.
- **The size hole.** A family whose realised side is one switch, or two fixed regimes of about a year, gets a one-sample
  line. This is section 1's few-sign-units mechanism: in a drifting judged block the line can pass it at many times its
  level, which is E-182's "worse than no line" case.
- **No outcome is read.** The family only writes extra decision points into its pinned text. A trend-following filter
  can also produce such a side with no gaming at all.

**Remedy (rule, before the pin).**
- **(a) Spacing.** Every regime between two consecutive declared decision points has a length in the band, except the
  span's first and last partial regimes. Better still, the points are spaced at the declared L. This closes crowding and
  the edge-reversal case.
- **(b) The flip's own runs floor**, one-sided. The number of declared decision points at which the non-tie series
  changes sign is at least the 1.25 % quantile of Bin(B - 1, 1/2). B is the number of regimes the span touches. Under
  independent regime signs this count is exactly that binomial.
  - At 657 the floor is 3 changes for B = 14 to 16 and 4 for B = 17. The miss under the band's flips is at most 0.0125 by construction (0.0112 at worst
    for B = 11 to 17).
  - The regime filter's in-band refusal bound becomes that miss plus the balance limit's. That is at most about
    0.0112 + 0.0247 < 0.05, so the refusal rule still holds.
  - Print and hold the bound per band and span, as for switching sides.
- **(c) The residual, restated.**
  - Strike both sentences.
  - State what remains: a side changing at only a few of its points, above the floor. An example is a 126-session
    alternation declared at 42: B = 16 and 5 changes, against P(at most 4) = 0.0592.
  - That residual is an ordinary class draw, which the class's validation covers.
  - In 51 to 62 (B = 11 to 14) the floor is 2 changes for B = 11 to 13. So three fixed regimes of about 220 still pass there as a
    55-session filter (P(at most 2 changes | B = 12) = 0.0327). That is the class's own tail, and it is stated.
- **If the lead pins the fifth text as it is**, the regime-filter kind is barred from any family's use until a revision
  under a new row adds (a) and (b). A family may still declare a switching side.

### V-2 (MINOR). The refusal rule and its figures hold for the dense case only; n should be read as the span's session count

**Where.**
- 2.5, the refusal rule: "No model of the band's own ... is refused with more than 0.05".
- Its figures, and the balance rule's "every session non-tie".

**The evidence** (illustration, seed 11, 40,000 sides per row, 42 to 50 at 657: runs [8, 26], b = 0.5982). Markov-50 is
refused:
- 0.0417 dense, against the printed bound 0.0462;
- **0.0507 when half the sessions trade**;
- 0.0604 at 0.3;
- 0.1020 at 0.15, about 99 non-tie sessions.

The regime filter at flip-44, under the balance limit alone, is refused 0.0126, 0.0193, 0.0205 and 0.0245 at those
rates. Thinning cancels nearby switches and adds variance to the mean side.

**The failure it allows.**
- **Power only.** Refusing more never raises a false pass.
- A sparse switching family in 42 to 50 is refused above the 0.05 the rule states, which is the operator's
  strict-line concern.
- The text also says "on n sessions" while R is counted on the shorter non-tie series. If n were read as the non-tie
  count, the intervals would collapse: at 99 non-tie sessions the 42 to 50 upper limit falls to about 8, and almost
  every Markov-50 side is refused.

**Remedy (a reading, no rule change).**
- n is the span's session count, never the non-tie count.
- The refusal rule and every printed refusal are for the dense case. A family trading on part of the span is refused
  more, and the family states its traded share beside its band.

### V-3 (MINOR). 4.4's "an upper bound on the share it removes from those already admitted" is true only as a joint share in the dense case

**Where.** 4.4, the "Its coverage cost" bullet.

**The evidence.**
- **As a joint share.** P(admitted and refused) <= P(refused) holds, but only in the dense case. The text itself says the
  sparse case refuses more.
- **As a conditional share.** It need not hold: P(refused | admitted) can exceed P(refused).
- **On the class it is smaller** (illustration, seed 11):
  - Markov-50: 0.0032 among the admitted, against 0.0098 overall;
  - Markov-30: 0.0007 among the admitted;
  - flip-21: 0 among the admitted.

  Admission favours many-run, balanced sides.

**Remedy (a reading).** "In the dense case, the share of all sides that the admission admits and the limit then removes
is at most this; on the class the share among the admitted is smaller still. The calibration measures the procedure
with the limit."

### V-4 (NOTE). The balance limit is a marginal envelope, not a joint one

- **The limit.** The limit sits at the class's 97.5 % envelope. So a side just under b with many runs (for example b_D =
  0.6651, long on 83 % of sessions, with evenly spread reversals) is admitted.
- **The class.** Such a side is rare among the class's admitted sides: Markov-50's admitted sides exceed b_D with
  0.0032 (illustration). Most class draws that imbalanced have few runs.
- **What it does.** F-1's extreme case is closed. The intermediate case rests on the procedure's calibration and on my
  earlier experiment, which E-184 chose not to run (adopted by design).
- **Remedy.** None needed before the pin. 8's residual might name it in one clause.

### V-5 (NOTE). The second column's effective floor is now R >= 5 for every family; the plan should say so

- **Why.** Under n-4's reading the check reduces to its loosest band. Since 51 to 62 dropped its floor, a switching side
  passes at 657 with R >= 5 whatever band it would otherwise declare, and with V-1 unremedied a regime filter passes with
  no runs floor at all.
- **Remedy.** 2.5's n-4 bullet prints the loosest limits per kind, held by T-0.

### The exact distributions

**The code.**
- **`markov_balance`.** The recursion is right. The state (side, count of long sessions) moves as follows:
  - long to long, with 1 - q, adds one long session;
  - short to long, with q, adds one;
  - short to short, with 1 - q, adds none;
  - long to short, with q, adds none.

  `sh` is updated from the old `lo_` before `lo_` is replaced. Then |mean| = |2k - n|/n.
- **`flip_balance`.** Its `np.roll` cannot wrap, since every partial sum lies within the array's ±W, with W = n. The
  phases are uniform over L.
- **`quantile`** is the least value with cdf >= 0.975.
- **`exceeds`** is P(> b).

**The spot-check** (seed 11, 200,000 draws, standard error about 0.0003):

| model | P(\|mean side\| > 0.5982), exact | simulated |
|---|---|---|
| flip-42 | 0.0186 | 0.0190 |
| Markov-50 | 0.0221 | 0.0226 |

- **The quantiles.** The simulated 97.5 % quantiles sit one grid step (2/657) above the exact ones. That is noise: the
  exact cdf at the quantile is 0.97509 for flip-42 and 0.97554 for Markov-50.
- **Markov-50's refusal bound.** It is 0.0462 (runs miss 0.0241 plus balance 0.0221). The simulated joint refusal is
  0.0441, with the runs miss alone at 0.0244, so the bound sits in the right direction.
