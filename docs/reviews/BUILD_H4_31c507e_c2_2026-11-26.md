## Blocking findings

### B-1 (blocking). Measure (31) at W = 126 counts accounts as lost, and payouts, after close 126

**Where.**
- `extended/h4_audit.py:596-606`, `encode_funded_accounts`: the ending kind is fixed at `max_window = JM.YEAR` (252).
- `extended/h4_audit.py:627`, `decode_funded_accounts`: hands that kind back as a fixed `"ending"`.
- `extended/journey_measures.py:337-340`, `account_ending`: returns a given `ending` without checking the window.
- `scripts/h4_rebaseline.py:1916-1919`: decodes the strings and calls `JM.funded_lifetime(unit_accs, 126)`.

**Evidence** (`probe_funded.py`, synthetic). Take one funded account: activated at close 100, breached at close 200,
payouts arriving at closes 110, 150 and 190.
- Read directly, as `user_beside.journey_record` does (it carries no `ending`), W = 126 gives `window`: censored,
  1 payout, 26 sessions.
- Read through H4's encode and decode, W = 126 gives `breach`: lost, 3 payouts, 100 sessions.
- At W = 252 the two agree.

So at W = 126, every funded account that is still open at close 126 but ends by close 252 becomes a loss at its later
end. Its payouts and sessions after close 126 are counted too. That breaks MEASURES 1.1 ("every field read at horizon
h is computed from closes 0 .. h only") and (31)'s censoring rule. The existing test
(`tests/test_h4_build.py:675-733`) only checks that one account decodes. It never reads at W = 126.

**What it changes.** The `funded_lifetime_payouts_126` and `funded_lifetime_sessions_126` rows of every
`h4_readings_*` file already written: the p10, p50 and p90, `sq_n_censored` and `n_finite`. Measure (31) is a beside
measure. It is not in W42's `REQUIRED` and not in `h4_w42_input`. The units files themselves are correct: each string
keeps `ended_on` and the arrivals.

**Remedy.**
- Re-derive the ending at the reading window. In `decode_funded_accounts`, or in the summariser, an account with
  `ended_on` blank or after W is `window`; otherwise use the stored kind. Better still: store the raw flags, not a kind.
- Add a test with an account that ends between closes 127 and 252, read at both windows.
- No H4-B re-run is needed. `merge_h4_b_parts` recomputes the readings from the units files.

### B-2 (blocking). The exact-run identity fails on every part. No treatment is declared, and the manifests cannot support one

**Where.**
- `scripts/h4_rebaseline.py:1629-1659`: the m-4 fix compares `first_pass`, `first_pay`, `rel[:h+1]` and the arrivals,
  then adds one to a single counter.
- `scripts/h4_rebaseline.py:2041` and `2163`: that counter is summed across arms, horizons and lanes.

**Evidence.**
- Every part manifest has `exact_differs > 0`. p14 p1, for example, has 1406 of 278528 lane-checks.
- MEASURES 1.1 admits a cut long run only "with U1's identity checked on the cut runs (the event days up to h equal to
  the exact run's)". The declaration's section 7 row (l.266) ties the exact runs to "U1's identity on the cut runs
  (MEASURES 1.1, m-8)".
- MEASURES 6 ("What follows", l.1162-1164) says: "with any [difference], each kind of difference is described and a
  reason or a treatment ... is declared under a row before the held-out runner is declared".
- The counter cannot say which kind of difference occurred:
  - an event day moved, which is the D-620 (3)(f) case where `Views` reads the exact run;
  - or only `rel` differs, for example the abandon taken at the long run's close h (`lockstep.py:1878`,
    `day + 1 < horizon_days`), which is causal and leaves the event days equal.
- It also cannot say on which arm or at which horizon. It counts lane-checks, not "the count of starts where they
  differ" per reading and horizon (section 12, l.395).
- No manifest names the record each reading read (m-8).
- On the synthetic job, `probe_exact.py` found no difference at all, so it cannot classify the real ones.
- Q-172 (E-256, E-264) found zero differences, but on the as-traded point arm and on event days only.

**What it changes.**
- The deciding `h4_units_*` `off` values are the long-run records, as the code was built to write them.
- If any differences are event-day differences, W42's inputs at h = 21, 63 and 126 differ from MEASURES' reading on
  those units. Examples: `first_pass_days`, `first_payout_days`, `net_month_m` and `peak_month_m`.
- The share is small, at most about 0.5 % of lane-checks per part. Its direction is unknown.

**Remedy.**
1. Classify the differences. A light run can do it: re-make the long run and the exact runs at `EXACT_HORIZONS` on
   the `off` arm for the 1 % audit sample's units, about 1,300 units per baseline (`probe_sample.py`). For each
   (start, seed, h), record which of these moved: first pass, first payout, an arrival day, arrival cash, `rel` and at
   which close.
2. The lead declares a treatment for each kind in a row before H4-C. For example: event-day differences read the
   exact run, as `Views.at` does; a `rel`-only difference at close h keeps the long run, with its reason.
3. Fix the runner for later use. Count by arm, horizon and kind. Count starts as well as lanes. Name the record read
   in each readings manifest.

Nothing already drawn needs to be re-run, unless the treatment is "read the exact run's record". That would re-make
the exact runs for the `off` arm only.

## N-1: the walk speedup is bit-for-bit identical

**What was compared.**
- `_walk_one` (`three_way_baseline.py:282-313`) and the inline move branch (`:505-535`) against the vendored-path
  originals: `RD.walk_run` read in `WALK_CHUNK` pieces, `RD.walk_outcome`, and `MV.move_outcomes(..., detail=True)`.
- The comparison used `==` on floats (with the sign of zero) and on exit codes. For the move it also covered `x`,
  `moved`, `G2` and `down2`.

**Cases** (`probe_walk.py`): 30,000 walks and 30,000 two-phase moves, with:
- steps s in {1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 12, 30};
- barriers at exact lattice multiples, and within ±1e-13, ±5e-10·s and ±2e-9·s of them (both sides of `SNAP`);
- barriers inside the first step, whole ticks, and fractional;
- n in {1, 2, 3, random, `WALK_MAX`}, for each phase;
- u = 0.5 ties, all-0.5 rows, and u set exactly to the top, bottom and both-barrier probabilities;
- every terminal state reached: walk target, stop and time; move target, stop, breakeven and time.

**Result: 0 differences** against each reference.

**The uniform stream and the key record** (`probe_keys.py`). On real `CoinLanes` (tests' role seed, no literal),
`RaceOver._walk_uniforms` was compared with the old `RaceOver._reader` read in 32-step pieces. The comparison covered
480 cases: 4 starts, twin on and off, NQ and MNQ, totals from 1 to 2048. Both the uniform arrays and the KeyGuard keys
and `checked` counts were **identical**.

**Production evidence beside it.** Every H4-B part's independent scalar oracle replayed every sampled race-arm row on
the scalar key path with no refusal. The part manifests' `oracle_checked_trades` sum to about 6.0 million rows across
the nine finished parts.

**What pins the identity, and what does not.**
- `test_n1` (`tests/test_h4_build.py:743-760`) checks only `_walk_one` against `walk_outcome`, on five shapes. It kills
  the SNAP mutant (N1f) and nothing else.
- Mutants it lets through:
  - N1a, the bottom probability using `rho_u`;
  - N1b, phase 2 never armed;
  - N1c, phase 2 re-reading phase 1's uniforms;
  - N1d, twin ignored;
  - N1e, arming at `x > 1` instead of `x >= 1`;
  - N1g, breakeven booked as a stop;
  - N1h, the walk key not recorded in the KeyGuard.
- The cross-file oracle (`test_three_way_baseline.py`'s `checks`) kills N1a, N1b, N1c and N1g.
- **N1d, N1e and N1h survive every test.** The code is right on all three (the probes above). These are gaps in the
  tests only. See m-a.
