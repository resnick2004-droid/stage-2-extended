## Blockers

### B-1. The walked mirror is not the coin twin for limit entries

**Where:** sections 5.2 (lines 258-261), 5.3 (lines 277-281), 6.1's limit rows (lines 321-323), and 9.4 (limit trade
sets are eligible for T\*). Also section 1.2 (lines 126-128) and section 0 (line 72): "P-16 ... zero by construction".

**What the text does.** A limit rests at the alert's price P from alert + L. It is void if marketable at that moment
and fills only on a trade-through by one tick. Its "twin" is the opposite side, booked at the same fill bar and
reflected about the trigger (`fill_bar="pair"`, `mirror_booking="symmetric"`), and `x_i = (net(side) - net(mirror)) / 2`.

**Why it is not the twin.** CHARTER section 3 and feature-rules section 5 define the coin twin as "the same entries
with a fair coin choosing the side, so supply, cost and schedule cancel". A limit's fill depends on its side:

- A buy limit at P rests only if the price rose during the latency, and it fills only when the price comes back down
  through P.
- The mirror of that fill is a sell entered as the price falls through P. In effect it is a sell stop, and a
  coin-sided sell limit could never have filled there.

So the comparison is "buy the dip-through against sell the break-down" at the same moment. Any serial structure of
1-minute NQ prices at the tick scale gives that comparison a sign that has nothing to do with the signal's side. Under
pure tick-level mean reversion (bid-ask bounce in bar extremes), the limit side wins mechanically.

**Evidence (a small synthetic check, run once, outputs discarded).** The draft's own walker
(`extended/walker.py`, `trade(..., fill_bar="pair", mirror_booking="symmetric")`) was run on synthetic 1-minute bars
built from a tick random walk, with and without independent tick-level noise (a bounce, so the observed prices mean-
revert). The setup: fair-coin sides, the limit at `price_at(alert)`, latency 2, a 30-minute window, gross R.

- The limit entries' mean walked-mirror "lift" for a **fair coin** came out several standard errors above zero.
- The market-entry control and the driftless no-noise limit control were both within noise.

Reproduce it as follows: about 150 synthetic days, alerts every 15 minutes, a 6-tick stop at 1R, noise uniform on
{-2, ..., 2} ticks. It is light: under a minute, no bars read. The size on NQ is unknown; the sign is structural.

**What it allows:**

- A limit T\* can pass C1 at 0.05/182 with no directional information in P-14's side. The mechanics of the limit
  produce the "lift", and C2 then prices it as an edge. That is a false CARRIED.
- P-16 at limit entries has a non-zero expected walked-mirror lift, so its "check, expectation zero" (8.1 item 1) would
  fail. Or worse, it would be read as noise.
- K0 (9.7, 10.2) cannot catch this. Its nulls flip the signs of `|v_i|` independently of the path, so they simulate
  the null that B-1 shows is false.

**Remedy (pick one before the pin):**

- **(a) The exact coin twin on the offer population, no seed.** At alert + L, at most one side's limit at P is not
  marketable (neither when the price is at P). Call it the resting side, and `n_i` that resting limit's net R (zero if
  it does not fill). With `s_i = +1` when the signal's side is the resting side and `-1` otherwise, the exact lift over
  a fair coin is `x_i = s_i x n_i / 2`. It is zero in expectation for any coin side, whatever the price dynamics.
  - The chain for limit trade sets is built on the resting side's fills, which do not depend on the side.
  - An offer whose signal side is void keeps its slot, as a stood-aside trade does (5.5's declared form).
  - P-16's limit lift then really is zero by construction.
- **(b) Make limit trade sets described only** (ineligible for T\*). They are the operator's R-4 "modelled beside" and
  remain on the menu. P-16's limit-entry walked-mirror lift is printed as a measured placebo of the mechanics.

Under either remedy, say in 5.3 that the walked mirror is the twin for market entries only.

### B-2. The latency-5 identity cannot hold as declared (P-16's alert; P-14's five bars)

**Where:** sections 5.1 (lines 249-250), 5.2 (lines 262-266), 6.1 `L`, 16.2 (P-16's latency factor), and 17.3 item 2
(lines 746-748).

**(a) P-16.** The text says an offer's alert is "the mark", the entry goes in at alert + latency, and "at latency 5 the
offers' entries are the registered ones". P-16's registration says the opposite:

- Its entries are "at the first bar opening after the mark ... an alert sent before the mark -- the mark and the side
  are known in advance -- the operator's five minutes counted from it". That is
  `vendor/stage2/prereg/P-16_rc-s150-all-nr.md` lines 168-172.
- The walker identity runs P-16 at delay 0 only. `identity/walker_identity.py` line 116 has: "the walker's market
  order is live at the mark: delay 0 only".

So at L = 5 the text puts P-16's entries five minutes after its registered ones, and `TRADES_SHA256` fails. 5.2 makes
that failure refuse every later step.

Latency is also not a parameter of P-16 at all. Its marks and sides are fixed in advance, so "3 and 2 minutes" only
moves its entries 2 or 3 minutes off its own clock. That is two thirds of P-16's 1,969,920 protocol cells, all
described, measuring a clock shift.

**(b) P-14.** Stage 2's entry is "five bars late": `fast_walk.entry_bar(o, t, 5, last)`, the sixth bar present after
the alert slot (`vendor/stage2/propedge/research/fast_walk.py` lines 24-27). The walker's market rule is "the first
present bar at or after alert + L" in clock minutes (`walker.py` lines 19-23 and 280-307). The two agree only when all
five minutes have bars. On any offer with a missing bar in those minutes, the A3-cache identity "offer for offer,
every column" fails. Such offers include thin overnight minutes, which R-2 now brings in.

**Remedy:**

- P-16: declare its timing as registered (entry at the mark, no latency axis; 16.2's P-16 factor drops from 3 to 1),
  or define its alert as the mark less five minutes and drop the variants anyway. Either way, write it in 5.1.
- P-14: either define latency L as Stage 2's `entry_bar` count of present bars (so L = 5 is the cache), or keep clock
  minutes (the more realistic for hand entry) and declare the identity's allowed differences. The second option means
  only offers whose five minutes after the alert all have bars are compared, the rest counted and printed.
- Add a T-2 case with a missing bar.

### B-3. The chain can pick its trade from a future fill once resting orders exist

**Where:** section 5.5 (lines 304-308), with 6.1's limit window (30 minutes).

"The offers of a session in time order ... the first offer whose order fills; then each later offer whose order goes
live at least five minutes after the previous chain trade's exit."

**The failure.** A limit from offer 1 goes live at minute 5 and works to minute 35. Offer 2's order goes live at minute
12 and fills at minute 15. Offer 1's limit fills at minute 20.

- Read in offer order, the chain takes offer 1, the first offer "whose order fills", and skips offer 2. At minute 15 a
  real user is already in offer 2's position, because nobody can know at minute 12 that offer 1 will fill at minute 20.
- Read in fill order, offer 1's limit is still working while offer 2's position is open. That is two orders or
  positions at once, against one position per firm.

Either reading is wrong in one direction, and the text does not say which it means. The same holds between two limit
offers.

**Remedy (one sentence each):**

- The chain is built in time order of fills.
- No order is placed while a position is open or another entry order is working. A new offer's order goes live only
  if the account is flat and nothing is working; otherwise it is not placed, and that is counted.
- A working limit is cancelled at a fill of nothing else, because nothing else can be working.

Then add a T-3 case with two overlapping limits and a limit overlapping a market order. Under B-1 (a), the chain for
limit sets is built from the resting side's fills, by the same rule.

## Should-fix

### S-1. One common trade set: the draft's reason is right only for Bonferroni (the answer to point 1)

**Where:** sections 9.4, 16.3 (lines 703-705), section 19 item 1 (lines 790-794), and Appendix C #3.

**What the draft gets right.** Bonferroni over J per-group tests at 0.05/(182 J) leaves Q-126's calibrated range,
which runs from k = 13 to k = 20, that is 0.05/182 down to 0.05/420.

- J = 2 gives 0.05/364, inside the range.
- J = 3 gives 0.05/546 and J = 4 gives 0.05/728, both outside.

So "for J of 3 or more" is correct arithmetic.

**What is wrong.** "A per-firm trade set needs ... a calibration at levels below k = 20's" holds only for Bonferroni
(and for Holm, whose first step is Bonferroni's). Two standard procedures keep **every** test at the family's full level
0.05/(k(k+1)), so they need nothing Q-126 does not already calibrate:

- **Fixed-sequence (hierarchical) testing.** Stage B picks each group's own trade set T_g by 9.4's rule restricted to
  the group, and removes duplicates. It orders the distinct T_g by a declared choice-block key (descending `Lstar`,
  ties by Appendix P's order) and writes the order in `fp_pick.csv`. Stage C tests them in that order, each at
  0.05/(k(k+1)), and stops at the first failure. The groups whose T_g passed with every earlier one go on to C2 and
  the floors.
  - The family-wise error is at most 0.05/(k(k+1)) under any mix of true and false nulls (strong control), because the
    first true null reached is rejected with probability at most the level, and testing stops there.
  - K0 runs per T_g. The judged reads are the same three purposes.
- **Intersection-union.** The carried design is the map g -> T_g. C1 requires every distinct T_g to pass, each at the
  full level. That is one claim and one level, the form already used for F-B's and F-03's intersection lines. It needs
  no ruling on Stage 2 D-621 (5). It costs power, since every group must pass, but the T_g are the same offers and side
  at different brackets, so their lifts are strongly correlated and the loss is smaller than for independent tests.

**Does the common T\* defeat the point?** In part.

- The operator's own example (MFF's larger contract cap changes the stop widths a plan can afford) concerns the
  bracket, which is exactly the axis the draft makes common.
- Under 9.4 (a), T\* is the width most groups can spend. A group whose own best width differs is judged on someone
  else's bracket, and cannot carry at all if T\*'s width is not allowed at its caps (6.3).
- What the draft does keep per firm (risk, mix, flip, session rule) is real. The lift itself is a property of the
  signal at a bracket, not of the firm, so the draft's reading is defensible, but it is not the operator's request.

**What per-firm trade sets cost:**

- Nothing on the choice block: selection there costs power, not size.
- On the judged block, up to four walked trade sets instead of one, still under the three declared purposes.
- Up to four confirmations, one per distinct carried T_g, in the same fixed sequence at 0.05, or one window read for
  all of them. Section 14's "one confirmation serves every carried group" no longer holds.
- Multiplicity: none beyond the family's k, under either procedure.

**Remedy.** Put both procedures to the lead under item 1. My recommendation is the **fixed sequence**, with a ruling
that D-621 (5)'s "one cell reaches judgement" is satisfied by a sequence whose family-wise error is the family's
level. If the lead keeps D-621 (5) literally, use the **intersection-union**, which needs no ruling. Keep the common T\*
only if the lead judges the operator's example to be about sizing rather than brackets, and say so to the operator.
Either alternative changes 9.4, 9.5, 10.3, 10.5, 13, 14, 16.2's judged rows (up to 4 lift tests, 4 K0s) and Appendix
C #3.

## Section 19, item by item

1. **One common trade set or one per firm:** see S-1. Fixed-sequence (with a ruling) or intersection-union (no ruling)
   keeps every test at 0.05/(k(k+1)) inside Q-126's range. The draft's "needs a calibration below k = 20's" is true
   only of Bonferroni.
2. **What a carried design claims:** the vehicle reading is sound under E-008 with E-023 (1). It requires three things
   of the registration:
   - it freezes pi\*_g;
   - it claims only the lift;
   - the confirmation tests only the lift, with the protocol's scorecard descriptive.

   Several groups sharing one lift is fine under the common T\*. Under S-1, each distinct T_g needs its own
   confirmation line.
