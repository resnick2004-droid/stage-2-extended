# Red-team read of Wave 3.4: the design search's engine (wt/wt-features 5ebb6d5)

Read cold on 2026-11-19 by wt-scout. **Target:** `git diff master...5ebb6d5` on `wt/wt-features`, which adds
`extended/search_space.py`, `surrogate.py`, `proposer.py`, `plateau.py`, their four test files and
`tests/search_toys.py`, and changes `extended/lever_space.py` and `extended/components.py` (per-date session windows
from `day_calendar.anchored_starts`). **Specification read:** the restart plan (4.3, Waves 3.4 and 5), the lever table,
CHARTER 3.3-3.9, rows E-208, E-210, E-221 (O-1), E-230, E-231 (3), and the Wave 2 template draft on `wt/wt-lit`
(`prereg/TEMPLATE_design_search.md`, section 6), as direction. The brief asked for an attack "without being told what
to expect". It named the areas to attack and hinted at no conclusion, so there was no hint to ignore.

Nothing here reads a bar, a walk or a result file. Every figure below comes from a **synthetic** check that I ran in a
scratch copy outside the repository (the scripts are described in section 4 so they can be rerun). None of them is a
research result.

## Verdict: **blocked** (must be re-declared before the merge)

There are **2 blocking findings, 11 minor, 4 notes.** The engine does what most of its docstrings say. Its door
refuses held-out tags and blocks, and I could not get round it. The sampler counts every cell it draws, refused ones
included. The surrogate does not chase pure noise, even on the full 29-dimension lever space. The per-date windows
stay inside their sessions on every search-year date.

Two defects change what the engine guarantees, and both have to be fixed before a search declaration relies on it:

- **B-1.** The door that leads to the surrogate and the plateau check is tied neither to the cell ledger nor to the
  declared blocks and seeds. A design that was never counted can be read, fitted and accepted as a finalist. A
  finalist can also be re-read on blocks and seeds the caller picks.
- **B-2.** The plateau check measures each neighbour against the finalist's own **selection** reading. That builds in
  the winner's curse. With noise in the readings, a true plateau is refused at any tolerance tight enough to refuse a
  spike of comparable depth.

## 1. Blocking findings

### B-1. The readings door does not bind to the cell ledger or to the declared protocol

**Where.** `extended/search_space.py:725-759` (`admit_one`, `admit`). `extended/surrogate.py:294-321` (`fit`).
`extended/plateau.py:189-207` (`check`: the finalist is passed through `admit_one` only; neighbours are read on
`f.blocks` and `f.seeds`, whatever those are). `extended/proposer.py:212-214` (`propose` records its batch under a
`round_index` and seed that nothing checks).

**Evidence.** `admit_one` checks the type, the tag, the blocks against `reader.blocks_of`, the seeds being integers and
the value being finite. It takes no ledger and no declared protocol. `Surrogate.fit` requires one protocol *within* a
fit (`surrogate.py:305-308`), but that can be any protocol. `plateau.check` takes any admitted `Reading` as its
finalist. The module docstring says the ledger "counts every cell the sampler, the round proposer or the plateau check
proposes" (`search_space.py:52-54`). That is true, but a reading never has to be one of those cells.

**Concrete failing inputs** (scratch probe 2, on `tests/search_toys.toy_space()`):

1. A design that was never drawn (`search_toys.SMOOTH_ARGMIN`), with no ledger row:
   - `Surrogate.fit(survey_readings + [Reading(SMOOTH_ARGMIN, ...)])` fits 17 readings;
   - `plateau.check(Reading(SMOOTH_ARGMIN, ...), ...)` returns `accepted=True`;
   - the finalist's id is still absent from the ledger afterwards.
   
   So a hand-picked design, or one chosen with the held-out years in mind, reaches the finalists without being counted
   (CHARTER 3.3 (f); the template's section 13, "every design tried is counted").
2. A finalist read on `blocks=(("2021-03-01", "2021-03-31"),)` (one month) with any seed tuple is admitted. Its
   neighbours are then read on that month and accepted. A caller can re-read the best design on several sub-blocks or
   seed sets and pass the luckiest one to the plateau check. Nothing records those re-reads, and nothing refuses a
   protocol other than the declared one.
3. `propose(..., round_index=1, seed=11)` followed by `propose(..., round_index=1, seed=12)` on the same ledger records
   both batches as `round-1` (4 rows, seeds 11 and 12). Nothing refuses a second round 1, so the caller can choose
   which of the two to read. The rows are counted, but the choice is free.

**The failure it allows.** Selection after the fact that the ledger does not see: designs read outside the count, a
finalist read on a protocol picked after a look, and rounds re-drawn under new seeds. All of these sit upstream of the
k-counted held-out test, and the count k spends would understate the search.

**Remedy.**
- Add a frozen `Protocol(measure, blocks, seeds)`, taken from the declaration.
- Change the door to `admit(readings, *, ledger, protocol)`. It refuses a reading whose design id is not a `proposed`
  row of the ledger, and a reading whose blocks, seeds or measure differ from the protocol. `Surrogate.fit`,
  `proposer.propose` and `plateau.check` take the same two objects.
- `plateau.check` also refuses a finalist that is not a `survey` or `round-<i>` row.
- `CellLedger.record` refuses a second `round-<i>` under a different seed, and a role seed that differs from the one
  declared for that role.
- Tests: the three inputs above, each refused, and a mutation that drops the ledger check, which those tests must
  catch.

### B-2. The plateau metric uses the finalist's own selection reading (the winner's curse)

**Where.** `extended/plateau.py:211` (`delta = got.value - f.value`) and `:30-34` (the rule). The tests use only
noise-free objectives (`tests/test_plateau.py:36-48`: `plateau_fn`, `spike_fn` and `slope_fn` are all deterministic).

**Evidence.** The finalist is, by construction, the lowest of many noisy readings, so its reading carries the most
favourable noise. Each neighbour is a different design with its own noise, so on average d = neighbour - finalist is
positive even on an exactly flat surface. Scratch probe 4 used `toy_space`, a surface equal everywhere plus
design-specific noise of standard deviation 0.3 (`0.3 * search_toys.noise`). In each of 20 surveys of 64 designs the
lowest reading was taken as the finalist, under the tests' own rule shape (steps 0.05 on x1-x3 and 1 on n, radius 1,
`side="worse"`, `min_share=1.0`):

| what was checked | tolerance 0.1 | 0.3 | 0.6 | 0.9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| true plateau accepted (of 20) | 0 | 0 | 1 | 3 |
| spike 0.6 deep (2 sd) accepted (of 20) | | | | 9 |

At the tolerance of 0.9 (3 sd), the spike is accepted three times as often as the true plateau.

**The failure it allows.**
- With a tolerance smaller than the selection bias, every real plateau is refused, and the search returns no finalist
  or only those that happened to draw unlucky neighbours.
- With a tolerance large enough to pass real plateaus, spikes of the same depth pass.

The memory note "check power of strict rules" applies directly: the rule has no shown power. CHARTER 3.3 (e) pins the
plateau rule's metric with 3.9's inputs, so the metric has to be right before anything is pinned.

**Remedy.**
- Compare the neighbours with an **unselected** reading of the finalist: a re-read on a fresh, declared seed set (its
  own spawn key under S3). Alternatively, express the tolerance in units of the paired difference's standard error
  (`Reading.se`) and require it to be given.
- Record which form was used in `PlateauVerdict`.
- Add a test of size and power: a noisy true plateau must be accepted, and a noisy spike of the declared depth refused,
  each at a rate fixed before the run, over at least 20 seeds.

## 2. Minor findings

**m-1. The plateau check can leave levers unchecked, and a categorical step on a lever that others depend on reads
nothing.** `plateau.py:84-96, 128-147`. `PlateauRule.steps` may name any subset of the finalist's active levers.
- Probe 2 (C): a spike of depth 5 in x3, with x3 unstepped, gives `accepted=True`; with x3 stepped, it is refused.
- `"cells"` on `<slot>.T1` or `<slot>.on` builds neighbours whose dependent levers (T4, T5, the window) are not
  redrawn. Every one of them is refused as `invalid`, skipped, and never counted against the finalist. Probe 2 (D):
  3 of 5 neighbours are refused and the verdict is decided by the `risk` steps alone.

*Remedy.* `validate` refuses a rule that leaves an active searched lever unstepped unless the rule lists it as exempt,
and the verdict records the exempt levers. A parent categorical either redraws its dependents (at their centre cell)
or is refused as a step.

**m-2. A plateau check can be re-run under another rule without trace.** The verdict carries every neighbour's delta
(`plateau.py:212-213`), so a looser tolerance can be applied after a look. Re-running the check records the same
neighbours again under the same role and leaves no record of the rule. *Remedy.* Put the rule's sha256 in each
`plateau` ledger row and in the verdict, and refuse a second check of one finalist in one ledger.

**m-3. A reading carries no outcome horizon.** `search_space.py:707-718`. `blocks` are "(start, end) ISO days", but
nothing says whether `end` bounds the start days or every session an outcome reads. A 12-month measure read from starts
in 2023 reads 2024 sessions and still passes as `("choice",)`. That breaks CHARTER 3.6 (d)'s horizon embargo.
*Remedy.* Add `Reading.reads_through` (the last session any outcome read). `admit` refuses it when it is after
2023-12-29.

**m-4. A reading is not immutable, and subclasses are admitted.**
- `Reading.design` is a plain dict inside a frozen dataclass. Probe 2 (F) mutated `x1` after admission, and the change
  stuck.
- `admit_one` uses `isinstance` (`:727`), so a subclass can override `tag`, `blocks` or `id` as properties. My
  toggling-blocks subclass happened to be refused, because `blocks` is read several times, but the route is open.

*Remedy.* Freeze the design at construction (a `MappingProxyType` over a copy, or a sorted tuple of items), and test
`type(r) is Reading`.

**m-5. Conduct tier 1 at sampling is a name list, and two edges leak.** `search_space.py:295-307`.
- `firms=True` passes, because `bool` is an `int` equal to 1 (probe 2, G).
- Names are matched case-sensitively: `"Copy_Trade_Across_Accounts"` is sampled and returned (probe 2, H).
- On the real lever space no lever value names a tier-1 practice, so the list is vacuous there. Tier 1 is actually
  enforced by the fixed `overlap`/`firms` values and, downstream, by `triggers` (news, BLS and FOMC windows, the side
  rule). The docstring's claim that tier 1 is refused at sampling should say that.

*Remedy.* `type(firms) is int`; match casefolded names; state in the docstring where tier 1 is really enforced.

**m-6. The weekday-span bounds exclude legitimate windows on most dates.** `search_space.py:580-597`. The offsets are
bounded by the tightest date. Computed from `session_slots()`: london's offsets stop at 325 and asia's at 415 (both set
by the US/UK mismatch weeks), and evening's at 55 (set by CST dates). So:
- outside the mismatch weeks (most of the year), no London window can reach 07:30-08:25 CT;
- on CST dates no Asia window can reach 00:55-01:55 CT;
- on CDT dates no evening window can reach 18:00-18:55 CT.

None of these windows is illegal. This is latent while the overnight slots are refused (`OvernightRefused`), but it
shapes any search after H2. *Remedy.* Declare it as an interpretation in the lever table, or anchor a window to its
session's end as well (an `anchor` lever: start or end), or clip per date with the clip recorded.

**m-7. Tests that cannot fail, and mutations that survive.** I ran 23 mutations in a scratch copy (section 4). 17 were
caught and 6 survived:
- the proposer no longer excluding already-read designs (`proposer.py:132`);
- plateau stepping inactive levers (`plateau.py:128`);
- the incumbent taken as the **max** predicted mean (`proposer.py:189`);
- `noise_share` returning a constant 0.99 (`surrogate.py:359`): nothing asserts that it reads *low* on signal;
- `<=` changed to `<` in `holds`;
- the redundant held-out-word check (note n-1).

In addition, `tests/test_surrogate.py:231-235` (`test_mutation_door_left_open_is_caught`) contains no assertion and
passes on any code. The noise test runs at one seed (21, `:116`). The module docstring says "Thresholds were set before
the first run" (`:7`), while the test's docstring says "the thresholds 0.8 and 0.5 are the first run's" (`:115`); those
two statements disagree.

*Remedy.* A test for each survivor; an assertion in the door-mutation test; the noise test over a declared set of
seeds; one consistent sentence about the thresholds.

**m-8. Reproducibility from seeds holds only for one set of library versions.** Scrambled Sobol, LHS and L-BFGS-B
depend on the scipy and numpy versions. `pyproject.toml` pins neither, and `FitRecord` and the ledger rows record
neither. *Remedy.* Record `scipy.__version__` and `numpy.__version__` in `FitRecord` and in the ledger's variant rows,
and pin them in the search declaration.

**m-9. The finalist order rule is absent, and anything admitted can be a finalist.** The template's section 6 requires
an order rule declared before the survey. Here `Round.mean` and `Round.acquisition` rank candidates, and
`plateau.check` takes any admitted reading. The test `test_the_surrogate_and_the_proposer_name_no_finalist`
(`test_surrogate.py:238-247`) only greps attribute names. *Remedy.* Covered by B-1's ledger binding, plus an
`order(ledger, readings, rule)` function, declared and tested, that is the only source of `plateau.check`'s finalists.

**m-10. The proposer is single-objective, and limit readings may come from another protocol.** CHARTER 3.3 (d) and the
template say constrained **multi-objective** optimisation. `propose` optimises the composite, with limits applied as a
probability of feasibility (`proposer.py:175-182, 188`). Each limit's readings pass the door separately, and nothing
requires them to share the composite's blocks and seeds. *Remedy.* Either declare single-objective plus limits as the
template's reading (the frontier then reported from the readings), or build the multi-objective form. In both cases
require one protocol across the composite and the limits.

**m-11. Windows follow two conventions, with no bridge between them.** Search designs hold offsets read per date
(`search_space.py:350-359`). `components.Design(day=None)` checks **absolute** windows against the intersection bounds
(`components.py:132-158`; asia `(120, 540)`). A runner that builds `components.Design(day=None)` from a search
design's offsets reads a different design, or refuses a good one. *Remedy.* Add
`search_space.to_components(design, day) -> components.Design(day=day)`, with a test across the three distinct date
patterns.

## 3. Notes

- **n-1.** The held-out-word check in `admit_one` (`:730-733`) is redundant, because `tag != "search"` refuses those
  tags anyway (mutation M2 survives). It is harmless.
- **n-2.** The docstring of `test_surrogate.py:109-115` types diagnostic figures (0.09, 0.08, 0.98, 0.72) from a failed
  synthetic run, and cites a "comparison over 30 seeds" that has no artefact. These are not results, but the comparison
  cannot be reproduced from anything in the tree.
- **n-3.** NY offsets 0-25 (08:30-08:55 CT) fall inside the closed 08:30-08:59 window (`triggers.CLOSED_*`). Those
  cells are legal but cannot fill on their first marks. They are spent cells, not leaks.
- **n-4.** The surrogate's MAP fit (marginal likelihood plus the SAAS prior) is neither of the template's two options
  ("fixed here or chosen by cross-validation"). The declaration should name it as the fixed procedure, with
  `PRIOR_TAU`, `BOUNDS`, `START` and the restarts pinned.

## 4. What I tried that found nothing

- **Getting round the held-out tags.**
  - Tags `held_out`, `HELD-OUT`, `judged`, `confirmation`, an empty tag and `other` are all refused.
  - Blocks are refused when they end 2023-12-30 or 2023-12-31 (the seal starts 2023-12-30, `reader.SEAL_START`), when
    they touch 2024, when they reach 2026-07-17 or start before 2020-01-02, and when they are reversed (ValueError).
  - A datetime string ending `2023-12-29T23:59` resolves to the choice block, which is correct.
  - A `Prediction`, a `Surrogate` and a dict are refused.
  - `believe` conditions only on the surrogate's own means, through a private `_GP`.
- **Bars, results and the confirmation window.** The new modules import `reader` for `blocks_of` only.
  `anchored_starts` is zoneinfo arithmetic and `search_days` is weekday arithmetic. Nothing writes under `results/`,
  and nothing reads NQ from 2026-07-17.
- **Per-date windows.**
  - The search years hold three distinct window patterns (winter, the mismatch weeks, summer).
  - The held-out years' calendar gives the same bounds, so a search design is valid there too.
  - No trading day straddles a daylight-saving change (all changes fall on Sunday, before the 17:00 reopen).
  - Early-close days are day-wide voids (`day_calendar`).
  - The RL3 live offset is 30 on every date.
  - The NY end clip leaves the last mark at 15:05 CT, before the 15:08 last entry.
  - `tests/test_lever_space.py`, `test_components.py` and `test_day_calendar.py` pass.
- **The count.** Refused cells are recorded with their class. Every L of an active component is recorded. Plateau
  neighbours are recorded before they are read. The replay test reproduces the ledger point by point.
- **The surrogate on noise and on signal** (scratch probes 3 and 5):
  - Toy space, 64 readings of pure noise, 30 seeds: `noise_share` never fell below 0.89, the prediction spread never
    exceeded 0.3 of the readings', and the in-sample correlation never exceeded 0.46.
  - The real 29-dimension lever space (`slots={"ny": 2}`), 128 and 256 noise readings, 3 seeds each: share 0.90-1.00
    and spread at most 0.25.
  - With a planted structure in S3: correlation between prediction and truth 0.96-0.98, and the 95 % intervals covered
    the latent truth 0.90-0.95 of the time.
  
  The SAAS prior holds up as claimed. Its uncertainty is the latent measure's, slightly under-covering.
- **Reproducibility.** The same seed gives the same survey, ledger, fit and round (the tests). Changing the seed changes
  them.
- **Mutations caught (17 of 23).** The door's block check; the non-Reading door; per-date bounds taken as a maximum;
  the RL3 constraint; the surrogate's protocol equality; its space check; limits ignored in EI; refused plateau
  neighbours unrecorded; a weak prior (tau 10); no mean variance; a wrong believer; latencies unrecorded; the firms
  check; a wrong ledger seed; anchored starts ignored; union in place of intersection in `components.session_bounds`;
  local candidates off.

**Scratch scripts (to rerun):**
- probe 1: the lever space's bounds and dimensions;
- probe 2: the door, ledger and plateau attacks, toy and slot spaces;
- probe 3: noise on the toy space, 30 seeds;
- probe 4: the plateau's selection bias, 20 seeds a tolerance;
- probe 5: noise and signal on the lever space;
- `mutate.py`: 23 single-line mutations over the four test files.

All ran light, under 1 CPU-minute each except probe 5 (about a minute), with `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`, on a copy of
`extended/`, `config/`, `vendor/stage2/{config,propedge}` and the four test files.

## 5. What I could not check

- **How large design-level noise is in real composites,** which decides how badly B-2 bites. The experiment: on H5's
  survey (search years only), re-read 20 surveyed designs on a second disjoint coin-seed set under the same blocks, and
  estimate each design's paired-difference standard deviation against plausible plateau tolerances. Cost: 20 designs
  times the survey's seed count, one heavy run under `run.lock`.
- **Whether the Wave 5 runner builds `Reading`s only from `reader` calls.** The door trusts labels, and the runner
  does not exist yet.
- **Whether `triggers`/`components` enforce tier 1 (news-day size, the side rule) for every design the sampler
  emits.** That is Wave 3.3's code, read here only as far as `components.schedule_day`'s day check.
