## Blocker

### NB-1. The exact limit twin `x_i = s_i n_i / 2` measures supply as well as direction

**Where:** section 5.3 (lines 321-329), 7.3 (lines 474-483), 9.7 (lines 604-606) and 1.2 (lines 125-129). Limit trade
sets are eligible for T_g (8.2 and 9.4).

**What it is.** Write:

- `p` for the share of a trade set's resting-side fills on which the signal is on the resting side (`s_i = +1`);
- `m+` and `m-` for the mean `n_i` over those fills and over the others;
- `m` for the mean over all of them.

Then the declared lift is

`lhat = mean(s_i n_i) / 2 = (p m+ - (1 - p) m-) / 2 = p (m+ - m) + (p - 1/2) m`.

- The first term is the per-executed-trade lift `lamhat = m+ - m` (7.3's), scaled by the share executed.
- **The second term is supply:** how many resting fills the signal trades compared with a coin's half, times their
  mean outcome.

For a fair coin, `p` is one half in expectation, so 5.3's claim "zero in expectation for any coin side, whatever the
price dynamics" is true. But P-14's null is not a coin. It is a side with no information about the trade's outcome
(`m+ = m-`, so `lamhat = 0`). Under that null, `lhat = (p - 1/2) m`, which is not zero when:

- **`m` is not zero.** For a limit, `m` is negative by construction: the costs sit inside `n_i` and do not cancel as
  they do inside a market pair's `v_i`. The declared fill-bar rule (a stop touch on the fill bar a loss, a target touch
  not counted, 5.2) adds a negative part as well, and a drift adds more.
- **`p` is not one half.** P-14's side is fixed at the breakout bar's close, and the resting side is set by the price
  move during the latency (a buy rests only if the price rose). Any continuation or reversal after a breakout makes `p`
  differ from one half, with no information about the fill's outcome.

**A limiting case makes it plain.** A side that always disagrees with the resting side never trades. Yet its declared
lift is `-m / 2`, which is positive whenever the resting fills lose on average after costs, as E-125 reads P-14's
trades.

**What it allows:**

- **C1 can pass on supply alone.** With `p` below one half (a reversal after the breakout) and `m` negative, the
  supply term is positive and adds to any noise, at 0.05 / (k (k + 1)), with no direction in the outcomes.
- **C2 is not bounded.** 7.3's lift-L arm shifts the executed trades by `lamhat x (L / lhat - 1)`, so `V_J(L_J)` rests
  on `lamhat_J x L_J / lhat_J`. When `lhat_J` is inflated by supply, that ratio is near one, and the value line prices
  the judged point estimate of `lamhat`, not a lower bound of it. A carry can follow from `lamhat_J` noise plus a
  positive supply term.
- **K0 cannot see it.** 9.7's limit form draws fair signs on `|n_i| / 2`. That is the law under a coin, not under P-14's
  null of a non-fair `p` with `m+ = m-`.
- **T-2 cannot see it.** It tests the exact twin's zero mean for a fair coin only.

**Why the market form escapes it.** For a market pair, `x_i = s_i v_i` with `v_i = (net(long) - net(short)) / 2`. Both
sides pay the same entry tick and fees, so the costs cancel inside `v_i`. Both sides always trade, so there is no
supply term. What remains is long bias times drift (N-8), which is direction in the broad sense and is the program's
estimand.

**This was my error.** My first read's remedy (a) stated only the coin property, and the text adopted it as ruled.

**Remedy (pick one before the pin):**

- **(a) Make P-14's limit trade sets described only, not eligible for T_g** (my first read's option (b); E-203 already
  allows it for "a limit set without the exact twin").
  - Print beside each one: `lhat_A` in the exact form, its decomposition (`p`, `m+`, `m-`, `m`, and the supply term
    `(p - 1/2) m`), and the walked-mirror diagnostic.
  - The counts are unchanged, since every cell stays counted. The operator's R-4 "modelled beside" is met.
  - This is the recommended fix: it needs no new statistic.
- **(b) Test the signal's choice among the resting fills against a placebo-selection twin.** That means a random subset
  of the same share `p`, CHARTER section 3 and E-023 (2), estimand `lamhat`. It needs a calibrated two-sample or
  selection statistic, which Q-126 (one-sample) does not supply. So it belongs to a later family, not this one.

A one-sample patch is not exact either way. Centring `n_i` on an estimated mean is not exact. Using gross R instead of
net leaves the negative part from the fill-bar rule and the drift part.

Under either remedy:

- 1.2's line, which says the exact twin is the twin for limit entries, becomes "is exact for a coin; for a side-choosing
  signal it includes supply" (or is restricted to P-16).
- 7.3's `lamhat` stays only as a described quantity.

## Should-fix

### SF-1. The gatekeeper's k rule counts every pinned family; an unnumbered pin breaks it

**Where:** header lines 6-7, the table's k row, 16.1, and `.claude/agents/prereg-gatekeeper.md` lines 73-74.

The gatekeeper computes k as "10 plus the number of families already pinned in `DECISIONS.md` (a withdrawn declaration
still spends its k unless a decision row releases it)". Pinned without a number, F-04 would be counted by that formula
for the next pin. RL3 and B-ON, queued by E-202 (5), would then be numbered as though F-04 held 13. Meanwhile F-04's
own stage-B row takes "the next free number", which may be 13, if F-04 alone was counted. So a number can be claimed
twice, or skipped.

`tests/test_declarations_fixed.py` may also expect every F-file's pin row to carry k; I did not open it.

**Remedy:**

- The pinning row (or a lead's row before it) states the rule. A pinned family whose number is assigned at stage B is
  not counted until its stage-B row; families pinned meanwhile take numbers in pin order; F-04's stage-B row takes the
  next free number at the stage-B run's commit.
- The gatekeeper's text and the pin test are amended with it.
- Stage B's MDL and kills use that number's level. If another family pins between the stage-B run and the stage-B row,
  stage B is rerun at the new level under a repeat row, never relabelled.

### SF-2. Reduction-ladder rung 2 cannot be measured before step 2

**Where:** section 18 (lines 917-930).

The ladder is applied "only while the measurement still exceeds a bound ... recorded in a row before step 2 runs". But
how much rung 2 saves depends on how many trade sets have `lhat_A <= 0`, which is a choice-block outcome known only
after the walks inside step 2. So the measurement "after rung 2" cannot exist before step 2, and the rule as written
cannot decide whether rungs 3 and 4 apply.

The choice is not an outcome leak, since it is on the choice block and Kill B kills those sets anyway. It is a rule the
text cannot execute.

**Remedy:** in the pre-step-2 measurement, count rung 2 as saving nothing (the worst case), so rungs 3 and 4 are
decided by the measurement alone. Rung 2 is applied whenever the ladder is entered. Alternatively, split step 2 into
2a (the walks and the 8.1 item 1 rows, light) and 2b (the mode cells and journeys), with a row between them recording
the ladder from 2a's counts. That is still choice-block only.
