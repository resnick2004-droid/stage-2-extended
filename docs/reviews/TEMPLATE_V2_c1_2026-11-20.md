## S-1 (CHANGES-A-CONCLUSION). The per-trade lattice step: as coarse as allowed, and refused by the trade's exit

**Where.**
- T l.301-304: "the largest whole number of ticks that divides both and keeps the walk's step count n at or under the runner's limit `WALK_MAX`. If no such s exists, the trade is refused and counted apart."
- T l.335-336: n = round(sigma² T / s²), halves up, at least one.
- GK l.115-118: "a function of l and g and nothing else".
- TT l.836-851: `_step(20, 40, 400, 1024) == 20`.

**Evidence 1: coarseness** (`walk_step.py`, `walk_short.py`; exact lattice DP, zero mean in every row).

The test's own case (sigma² T = 400 tick², l = 20, g = 40):

| s | n | P(stop) | P(target) | P(time exit) | E[X²] | E[max(X, 0)] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20, the template's | 1 | 0.500 | 0.000 | 0.500 | 400 | 10.0 |
| 4 | 25 | 0.327 | 0.043 | 0.630 | 337 | 7.9 |
| 1 | 400 | 0.318 | 0.046 | 0.637 | 335 | 7.8 |

A fill two minutes before the 15:10 flatten (sigma 8 ticks per root-minute, T = 2, bracket 20/40):

| s | n | P(stop) | E[X²] |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20, the template's | max(1, round(0.32)) = 1 | 0.500 | 400 |
| 5 | 5 | 0.063 | 123 |
| 1 | 128 | 0.078 | 125 |

When sigma√T is large against the bracket, every lattice gives the race and the choice does not matter (the third case in `walk_step.py`). The walk exists precisely for trades whose T is short against their bracket: fills near the flatten, near a news window, or with a short max hold. That is exactly where the largest s collapses the walk to a coin flip of plus or minus s. Each outcome stays mean-zero, but its variance and its barrier-hit chance are wrong, sometimes by a factor of six. The "at least one" floor makes this worse: when sigma² T / s² < 1/2, the variance is inflated by s² / (sigma² T).

**Evidence 2: refusals.**
- s = 1 always divides l and g, so a refusal happens exactly when every divisor d of gcd(l, g) has sigma² T / d² > 1024 (after rounding).
- For a coprime bracket (l = 37, g = 55, the kind volatility-unit stops give), that is any sigma√T above 32 ticks: about 8 NQ points, for example sigma 8 ticks over T = 16 minutes.
- **Only a walked trade needs s, and the law is read "from the run's terminal state"** (T l.305). So whether a trade is refused depends on whether its realised path ended at a time limit.
- For the table's last row, T itself is "the run's exit time" (T l.317), not known at the fill.

**The failure it allows.**
- **The inputs:** any search with time-limited trades, under this template, on NQ.
- **Wrong output (a):** the walked trades' outcome distribution varies with the bracket's gcd and with T's shortness. Through the contract's convexity (T l.342-345), that shifts P_B, ruin and pass speed. The search will find "bracket shapes with a large gcd" and "late entries" as levers, and neither is a property of the market.
- **Wrong output (b), if "refused" means the trade is dropped:**
  - It brings back a not-taken state, decided by the trade's later exit. Two trades identical at the fill fare differently (against T l.264-268, N-1).
  - Every dropped trade's costs vanish. At zero edge a trade's net is minus its costs, so dropping some trades is a positive bias for designs whose time exits fall on coprime brackets.
- **Wrong output (b'), if "refused" means the run is refused:** M-3's original failure stands, now for every coprime bracket. The run is refused, or a drafter restricts the bracket grid after seeing which brackets run.

**Remedy (a rule changes).**
- s is **the smallest admissible whole-tick step**, s = max(1, ceil(sigma √T / √`WALK_MAX`)), with sigma and T as the template already fixes them at the alert and the fill.
- **Drop "divides both".** The template already resolves fractional barriers by the two-point sub-step (T l.322-327). L_race and g' are fractional anyway, so divisibility buys nothing.
- Then nothing is refused. GK's "nothing else" becomes "of sigma, T and `WALK_MAX`".
- Correct TT l.850-852's assertions, and add an exact test that the walk's P(stop) at n = 1024 is within a stated tolerance of the one-tick lattice on a small grid. The probe above is a template for that test.
- For the last table row, where T is the realised exit, either take T as the limit known at the fill (the fill-time law's T) or state it as a declared deviation with its share printed. Never let it decide a refusal.
- The runner's `walk_step_for` (staged on wt-three) is held to the same rule.

## H-1 (MATERIAL). A firm's state in the joint test is undefined

**Where.**
- HT l.50-52: "a firm whose search closes without finalists ... listed as none, with its closing row (a failed power gate, every design failing a gate, every finalist failing its no-harm floor)".
- HT l.56: "Only when every firm's search has a finalists' row or a closing row".
- T l.73-76 ("each have finalists") and l.607.
- GK l.179-180.

**Evidence.**
- **"Closing row" has no form.** There is no token, unlike `heldout-finalists`. Its causes are given in parentheses as examples. A failed calibration (T l.451-452), a lead's withdrawal, a bug found in the runner, or a finalist order rule that yields an empty list are not named.
- **One row per firm** (HT l.28-32), and "within a firm, the search's own pinned finalist order" is singular. Three cases are unresolved:
  - a firm with a closed search and a successor in progress, which T l.452-454 allows (wait for the successor, or proceed with "none"?);
  - a firm with two searches that both have finalists (for example, two account sizes);
  - a firm whose search is never declared (MFFU's or Lucid's protocol not built) or never reaches a row.
- **Nothing in the reader checks any of this.** Rule 1 sees only F-HT's own token (`reader.py` `_finalists`).

**The failure it allows.**
- Topstep's search-year finalist looks weak, and the lead knows from Stage 2's 2024-2026 reads how that family fared late.
- A judgement-based "closing" of Topstep's search, or a decision to proceed before MFFU's successor search finishes, or to wait for it, re-orders who is tested first.
- That is the selection channel M-1 named, moved from "the order" to "who is in it".
- Alternatively, the test waits forever.

**Remedy.**
- **A closing token in the reader's positional form** (for example `heldout-closed: F-NN @ <sha> -- <cause>`), its causes a closed list fixed now:
  - the calibration fails;
  - the power gate fails;
  - every surveyed design fails a gate;
  - the finalist order rule yields an empty list;
  - every finalist fails its no-harm floor;
  - a withdrawal under E-005 (d).
- **A firm's entry is its newest pinned search.** A successor may be pinned only before a fixed event, fixed now (for example, before the first firm's finalists' row). After that event, a closed firm is "none" and is not waited for.
- **A firm with no pinned search** at that event is "none".
- **Two searches of one firm:** refused, or ordered by a rule fixed now (for example, by pin row).
- **A test**, given synthetic rows, that the HT drafter's table matches these rules.
- **Align T l.75** to "a finalists' row or a closing row", citing E-254 as the reading of CHARTER 3.6 (c) and 3.7's "held until each firm's search has finalists".

## H-2 (MATERIAL, power). The firm-block order starves the later firms, and only now can it be chosen

**Where.** HT l.45-48 (the order), l.80-89 (power at place, with no formula).

**Evidence (toy arithmetic only, not a result).**
- Each finalist has power p at the size that matters, and each firm brings m finalists. Under independence, place i's power is the product of the earlier powers and its own.
- With p = 0.8 and m = 3 per firm:
  - firm-block order: Topstep's first 0.8, MFFU's first 0.8⁴ ≈ 0.41, Lucid's first 0.8⁷ ≈ 0.21;
  - round-robin by rank (each firm's first, then each firm's second, and so on): 0.8, 0.64, 0.51.
- The Fréchet lower bound, which needs no dependence model, is max(0, Σ p - (i - 1)): 0 for Lucid's first under firm-block order.
- Positive dependence (the same held-out history) raises the true value, but its size is unknown.
- **No search can know its place power before its go row**, because the earlier firms' list lengths are unknown then. So each firm's own gate overstates the power its finalist actually has.

**The failure it allows.** The program's stated aim is each firm's own journey (the per-firm protocol). Under the block order, a good MFFU or Lucid design most likely goes untested because a Topstep finalist failed before it. Exit (B) then reports "the line's power" from a gate that does not describe the test it ran.

**Remedy (the lead's, before any search pins).**
- Choose between block order with a per-firm cap on finalists, and round-robin by rank. Either is fixed before any look.
- Declare now the place-power formula. For example, print both the independence product and the Fréchet lower bound, neither deciding.
- Ask each search's power gate to print its first finalist's place power under the chosen rule, at a declared assumed list length.
