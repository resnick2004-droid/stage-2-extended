## Blockers

### B-1. The major-news stand-aside decides on information that does not exist when the trade is entered (look-ahead)

*Where:* 4.7 item 3, lines 457-463. It is cited by 2.6 line 241, 10.3 line 854, 12.4 line 913 and 13.1 line 980.

*Evidence:* "a trade whose position or resting order **would be open** inside the window the record states, around a
release of a pinned calendar ... **stands aside**". The rule reaches `RI`'s and `PRO`'s funded accounts, through
`mffu.news_tier1_rule`, whose list includes FOMC minutes, with a window of two minutes either side.

Take a P-16 trade entered at 10:00 CT on a minutes day. Whether it is still open at the window's start depends on its
exit, which lies in bars after the alert. The rule as written therefore keeps the trades that exit early and drops
those that last. A trade's duration is correlated with its outcome (quick stops against slow grinds). This is a
quantity at alert time computed from bars that had not closed. The merge checklist forbids it ("no feature reads a bar
after its alert").

RD-01 handled the same firm rule at TradeDay as a count, plus a bracket (its G-3), and never as a stand-aside.

*Failure allowed:* on the 53 minutes days of `config/calendar_fomc_minutes.csv`, part F's `RI` and `PRO` rows (and
`RE` and `RED`'s, if the rule reaches them, S-2) drop trades on future information. The tier-1 count of 13.1 then
reads zero by construction, not by compliance.

*Fix:* replace it with a rule that can be applied at the moment of entry. Either:

- (a) on a pinned release day, at an account the rule reaches, no entry between the session's open and the window's
  end; or
- (b) a forced flat at the window's start, priced at the open of the window's first bar (bars are stamped at their
  open, so this is the last bar closed before the window). RD-01 G-3's bracket stands beside it, and the forced exits
  are counted.

Either way, the decision at entry uses only the clock and the calendar.

### B-2. At Builder's lenient corners, the pass ceiling is not a bound, so a correct engine breaches it

*Where:* 5.1 U-27, lines 548-550; Appendix M rows 14 and 16; 9.6 lines 799-802.

*Evidence:* U-27's lenient end is "a check at the day's end only (on the coin: the trade runs to its stop or target, and
the account fails if its close is at or below the floor)". The account can then close *below* the floor. The bound
`p <= D/(D+T)` comes from optional stopping, and its proof needs the balance at failure to be at least `-D`. Overshoot
below the floor breaks that, while the martingale itself still holds. The draft's sentence "trailing floors, locks
above start, ... only lower it" (line 801) does not cover this case.

I ran a small synthetic check: a zero-edge binary coin, an end-of-day breach only, an end-of-day trailing floor locked
just above start, no consistency rule, and no daily limit (U-28's lenient end, the same corner). Several `(w, r)` cells
on the declared grid came out well above `D/(D+T)`, beyond four binomial standard errors at 400,000 paths. The mean
balance stayed at zero within noise.

*Failure allowed:* step 2 stops on a correct engine at evaluation mode-corners 14 and 16. This is the same failure the
first text had (old B3), now confined to two corners.

*Fix:* at a corner where the floor is checked at the close only, drop the pass-ceiling check, or replace it with the
bound `p <= E|fail balance| / (T + E|fail balance|)`, computed on the cell's own failures. Keep the martingale check
there. List these corners in step 0's `boundary_cells`-style output, so that N counts them correctly.

## Should-fix

### S-1. Three flaws in the oracle test design (9.6, lines 791-820; T-9 line 1110)

- **(a) T-9's power assertion fails one time in seven.** "A battery with one check shifted by six of its standard
  errors breaches." With N = 10,681 and a family-wise 0.01, the per-check cut is about 4.8 to 4.9 standard errors. A
  shift of 6 SE then breaches with probability of only about 0.86 to 0.89 (arithmetic of the declared design). So a
  correct build fails this test on roughly one seed in seven, and the only way through would be to change the seed or
  the test after seeing it fail.
  *Fix:* shift by at least 8 SE (power above 0.999), or assert the breach share over many batteries against the
  computed power.
- **(b) The boundary-cell equality omits expired accounts.** Its quantity is `T 1{pass} - D 1{fail} + B 1{unresolved}`.
  But 9.3 counts "expired" and "unresolved at 504" separately. The two boundary plans, `L6` and `L11`, both carry a
  verified 90-day lifespan (`labs.*.eval_lifespan_days`), and small-`r` boundary cells such as `steady (1.0, $250)`
  expire often. If "unresolved" excludes expired accounts, the quantity's mean is not zero on a correct engine. 7
  item 8's list of boundary conditions also omits "no lifespan binds", so "attained exactly" is false at those cells.
  *Fix:* define the quantity's third term over every account neither passed nor failed (expired included), and add the
  lifespan to 7 item 8.
- **(c) The false-alarm batteries do not test the skewed case.** "A mean by the normal interval" at a tail of about
  1e-6 assumes the t-statistic's far tail is close to normal. The funded quantity (balance plus payouts) is strongly
  skewed. An Edgeworth estimate puts the one-sided tail at a few times its nominal value for skewness of order ten at
  n = 50,000. The batteries of T-9 draw "mean-zero quantities", which may be symmetric, so they cannot reveal this.
  *Fix:* draw part of each battery from skewed mean-zero laws shaped like the funded quantity, or use a bootstrap-t
  cut. Otherwise the family-wise 0.01 is a nominal figure on the funded checks.

**On power, as the brief asks:** the width is adequate for what these oracles are for. A wrong barrier race, a payout
double-count, or a biased win probability moves a cell's mean balance by many standard errors, and a systematic fault
repeats over hundreds of cells. At about 6.5 SE per check, the pass-probability check sees shifts of roughly a
percentage point. The martingale check sees per-trade win-probability errors of under a percentage point on cells with
a handful of trades. Rule-semantics faults are not visible to any oracle at any width; that remains T-3's job, as
before.

### S-3. The U-rules need figures that live only in the quotes of unverified entries

### S-4. Censored attempts are treated two different ways between the means and the renewal

*Where:* 9.4 line 758; 9.5 lines 770-779.

- **In the renewal**, "the number of failed attempts before the first pass is geometric with the cell's non-pass
  probability". Yet attempts unresolved at 504 "enter as censored at the horizon, never as a duration". With three
  outcomes per attempt (pass, fail with a duration, censored), the law is "k fails, then a pass or a censored end",
  each fail with probability `q_fail`, not `1 - p_pass`.
- **In the means**, "fees per pass = fees per attempt over the pass probability" (Wald) implicitly treats a censored
  attempt as a failure followed by a fresh purchase.

*Failure:* at cells with visible unresolved mass (small `r` at `TS`, which has no lifespan), the renewal law and the
Wald means describe different users.

*Fix:* choose one convention and state it in both places. The simplest is to treat a censored attempt as a failure of
duration 504, followed by a new purchase, and label it.

### S-5. The memory bound and the worker count can contradict each other at step 3

*Where:* 18, lines 1157-1172.

The bound is 2.5 GB with "at most two workers". The measure cited is E-010's peak for U1's identity (about 1.4 GB),
and RD-02 and RD-03 read that figure per worker. Two such workers exceed 2.5 GB, so step 3 would "not be started".
The text forbids adding a worker at run time, but it does not say whether running one worker instead of two is
allowed. Nor does it say whether the bound applies per process or summed over the job's processes.

*Fix:* state that the bound is the job's summed working set, and that the step may run with one worker. That is not a
design change, since the replays are deterministic. Check the 24-hour bound for step 3 against one worker.

### S-6. The coin's stacking exposure needs an exact definition

*Where:* 4.7 item 5, lines 472-474.

"On the coin, from 9.5's renewal arithmetic (the chance that a journey meets it before its first payout)." D-145's
trigger is two floors within the 21 sessions before a payout. The renewal as declared is a law of total time, not of
the times of failures within a trailing window.

*Fix:* define the quantity: the probability that at least two failed attempts end within the 21 sessions before the
first payout's session. Say that it is computed by the same convolution, with a window state, and test it in T-9
against a direct simulation, as the renewal is.
