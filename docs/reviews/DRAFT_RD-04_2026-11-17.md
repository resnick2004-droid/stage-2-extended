## Verdict: pin after the listed changes

The red team's three words would be **must be re-declared**: five defects change declared rules (M1 to M5). Nothing is
pinned yet, though, so every remedy is a text change before the pin, plus one small change to Q-121's module on its
own branch (M4). The draft's design holds: the map, the two factors, the frozen inputs, the k = 1 identity, the
counts, the once rules and the seed block. No redraft is needed.

The draft is careful and mostly right. I found no look-ahead in any quantity that decides, routes, sizes or offers
anything. The early block of (c) carries no judged-block bar through either factor.

The defects are these:
- the cost reading `sc` cannot do what it says, because the engine re-prices the fee;
- the zero-edge twin is left undefined under a frozen e;
- (c)'s once rule can be bypassed outside a clean run;
- the (a2) factor's population and statistic are underspecified, and Q-121's refusal must be parameterised;
- (a2)'s by-mark judged rows foretell a judged-block floor, and the leak clause binds nothing mechanically.

### MATERIAL

**M1. `sc` cannot scale the round-turn fee: the engine re-prices it at size and undoes the scaling. If the build
notices, the draft's no-fallback clause stops all of (c), the headline included.**
- *Where.* Draft 4.3 (lines 242-244); Appendix C item 8 (line 858); 8.1 item 6 (lines 480-481); T-4 (line 720); 4.6
  (lines 314-317).
- *Evidence.*
  - P-14 and P-16 run FV3-C's engine under `config/fv3_corrected_portfolio.yaml:151`, `costs: per_trade_at_size`
    (Stage 2 D-324).
  - The trade record carries the fee "a micro-equivalent priced in" (`score_fv3_grid.py:1214`, `fee_micro_eq`).
  - The engine's sizing replaces that fee with the fill model's round turn at the contracts held:
    `adj = (fees[j] * contracts - fee_dollars(contracts)) / realised_risk` (`propedge/campaign/sizing.py:795-801`,
    `self._fee = log["fee"]` at line 449; `fee_schedule` built at `score_fv3_grid.py:1249-1257`).
  - So if the re-composed `cell_trades` multiplies `fee_micro_eq` and the cost column by k, the engine adds back
    k x fee x n and charges the full fee at size. The fee scaling is silently undone, and the row still says
    "costs scaled by the factor".
  - If the builder instead scales only the cost column, the add-back over-credits by (1 - k) x fee x n.
  - The only real route is the engine's `fee_schedule`. That is one scalar pair per campaign, so it cannot carry a
    year or session factor.
  - 4.6 then says the build "stops and reports; this declaration does not fall back". That stops (c) whole, including
    `yr_act`, which needs no cost scaling at all.
- *Why the identity and tests miss it.* 8.1 item 6 compares trades only, at k = 1, where x * 1.0 == x. T-4 tests the
  trade record on toys and never runs a campaign at k < 1.
- *What goes wrong.* Either `yr_sc` and `tr_sc` are printed as "all per-trade costs scaled" while carrying the full
  fee, or the build halts the headline for a beside arm's plumbing.
- *Remedy (text).*
  1. Define `sc` as the **tick costs only**: the entry tick, the tick on a stop or breakeven, and the two flatten ticks,
     each multiplied by k. The round-turn fee stays as the engine charges it at size in both readings, and
     `fee_micro_eq` is never altered. This is Appendix C item 8's own reasoning carried one step: the fee is dollars per
     contract, the contract's, held fixed like the subscriptions. It is also exactly pitfall 1's object, the tick grid.
     Amend 4.3, item 8, the 9.1 item 8 label ("tick costs scaled by the factor (D-122)") and 13.1.
  2. Add to T-4 a toy campaign at k < 1 through the engine, asserting that the fee charged equals
     `SizingRule.fee_dollars(contracts)` under both readings and the tick costs differ by exactly (1 - k) of their sum.
  3. Re-scope 4.6's stop: a piece of a beside cell that cannot be built stops that cell, named and recorded, and never
     `k1` or `yr_act`. That is a declared failure scope, not a fallback.

**M2. The zero-edge twin of a new cell is undefined under the frozen e. Read literally, it is not zero-edge, and 8.3's
twin oracle then tests the wrong thing.**
- *Where.* 4.4's shift row (line 259); 6.2 (line 381, "with its zero-edge twin"); 8.3 twin oracle (lines 515-516);
  9.1 item 7 (lines 572-575).
- *Evidence.* The gate's lower-bound arm shifts each R by `lb - e` (`gate.py`, `shifted_blocks(blocks, lb - e,
  delta_by_rr)`; `customer_slices.arm_blocks:182-195`), so freezing `lb - e` at `k1` is implementable through the
  arguments. The twin, though, is `portfolio_stage.zero_edge_blocks(blocks, e_by, fr)`, which shifts by `-e_by`
  (`portfolio_stage.py:151-153`, called from `atlas_a3.campaign:455-461` with the same `e_by` the gate gets).
- *What goes wrong.* With `k1`'s e passed in, the twin of a rescaled cell has a mean of e_cell - e_k1 per trade,
  not zero. The compression changes the mix of flattens, which pay two ticks, against stops and targets, so e_cell
  differs from e_k1. The twin oracle (propsim D-02: payouts per funded account at most $2,000 and a pass rate at most
  2/5, each within its interval) can then fire on a correct engine and be misread as "an engine or adapter error". Or
  it passes for a twin that is not one.
- *Remedy (text).*
  1. The twin of every new cell is shifted by the cell's **own** e per R, computed on its rescaled trades by the
     registered rule. This is the only use of the cell's own e, and it is stated in 4.4 beside the frozen shift.
  2. The gate's `e`, `lb`, `e_by_rr` and `lb_by_rr` for the point and bound arms are `k1`'s.
  3. Add an oracle: the twin's realised mean R per trade is zero to 1e-12 on every new cell.

### MINOR

**m1. T-2's shift-invariance test is false as written.**
- *Where.* T-2 (line 718); 4.2 (lines 226-228).
- *Evidence.* k_d = min(1, L_ref / L_d), and L_ref is the median over 2020-2021. Changing every session from a day d
  inside 2020-2021 moves L_ref, and so the factors **before** d. A synthetic check with d inside the reference span
  changed 122 of 300 earlier factors.
- *What goes wrong.* A correct build fails T-2, or T-2 gets weakened after the pin.
- *Remedy.* T-2 holds L_ref fixed, passed as an input, and asserts invariance of L_d and of k_d given L_ref. 4.2 says
  "causal" means L_d is prior-only, with L_ref a declared constant over the reference span.

**m2. The year factor is not causal, and a reference year is compressed. Both should be said, not left to
inference.**
- *Where.* 4.2 (lines 220-225); pitfall 2 (lines 270-272); pitfall 9 (lines 294-296).
- *Evidence.* k_y uses the whole year's sessions, so a January session's path depends on December's trailing ranges.
  **This is acceptable here:** no quantity in (c) that offers, sides, routes, sizes or settles a trade reads k. Offers,
  sides, rungs, marks, the state table, the shift and the capacity are all frozen from the actual-bar build (4.4), so
  k shapes the replayed path and decides nothing.
- *Second point.* L_ref is the pooled median of 2020 and 2021. Unless the two years' medians tie, one of them has
  L_y > L_ref and is compressed itself (shown on synthetic data). Pitfall 9 implies the reference years are unchanged.
- *Remedy.* Label `yr` rows "the year factor uses the year's own later sessions: a counterfactual's scale, not a rule
  a user could follow". Pitfall 9 states that the reference year with the higher median is scaled to the pooled level.

**m3. The label "the tick grid's bounce not removed" is wrong in direction.**
- *Where.* 4.3 (lines 246-247); pitfall 1 (lines 268-269); 9.1 item 8.
- *Evidence.* The map rounds each price's offset from O_d. A real one-tick move becomes 0 or 1 tick, about k ticks on
  average: on 2,000 synthetic paths, the mean size of former one-tick moves tracked k (correlation 0.98). The replay
  therefore **compresses** the bounce with the path. A real calm market at the same contract keeps a one-tick bounce,
  and neither reading restores it.
- *Remedy.* Relabel: "the tick-scale bounce is compressed with the path; a real calm market keeps it; neither reading
  restores it".

**m4. The Focused Trader scope rests on an inconsistent cost argument.**
- *Where.* Appendix C items 9 and 10 (lines 859-860); 6.4 item 4.
- *Evidence.* Item 10 calls the ladder and placement replays "cheap beside the walks". Item 9 calls the same kind of

- *Remedy.* Run the ten binding models on `tr_act` too (20 more replays, beside), or state a measured cost.
