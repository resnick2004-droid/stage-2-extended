## Findings

### B-1 (CHANGES-A-CONCLUSION). The no-harm verdict is not bound to the side rule, the trigger or the declaration it was read on

**Where:**
- `extended/playbook.py:703`: a result file is matched on `parameters.design_id` and `parameters.convention` only.
- `:794-800`: the floor is the cell `no_harm|search|<component name>|verdict`, which must read `pass`.
- `extended/trigger_sides.py:37`: P-14 has two rules, `with_breakout` and `against_breakout`.
- The no-harm file's format is the builder's own (interpretation 2). It records no side rule, no trigger parameter, no
  margin and no producing script.

**Evidence** (`scratchpad\w35v2\probe_a.py`, `probe_b.py`). Each of these is ACCEPTED:
- **P3.** pm_brk's rule is flipped from `against_breakout` to `with_breakout`, with a new carrying-row pin for the
  declaration and the same no-harm file. The page prints the flipped rule under "Side (the trigger's own, cleared by
  the no-harm floor under E-9002)", and every alert of pm_brk prints "The trigger's own side, cleared by the no-harm
  floor".
- **P4b.** P-14 with `k: 7`. `triggers.P14Breakout` never checks `k`, and the playbook prints "k: 7" and alerts on
  `brk7+`. The floor was read, if at all, on a different trigger.
- **P17.** A no-harm file written by `scratch/anything.py`, with `prereg_id: null`, is accepted. Its `pass` cells
  are believed as written. The 3.9 margin and the composites behind the word are never seen.

**What it allows:**
- **A side that failed the floor, or was never read against it, reaches a playbook.** The no-harm row and file can
  belong to an earlier version of the design: same id, same component names, other rule. The lead pins rows, but the
  module exists to refuse exactly this mis-pairing.
- **Selection after the fact (interpretation 4).** Two P-14 rules exist, and nothing records which was fixed before
  the floor was read. Reading both and declaring the one that passes spends the floor twice on the search years, and
  no row counts it.

**Remedy:**
1. The no-harm manifest's `parameters` carry the sha256 of the pinned declaration it was run on. Alternatively, per
   component: the side rule, the trigger's source and params, the entry, the stop and R. The playbook refuses on any
   mismatch with the loaded declaration.
2. The file holds, per component:
   - the trigger side's composite;
   - the coin side's composite;
   - the margin, with its 3.9 row.

   The playbook recomputes `pass`, rather than reading a word.
3. The manifest's `script` and `prereg_id` must be the declared no-harm runner's.
4. Each P-14 component's rule is fixed under a row before its floor is read, or both rules are counted in the
   floor's multiplicity.
5. Hold P-14's `k` to the registered `P14_K` (or to declared cells).
6. Tests: a flipped rule refuses, another `k` refuses, a foreign script refuses.

### B-2 (MATERIAL). The side is computed from an offer the caller builds: its tag and role decide the side

**Where:**
- `extended/trigger_sides.py:120-140`: the side is the sign of `offer.tag` (P-14), or `offer.role` with the level's
  rule (RL3).
- `extended/playbook.py:978-1012` (`_check_offer`) checks the offer's fields for consistency with the trigger's
  constructor (decision, alert, live, entry, expiry, sub, group). It cannot check that the trigger made this offer on
  this day's bars. `T.Offer` is a plain dataclass.

**Evidence** (probe_a):
- **P1.** The evaluation's coin drew BUY for pm_brk (`against_breakout`). An offer built with `tag="brk30-"` is
  ACCEPTED. The alert prints "Side BUY (long): the breakout was a close below the previous completed reference bar's
  low". That is the coin's side, attributed to the trigger, with an invented account of the market.
- **P2.** An RL3 `pd_hi` ticket with `role=-1` is ACCEPTED as "a support", BUY limit. The role comes from the bars
  (level against the 08:29 close), and here it is the caller's word.

`TriggerSide` is typed, but its input is not. The lead's R-1 ruling ("anything the coin decided offers no side") holds
only for a caller who passes `trigger.offers(...)` unchanged. An adapter that rebuilds offers from a schedule and sets
the tag from the side would reproduce cycle 0's R-1 one level up.

**Remedy:**
- `render_offer` takes the day's bars (a `T.History` whose bars stamped at or after the alert are NaN) and the day
  index. It calls `trig.offers(hist, i, ctx)` itself and renders only an offer equal to one of them.
- Add a look-ahead test: scrambling every bar stamped at or after the alert changes nothing in the alert.
- Alternatively, mint offers in `triggers` as `TriggerSide` is minted.

All of this is synthetic in the tests (a hand-built `History`).

### B-4 (MATERIAL). RL3's one-cancels-other pair is alerted ticket by ticket, to any account of the pool, and the alert's own check forbids the second ticket

**Where:**
- `extended/playbook.py:1033-1140`: one alert per ticket, and the account is the caller's.
- `:1132`: "enter only if, at this firm, no position is open, no entry order is working".
- The goldens send one day's `pd` pair to two accounts (`tests/test_playbook.py:169-170`: `pd_hi` to ts_c3, `pd_lo`
  to ts_a1).
- The evaluation places the group as one (`components.py:23-30`: "one group at a time ... the earliest fill is the
  position; the others are cancelled at that fill").

**Evidence** (`probe_c.py`). With `{"hi": "break", "lo": "break"}`, both of these are ACCEPTED for 2021-03-16:
- "STOP 1 tick above the level", BUY, in ts_a1;
- "STOP 1 tick below the level", SELL, in ts_c3.

Neither alert names its partner or says one-cancels-other.

**What it allows:**
- **The user follows the check.** They place the first ticket, then the second alert's check ("no entry order is
  working") tells them to skip it. They trade one ticket, which is not the evaluated design.
- **The user places both.** Across two accounts no platform OCO exists. Both can fill:
  - two positions at one firm, against the one-position rule;
  - with break/break, a long in one account and a short in the other, which is conduct tier 1.

**Remedy:**
- An RL3 group is one alert: both tickets, one account, stated as one OCO pair, each with its bracket, and "when one
  fills, cancel the other at once".
- `render_offer` refuses a single ticket of a group, and refuses a group split across accounts.
- The pre-entry check counts the group as one order.
- Add a golden for the pair.
- Whether TopstepX can hold two bracketed entries as an OCO is a platform fact to verify before it is printed (see
  "could not check").

### B-5 (MATERIAL). The alert's "offered today" uses facts known only after the alert, and no alert can be rendered for a live day

**Where:**
- `extended/playbook.py:1021-1031` calls `triggers.context_for`, which calls `day_calendar.day_flags`.
- `day_calendar.py:228`: the day is void when its **last** bar starts before the walk's last stamp.
- `:257`: H2's `missing_after_reopen`, the overnight to 08:29, for any overnight reader.
- The early-end and data-defect lists.
- `:209-213`: every date after 2023-12-29 is refused.
- The builder's own case `short-last-bar` (`tests/test_playbook.py:627`) asserts that a 14:00 last bar voids an
  11:21 alert.
- The playbook prints these rules to the user as void days (`VOID_WORDS`). Interpretation 7 forces all five.

**Evidence:**
- **P10.** A London alert at 03:10 CT becomes "NOT OFFERED ... the void calendar voids today" because the day's last
  bar is at 14:00, eleven hours later.
- **P11.** An alert for 2026-11-20 refuses: "lies in NQ's confirmation window". So does any day after the search
  years.

**What it allows:**
- **Every alert the module can produce is historical.** Its offered or not-offered decision is conditioned on later
  bars.
- **The page asks for what the user cannot see.** It tells the user not to trade on "an early end of the day's bars,
  or a data defect" and on "too many minutes missing". A user at the alert cannot see either, and the live market has
  no such defects.

The deliverable is "the exact alert format, so that it can be traded by hand" (CHARTER 3.5). Its day rule is not
executable by hand.

**Remedy:**
1. Split the day context:
   - **known before the alert:** FOMC, BLS, exchange holidays and scheduled closes, rolls, the previous day's state;
   - **evaluation-only data voids:** the day's last bar, early ends, data defects, the same day's H2 counts.

   The alert uses only the first.
2. The playbook lists the data voids as the evaluation's convention, not the user's, with their effect reported
   beside the expected journey.
3. Add a pinned forward calendar (FOMC, BLS, holidays, the release record), so that a live day can be rendered. No NQ
   bar is needed for it, so the confirmation window stays unread.

### B-6 (MATERIAL). A date that passes the ISO check never matches, so a declared news window or void day silently does nothing

**Where:**
- `extended/playbook.py:451-456` (`_iso`: `date.fromisoformat`, which in Python 3.12 accepts the basic form
  `20210317`);
- matched as strings at `:1015-1018` (`day in w["dates"]`) and `:1046` (`v["date"] == day`).

**Evidence** (`probe_d.py`). A declaration with the afternoon release on `"20210317"` and the void date `"20210319"`
loads. The page prints "on 20210317" and "20210319: ... no trade that day". Then:
- an order going live at 12:01 CT on 2021-03-17, inside that release's 11:57-12:06 window, is ACCEPTED as a TRADE
  ALERT;
- an alert on 2021-03-19 is ACCEPTED as well.

**What it allows:** an entry into a declared release window, which breaks conduct tier 1's "no maximum size into major
news". It comes from a declaration that passes every check, and the page shows the user the window the alert ignores.
My mutant X37 (the release-date check removed) also survives, so no test holds this guard.

**Remedy:**
- Require `date.fromisoformat(v).isoformat() == v` for every date in the declaration, or compare dates as dates.
- Add a test with a basic-form date, and one with a padded date.
