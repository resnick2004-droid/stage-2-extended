## (2) Did any change add a defect? What I checked, and what I found

**The hand check of derived seeds (4.1)** is right on every expression it names (J).
- `pe.SEED + 100,000 i + 1,000 j`: the base is 202 modulo 1,000, while the blocks' seeds lie at 110-129 modulo 1,000.
  So no multiple of 1,000 lands it in either block.
- `fh.SEED + 1, + 2` and `sc.SEED + 1` sit far below.
- `R.F.LADDER_SEED + i` runs over a short literal sequence from a base far below.
- **One nested offset is not mentioned:** `planted_effect.power_cell` passes `seed + 1 + i` on, for i < 200
  replications, on top of the `pe.SEED + ...` above. Its residues modulo 1,000 run 203 to 402, so it does not land
  either.
  - This repository's only runtime-argument offset, `lift_bound`'s `seed + 1000 G + lags`, cannot land for any
    integer G.
  - So the text's "none lands in either block" holds. Only the nested case should be added to the hand check's record
    when the allow-list is written. That is a record, not a rule.

**E >= 4's gloss.** 4.4's heading "E >= 4: no run holds more than half the non-tie sessions" reads as an equivalence.
The body states the true direction: E >= 4 implies s <= 1/2. E >= 4 also refuses series concentrated in two or three
long runs, for example two runs of 45 % each. Those are dominated too, and the condition decided is E >= 4 itself.
This is a reading, not a defect.

**Does the subset lottery reach the Monte Carlo persistent models (4.2, "That is condition 3's job, on the exact
part")?** The text asserts that the lottery bites on the exact part. On check I's synthetic population it holds.
- **At mean dwell 50**, `cons` with no margin had a worst per-subset ratio of about 0.29 of nominal over 24 subsets,
  both in scope Q and as the procedure. m = 1.25 sat at 0.02 or below.
- **At mean dwell 80** the lottery does reach the longest Monte Carlo model.
  - `cons` with no margin, in scope Q, was above nominal on 4 of 24 subsets, the worst at about 1.8 times, with its
    interval wholly above on one subset. But `cons` is not eligible in scope Q.
  - As scope D's procedure, the same subsets sat at 0.10 or below.
  - With m = 1.25 the worst was 0.05 in scope Q and 0.02 in scope D.
- On check G's population with the same drift, flips at 55 and 63 had `cons` at up to about twelve times nominal, on
  94 of 230 subset-models.
  - There, m = 1.25 still had bad subsets.
  - m = 1.5 reached 0.63 of nominal, and only m = 1.75 met a quarter.
- So the Monte Carlo persistent models' tails are far less lumpy than the exact flips'. A switch-timing side has many
  configurations, while a flip has a dozen sign units.
  - Every margin of 1.25 or more sat at 0.05 or below on the Markov models.
  - So the candidate that condition 3 would admit at this drift, m = 1.75, would sit far inside nominal there.
- So on this evidence the text's placement is right. P_MC's blindness to the lottery on the Monte Carlo persistent
  models costs little once condition 3 holds.
- This is synthetic: it supports the text's assertion, it does not prove it for P-16.

**The tie-dropped series and the procedure's nulls are consistent.**
- The flip, alternating and Markov nulls have no ties.
- The coin null's ties are dropped.
- The synthetic nulls are always admitted.
- The test list covers the constructed cases.

**Condition 3 is redundant with the exact floor** (a ratio of at most 1/4 implies at most 1). That is harmless.

**Nothing in the diff touches a file outside `docs/calibration/`.**

## (3) Complete enough to build from, and pinnable as is?

**Yes.** Every index, order, convention, generator, sample and consequence a builder needs is fixed in the text. I
found the two exceptions below, and neither changes a declared rule. Both are for the pinning row, as readings.

**R1. A scalar re-decision disagreement.**
- The text says the scalar forms re-decide rejections "as in Q-82" on Monte Carlo nulls, and on a declared sample on
  exact nulls (2.1, 4.1).
- It does not say what a disagreement does to the choice or to the criterion. In Q-82 a disagreement printed DIFFERS
  and was counted, and the verdict was left to the criterion.
- **Reading for the pinning row:** a disagreement in either run is a failed check under the failure protocol. The
  choosing row is not written, nor the validation's verdict accepted, until it is diagnosed. The lead's own choosing
  row is the gate the text already gives (6.3), so this is a reading of the lead's procedure, not a change to a rule.

**R2. Condition 3 on the fixed-stream exact nulls.**
- There are twenty such nulls: `offered` and `taken`, at both values, on the five exact models.
- Their exact sizes are deterministic and identical in the validation, so the quarter guards nothing there. It is as
  ruled ("every exactly enumerated calibration null").
- If it binds, its cost appears in the refused-candidate report. No change is needed: the reading records that the
  report is where that cost is seen.

**Code-level details.** The order of draws inside a generator (a Markov side's first sides and switches, a thinning)
is the code's, fixed at the build's commit. It cannot see an outcome, so it is not a choice in the sense the brief
means.

**Nothing else is open.**
- The first read's MATERIAL 1-5 and minors stand answered, as checked in the re-read.
- The re-read's MATERIAL 1-4 and minors are answered here.
- Section 9 records every change and every removal.
