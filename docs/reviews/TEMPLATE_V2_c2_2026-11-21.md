## H-1' (CHANGES-A-CONCLUSION). State (c) is terminal from the start: the firms tested are decided by pin timing

**Where.**
- HT l.70-73: "(a) finalists ... (b) none: its latest pinned search has a closing row; (c) none: no search of the firm
  was ever pinned."
- HT l.83-84: "This declaration is written as soon as every firm is in a terminal state. After the third terminal
  state, no new search may be pinned until this declaration's held-out row is written."
- Copies: T l.77-79 (section 2), T l.623 (step 8), GK l.83, GK l.191-194.

**Evidence.**
- **The test's own helper agrees** (`state_probe.py`, TT l.1099-1126):
  - with no events at all, every firm is `c`, so "all terminal" is True today;
  - after `pin Topstep F-13` and `finalists Topstep [t1, t2, t3]`, the states are `{Topstep: a, MFFU: c, Lucid: c}`.
    Every firm is terminal, so the held-out test is due, and its order is Topstep's three finalists only;
  - with MFFU pinned one row earlier, MFFU is `open` and the declaration is blocked.
- **The program's sequence makes the first case the default.** Q-190 is "The Wave 5 Topstep design survey, then the
  MFFU and Lucid surveys", and CLAUDE.md says "Topstep $50K first".
- **No fixed event bounds (c).** My remedy for H-1 put "a firm with no pinned search *at that event*" and "a successor
  may be pinned only before a fixed event, fixed now". The text keeps neither. Its only event is "the third terminal
  state", which is endogenous: with (c) always terminal, it is reached at the first firm's terminal state.
- **The bar is not held by the helper.** In `state_probe.py` (v), a Topstep successor pinned after all three firms
  are terminal is accepted and turns Topstep back to `open`.
- **"Never waited out by choice"** (HT l.85-86) has no deadline, so it cannot be checked.
- **Cause 7, `withdrawn`**, has "its reason in the row" and no time limit (HT l.82). Its source, E-005 (d), is F-01's
  withdrawal clause, so the "closed list" stays open: any search can be closed for any stated reason. That includes
  after its finalists' row. Which of (a) and (b) holds for a search with both rows is unstated; the helper returns (a).
- **"Only a firm's latest pinned search counts. An earlier search's finalists are void once a successor is pinned"**
  (HT l.69). A firm's search-year finalists can be voided after they are seen, at any time before the third terminal
  state.
- **The CHARTER reading.** CHARTER 3.6 (c) and 3.7 say the held-out years are "held until the Topstep, My Funded
  Futures and Lucid searches each have finalists". Counting a never-pinned firm as done is a reading of that sentence.
  E-254 (2) covers only "a firm without finalists keeps its place with none", which is a closed search. No row reads
  "never pinned".

**The failure it allows.**
- **Input:** the queue as written. Topstep's search pins, surveys and writes its finalists' row before MFFU's or
  Lucid's search is pinned.
- **Wrong output:** the template obliges the held-out test with Topstep's finalists alone and refuses MFFU's and
  Lucid's pins until its held-out row. After that row, NQ's held-out years are spent (CHARTER 3.6 (c)). The per-firm
  program's MFFU and Lucid designs can then never be tested on NQ without buying new data.
- **The other branch:** the lead pins MFFU's and Lucid's searches early. Then whether they are in the one test is a
  choice made with Topstep's search-year results in hand, by a researcher whose held-out ledger is not empty. That is
  the selection channel H-1 named, moved from "who closes" to "who pins in time".
- **Through `withdrawn` and the successor-voids rule,** a firm's finalists can be voided or withdrawn after their
  search-year results are seen. Under the alternation this moves every later firm up a place:
  - withdraw MFFU, and Lucid's rank 1 goes from place 3 to place 2;
  - its independence power rises from p_T p_M p_L to p_T p_L.

The level stays valid, because nothing reads the held-out years. What changes is which designs can be carried, which
is the conclusion.

**Remedy (the lead's choice; one sentence each, plus a test).**
1. **Bound (c) by a fixed event, declared now and independent of every design search's outcomes:** "(c) none: no
   search of the firm was pinned **before E**." There are two candidates:
   - **E = the first go row of any firm's search.** All searches entering NQ's test are pinned before any survey
     runs, so membership reads no design outcome. Its cost is that Topstep's survey waits for MFFU's and Lucid's pins,
     and their H4s and W42-R1 applications.
   - **E = a calendar date written now.** This is weaker on the selection channel, but the date reads nothing.

   After E, no search for NQ's held-out test is pinned, a successor included, except a successor to a search closed
   by causes 1-3 (calibration, limit clause, power gate), which read no design outcome. Then "as soon as" is read
   after E.
2. **`withdrawn`:** allowed only before the search's go row, or for a defect named by the failure protocol (an
   identity failure, a runner bug), cited by its record. A search whose finalists' row exists stays (a) unless its
   finalists' token is shown invalid by such a record. State that (a) takes precedence otherwise.
3. **"Only the latest pinned search counts"**: a successor may be pinned only before E (as in 1).
4. **The lead's reading row** of CHARTER 3.6 (c) and 3.7's "held until each firm's search has finalists", covering
   (b) and (c) under E.
5. **TT:** `_firm_states` takes E and refuses a pin after E (other than the causes-1-3 successor) and a withdrawal
   after a go row without a record. Assert that `[pin Topstep, finalists Topstep]` with no MFFU or Lucid pin before E
   gives `c` only if E has passed, and that an empty history does not make the declaration due.

## M-5 (c) (MATERIAL, fixable by wording). The no-free-retry clause re-opens the retry it names

**Where.**
- T l.462-466: "A failed limit clause or power gate is never a free retry ...: the successor inherits W42-R1's limits,
  bankroll budgets, weights and size that matters unchanged; a change comes only by a decision row declared before
  any further search-year read, and is labelled; it changes only what the failure's diagnosis names, citing the
  record; every closed attempt of the line is counted and printed beside the verdict."
- GK l.154-157 is shorter: it omits "changes only what the failure's diagnosis names" and "every closed attempt
  counted".

**Evidence.**
- The decision row is written after the failure is known. A power gate that fails at the size that matters δ has a
  "diagnosis" that names δ, or the power target. So "a change ... only by a decision row" lets the successor raise δ
  until the gate passes.
- Likewise for a failed limit clause: a baseline-equal design fails the limits, and the diagnosis names the limits, so
  the successor loosens them.
- CHARTER 3.9 allows "re-declaring the search". It also says the limits, budgets, weights and the size that matters
  are "set from H4's search-year results by a rule declared before H4 is read". A row written after a gate failure is
  not such a rule.
- W42-R1 (E-250; `WAVE42_SETTING_RULE.md` l.14, l.427-428, l.443) says "if it fails the search does not pin". It
  declares no next option for a failure.
- The test's "bad" needle is `"may re-set"` only. Mutant R1 ("The successor may raise the size that matters") and
  mutant R2 (GK: "the successor sets new limits ...") both survive.

**The failure it allows.**
- **Input:** the gate fails at the lead's δ.
- **Output:** a labelled row raises δ, the successor's gate passes, and exit (B) reports "the line's power" at the
  raised δ. The closed attempt is printed beside it.
- **What it does not touch:** the held-out level. Nothing reads the held-out years.
- **What it does change:** the gate's meaning, and, through re-set limits, pass (a) at the held-out test.

**Remedy (row and template text).** Replace "a change comes only by a decision row declared before any further
search-year read" with:

> "the size that matters, the power target and the limits are never changed after a gate that read them failed,
> except by an option W42-R1 itself declared before H4 was read, which the successor takes; the bankroll budgets and
> the weights only by a decision row declared before any further search-year read, labelled, naming what the
> failure's diagnosis names".

Copy it into GK l.154-157 in full, and needle both copies.

## s-a (MINOR). The walk test's tolerance cannot catch a real bias

**Where.** TT l.883-885: `p2, p1 = _p_stop(37, 55, 2, 1024), _p_stop(37, 55, 1, 4096); assert abs(p2 - p1) < 0.02`.

**Evidence** (`walk_bias.py`, exact DP; every mean zero to 1e-14). The table gives |P(stop) - P(stop) on the one-tick
lattice|:

| case | s, n | the template's walk | barriers rounded to the lattice | rounded inward |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 37/55, sigma√T = 64 (the test's case) | 2, 1024 | 0.0001 | **0.0117** | 0.0081 |
| 37/55, sigma√T = 32.1 | 2, 258 | 0.0003 | **0.0144** | 0.0144 |
| 33/65, sigma√T = 32.1 | 2, 258 | 0.0002 | **0.0163** | 0.0163 |
| 21/43, sigma√T = 32.1 | 2, 258 | 0.0001 | **0.0216** | 0.0212 |
| 37/55, sigma√T = 64.1 | 3, 457 | 0.0002 | 0.0081 | 0.0081 |

Against the quarter-tick lattice, the template's walk is at most 0.0004 off, and E[X²] is within 0.2 %. **The
template's rule is sound.** Its sub-step is unbiased for barriers off the lattice, to well under the coin noise.

**But the test cannot tell it apart from a broken rule:**
- A runner that rounds the barriers to the lattice is the rule T l.312 forbids. It differs by 0.008 to 0.022, and
  most of those cases pass a 0.02 tolerance.
- The test also probes s = 2 only at n = 1024, its finest point. The coarsest n for s = 2 is about 258, just past
  sigma√T = 32.
- `_step` (TT l.847-852) tests the test's own one-line helper, so it cannot fail because of the template.

**Remedy.**
- Use a tolerance of 0.002 at n of about 258 and 1024, with P(target) and E[X²] (relative 1 %) beside.
- Add a negative control: the same DP with rounded barriers must fail it.
- Hold the runner's step function to the same test when it lands (s-c).

## s-b (MINOR). Which T the last row uses

**Where.** T l.310-312: "For the table's last row, T is the limit known at the fill (the fill-time law's T); the
realised exit time decides nothing about s." Against it:
- l.325: "the stopped walk, T to the run's exit time";
- l.340-341: "T ... known at the fill except for the table's last row".

**Evidence.**
- Read as the walk's T, the sentence changes the table's law for unscheduled exits. Read as s's T only, the walk takes
  n = round(sigma² T_exit / s²) with s set from T_fill.
- `lastrow.py`: sigma 8 ticks per root-minute, T_fill 300 minutes, so s = 5.

| T_exit | n | E[X²], s = 5 | E[X²], one tick | target sigma² T |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 min | 1 | 25.0 | 16.0 | 16 |
| 0.5 min | 1 | 25.0 | 32.0 | 32 |
| 1 min | 3 | 75.0 | 63.9 | 64 |
| 2 min | 5 | 123.4 | 125.2 | 128 |

- S-1's coarseness returns in miniature for the share of trades on that row. The effect is small in ticks, but it
  is a variance error that a search can find through convexity.

**Remedy.** Now that nothing is refused, take s from the walk's own T. Reading the realised exit there decides only the
lattice's fineness, never a refusal, and the walk already reads it. Or state the variance error as a declared
deviation with its share printed. Write one T per use.

## What I tried that found nothing

- **The step formula's bound.**
  - s = ceil(r/√W) gives r/s ≤ √W, so round(r²/s²) ≤ W, for integer W. A floating-point tie only coarsens s by one,
    which is harmless.
  - sigma = 0 gives s = 1 and n = 1: a one-tick variance, negligible.
  - Nothing is refused. GK, red-team and merge-checklist carry the formula.
- **The sub-steps' mean.**
  - The down-step of rho with probability s/(s + rho), and the both-barriers step with rho_d/(rho_u + rho_d), are
    mean-zero by hand.
  - In the DP, E[X] is about 1e-14 in every case.
  - At infinite T, P(stop) is exactly g/(l + g), by optional stopping.
- **Look-ahead in the walk.** sigma comes from bars closed at or before the alert (T l.341-342). Under the README's
  open-time stamps, that is the bar stamped t-1 and earlier. s and n read no post-fill bar, except the last row's T,
  which is declared and its share reported.
- **The alternation's validity.**
  - It is a deterministic function of the pinned lists, fixed before any held-out read.
  - Fixed-sequence testing at the full level controls the family-wise error for any such order.
  - Intersection-union per finalist across its own baselines is unchanged.
  - A long list in one firm no longer delays another firm's rank 1 (`_joint_order` cases, TT l.1180-1186).
- **The place-power formula.**
  - The product under independence is labelled as such.
  - The Fréchet lower bound max(0, Σp - (i-1)) is the correct bound for an intersection, and it decides nothing.
  - Positive dependence (the shared held-out history) puts the true value above the product, so neither number
    overstates power under positive dependence.
- **k.** One k, assigned at F-HT's held-out row, which is now its token row, so rule 1 enforces the order. "No repeat
  row" is held in HT and GK. A search's withdrawal before that row spends nothing. No search holds a k.
- **The joint runner** (m-4): the run is refused without an identity reproducing each search's search-year outputs
  path for path (mutant X2 is killed).
- **C-1 and m-1.** `_slot`'s four boundaries match T l.292-296. "Kept alive" and "not replaced" are refused by
  needles.
- **Reading NQ from 2026-07-17.** No new text touches the confirmation window. It is read only for a carried design,
  and is a purchase (HT l.141; T l.100-101, l.670).
