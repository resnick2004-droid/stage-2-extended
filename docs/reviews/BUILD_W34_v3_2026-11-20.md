### rm3-a (MINOR). The "exact" law assumes exchangeable noise *across designs*, which common coin seeds break; the size rises to at most k * P(t > c)

**Where:** `extended/plateau.py:31-44` (the docstring); `RHO = 0.5`.

**Evidence:**
- The derivation is checked and right. Conditional on the re-read vector, the k paired t-statistics are independent.
  Each is (Z - delta) / sqrt(X / (n - 1)), where delta ~ N(0, 1) and X is noncentral chi-square on n - 1 degrees of
  freedom with noncentrality chi-square on n - 1.
- "Exchangeable" carries the weight: every pair of designs must have equal variance and equal correlation.
- The same coin seed across designs, as in a seed-keyed redraw, makes the noise a field that decays with lever
  distance. Two neighbours on opposite sides of the finalist are then further from each other than from it, so
  corr(D_i, D_j) = 1 - (1 - r_ij) / (2 (1 - r)) < 1/2.
- `field.py` measures this at n = 16, k = 10, five axes, step / length-scale 0.5:

| nugget share | corr(D), opposite | corr(D), orthogonal | accepted at the exact c | accepted at the Bonferroni t |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | +0.50 | +0.50 | 0.9492 | 0.9610 |
| 0.5 | +0.38 | +0.45 | 0.9469 | 0.9590 |
| 0.1 | -0.10 | +0.27 | 0.9390 | 0.9538 |

- Under any dependence the size is at most k * P(t_15 > 2.796), which is about 0.068.

**The failure it allows:** true plateaus are refused up to about 1.4 times the declared rate. This costs power on good
finalists ("check power of strict rules"). It never lets a spike through.

**Remedy** (take one):
- Record beside `size_at` the bound that holds under any dependence, k * (1 - F_t(c)).
- Or declare the Bonferroni t quantile t_{n-1}(1 - size / k), which is valid under any dependence (2.947 against
  2.796 here).
- Or estimate the k x k correlation of the paired differences from the re-read itself and report it with the verdict.

Either way, state the exchangeability assumption beside the plateau verdict.

### rm3-b (MINOR). The quadrature is not exact at small n and large k, and nothing sets a floor on the re-read seeds

**Where:**
- `extended/plateau.py:96`: `_QM = 128` probability midpoints, which cut off both tails of X.
- `:142`: the `brentq` bracket stops at 60.
- `extended/search_space.py:580`: the floor on re-read seeds is 2.

**Evidence** (`law.py`, `law2.py`; simulated acceptance with an SE of about 0.0005-0.0009, against 0.95):

| n \ k | 10 | 30 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 0.9477 | 0.9439 | |
| 4 | 0.9483 | 0.9449 | |
| 16 | 0.9502 | 0.9485 | 0.9481 |

- At n = 2, k = 10 the acceptance is 0.9483.
- At n = 2, k >= 30 (and somewhere above 10), `critical_value` raises a bare `ValueError`, not `PlateauRefused`.
- At `_QM = 1024`, n = 4, k = 30 gives 0.9502, so the error is the quadrature's, not the derivation's.
- At the declared 16 seeds and k = 10 the error is nil, and the test band (+-0.012) is honest there.

**The failure it allows:** a declaration with few re-read seeds and many neighbours refuses true plateaus at a size
of up to about 0.056.

**Remedy:**
- A floor on re-read seeds in `begin` (16, the test's `SIZE_N`, or a declared one).
- A finer or tail-aware quadrature (`_QM` of 1024, or `scipy.integrate.quad` over X).
- A widened bracket that refuses with `PlateauRefused`.
- The law test extended to the declaration's largest k.
