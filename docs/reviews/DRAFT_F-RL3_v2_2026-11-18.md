## 2. The limit twin (the brief's point 2)

### 2.1 What F-04's two findings were, and why RL3's structure differs

**F-04 B-1.** P-14's signal chooses a side, and the order type is fixed: a limit at the alert price. The walked
mirror of a resting buy is a sell entered as the price falls through P. In effect it is a sell stop, which a
coin-sided *limit* could never have filled. So the comparison mixes order types, and the mixture has a sign at the
tick scale.

**F-04 NB-1.** The exact twin `x_i = s_i n_i / 2` on the resting side's fills fixes that, but leaves the supply term
`(p - 1/2) m`. Here `p` is the share of resting fills on the signal's side, and it is set by the price move during the
latency.

**RL3 is a different object.** At each level, RL3's grid ties the side to the order type: `fade` is a limit at the
level, and `break` is the opposite side as a stop one tick beyond it. The walker's rules (`fill_order`, lines 315-331)
give the following.
- **The same bar.** A fade buy limit at a support P fills on the first bar from `live` whose low is at most P - 1 tick.
  A break sell stop at P - 1 tick fills on the first bar whose low is at most P - 1 tick: the same bar. The same holds
  for a resistance.
- **The same prices.** Under symmetric booking, the fade's mirror is booked at 2 x trigger - P, which is one tick below
  the print a tick through: the break stop's own fill at one tick of slippage (`mirror_price`, lines 514-524). The text
  says the same in its own terms: break and fade at `rn100` are "near-exact negatives under symmetric booking" (5.6).
- **The only exceptions** are:
  - the void boundary: a reference exactly at P voids the fade but not the break;
  - a gap bar: the fade fills at P and the walked mirror at 2 x open - P, against the break's open less a tick.

  Both are countable.

So **the fade's walked mirror is the break order at the same level**: realisable, placed at the same time from the
same information, and filled on the same bar. A fair coin choosing at the ticket between the family's two resting
orders at that level gives the fade's pair term exactly. That is the CHARTER's coin twin ("the same entries with a fair
coin choosing the side, so supply, cost and schedule cancel"):
- **supply cancels:** the same fill bar;
- **schedule cancels:** the same ticket and the same `t_live`;
- **cost cancels:** both legs are booked a tick off the same trigger.

F-04 B-1's ground for rejecting the walked mirror ("a stop a coin could not have filled") does not hold here.

### 2.2 Are RL3's C1 and C2 open to NB-1's supply bias?

**As declared (the walked mirror, the pair rule, symmetric booking for C1, rung 1 for C2): no.**
- Every fill has its mirror on the same bar, and both legs are walked, or both are zeroed by the pair rule. So there is
  no resting-side share `p` and no term `(p - 1/2) m`.
- Costs sit symmetrically about the trigger.
- The latency decides only which tickets are live (the void at `t_live`). It does not decide which leg of a pair
  trades.
- Conditioning on the rule's own fill is conditioning on the break's fill, which is the same bar, so it introduces no
  selection between the legs.

**What the walked pair is still open to: F-04 B-1's measurement part.** That is print noise at bar extremes.
- A trade-through by one tick is often the bar's extreme print. Under a bid-ask bounce, the trigger sits about half a
  tick below the mid for a buy. Symmetric booking then hands the fade about a tick on each barrier against the break.
- This is a fill-model artefact, not direction. It is exactly what F-02's T-13 (b) ladder exists for: symmetric booking
  moves to rung 1 where T-13 (b)'s null interval lies on the fade's side, then rung 2, then a kill (5.5; F-02 lines
  363-376). F-02 states that under its generator "symmetric booking leans toward `fade` and against `break`".
- **So C1 and C2 for fade are protected to the extent that T-13 (b)'s generator represents NQ's print noise** (SF-4).

**A true mean reversion of the price, if NQ has one at these crossings, is not a bias.** It is a real reversal, and a
user can bank it with realisable orders on both legs. At `pd`, `on` and `pdc` it is in the estimand the text declares
("a reversal after these crossings", with no level claim; 9.8). At `rn100` C1' differences it out, with placebo
crossings that share the mechanics, before any level claim (9.8).

### 2.3 C1' or "described only"?

**Neither is needed for E-008's trades-first verdict.**
- C1 on the walked pair is E-008's lift over a realisable coin twin, on the same fills.
- Making C1' decisive for every fade cell would be a test of a level claim the text does not make at `pd`, `on` and
  `pdc`. It would not be valid there either: the placebo matches neither distance nor path at those kinds (G1, 5.8).
- At `rn100`, C1' already decides the level claim (9.8).
- Describing the fade cells only would treat a realisable twin as if it were F-04's unrealisable one.

**E-203's exact twin is where NB-1 bites, maximally (the blocker, NB-1 below).** For a fade ticket, the resting side
at `t_live` *is* the fade side whenever the ticket is not void. Hence:
- on the non-void tickets, `s_i = +1` always: `p = 1`, `lhat = m / 2`;
- with the void tickets in the offer population, `p` is the non-void share, set by the opening's moves, and
  `lhat = p (m+ - m) + (p - 1/2) m` is dominated by the second term.

In C1's win-probability units `m` is the resting fills' win rate, which is positive, so that "lift" is positive with
no direction at all. In net R it is the limit trades' own mean after costs. Either way it is a profitability reading,
not a lift.

### 2.4 Does T-13's synthetic walk include the case that bites?

T-13 (b) is F-02's 17 item 5 (b), carried at line 1209. Its walk:
- a martingale mid, one tick a step, 400 steps a minute;
- rare jumps of 2 to 8 ticks;
- a **one-tick bid-ask print bounce** (the print at the ask after an up-move, at the bid after a down-move).

Its tickets:
- live every five minutes;
- a level 20 to 60 ticks from **the close of the bar before live**, above or below by a fair coin.

So, item by item:
- **Mean-reverting prints: yes, but at one tick only.** It contains the case that bites the walked pair's booking.
  Transient extreme prints of more than one tick, at which a one-tick trade-through is triggered with the mid
  unmoved, are not in it (SF-4).
- **A mean-reverting mid: no.** For RL3's walked pair this is direction, not a bias (2.2), so its absence is correct
  for a test of the mirror's zero.
- **A latency during which the resting side is set: no.** The level is set from the bar before live, so there is no
  latency and no window. For the walked pair the latency creates no supply term (2.2), so the specific F-04 mechanism
  cannot arise. But the switch is decided on a ticket population whose distances at live are unlike RL3's: no
  information 30 + L minutes old, no void filter, no near-price levels at live (SF-4).

## 3. Findings

### NB-1 (blocker). E-203's exact twin is degenerate for RL3, yet the text offers it to the lead as C1 for fade and reads it beside

**Where:** 19 item 4 (lines 1352-1360), 9.7 (lines 845-846), 5.5 (lines 359-360).

**Evidence.** As section 2.3 shows, on the fade's offers the signal's side is the resting side by construction.
- `p` is one, or the non-void share.
- The exact twin's lift is the supply term plus `p` times nothing: `lhat = (p - 1/2) m` with `m+ = m`.
- In win units `m` is positive.

So the alternative 19 item 4 offers ("the exact twin as C1's construction for the `fade` cells") would pass C1 at
0.05/210 with no direction in any outcome. And 9.7's beside reading "the walked mirror against E-203's exact twin"
compares a lift with a profitability figure, under a heading that calls both a lift.

The text's framing invites the lead to apply E-203's general sentence ("for a limit entry the walked mirror is not the
coin twin ... a limit set without the exact twin is described only"). Applied literally, that sentence either makes
the fade cells "described only" for a reason that does not hold here (2.1), or replaces C1 with a construction that
cannot fail.

**Failure it allows.**
- If the alternative is adopted, a fade pick with no reversal at all is carried.
- If E-203's sentence is applied, the fade cells are retired on a defect they do not have.

**Remedy.**
- Strike the alternative from 19 item 4.
- Replace it with section 2.1's argument, stated as the reason RL3 falls outside E-203's F-04 ruling: the fade's
  mirror is the realisable break stop at the same level, from the same ticket, on the same bar, at the break's own
  fill price under symmetric booking. Ask the lead to rule that.
- Drop 9.7's exact-twin reading, or relabel it "the resting fills' own mean (supply and profitability, not a lift)".
- Add to T-4 a check on synthetic bars that the fade's fill bar equals the break stop's at the same level, with the
  void-boundary and gap exceptions counted, and K4's count of those exceptions per (cell, R) at stage A.

### SF-1. The alert at 09:00 is stipulated, and the dominant policy is dropped without a record

**Evidence.**

- A carried pick at 5 minutes is a policy a user would rationally replace by the untested 09:00 one.
- Read loosely, the latency answer ("a shorter delay helps by X") misstates the operator's question for pre-session
  tickets, where no delay need bind.

**Remedy.** Either way, the second text's latency design otherwise stands as the lead ruled it; the choice between
these two is the lead's.

### SF-3. The 90 paired readings: the judged block's exposure and their multiplicity

**Where:** 9.7 (lines 825-834), 18.1 (`stage_C_latency_beside`, line 1260), 5.9's table (line 451), 16 item 5.

**Evidence.**
1. **Exposure.** `stage_C_latency_beside` reads the judged fills and walks of every (rule-kind, stop, R) whose
   siblings passed stage B's eligibility. That is up to every latency of every eligible (rule-kind, stop, R), not just
   the pick's. `stage_C_judgement` already reads the pick's two siblings, and E-202 (2)(c) asks only for "the
   five-minute cell reported beside every pick".
   - The new purpose makes the judged block read for the whole eligible grid.
   - 13's closure clause ("a later family on these levels ... is judged on data this family did not read") then leaves
     a successor with no unread development block at any of those (kind, stop, R).
   - F-02's re-declaration into RL3 was possible only because F-02 read nothing.
2. **Multiplicity.** Each of up to 90 differences carries its own nominal one-sided 0.05 bound. Some will clear it by
   chance, and 13 says "only the paired difference speaks to whether a shorter delay helps".
3. **The bound is valid as a reading.** `two_sample_bound` builds its variance from the within-block difference of
   residuals (`diff_stats`, lines 345-372; the module's note says it "holds whether the two samples are disjoint ... or
   nested"), so overlapping siblings are handled. This is not a defect.

**Failure it allows.**
- The operator could read a chance difference among 90 as "a shorter delay helps".
- The judged block is consumed for cells never judged.

**Remedy.**
- Either restrict the judged-block differences to the pick's (rule-kind, stop, R), which `stage_C_judgement` already
  reads, and keep the grid-wide differences on the choice block only (8 item 8);
- or keep the purpose, but state the exposure cost in 13 and 2, and add beside the 90 a family-wise reading
  (Benjamini-Hochberg at 10 %, or a Bonferroni count). Label each one "a reading, not a finding".

The lead chooses.
