## 2. The new material, point by point

### 2.1 T-4's same-bar check (17 item 4, lines 1217-1220; 7.2 item 7, lines 721-722)

**What the walker does.** I checked `fill_order` and `mirror_price` against the text's claim.
- **Same bar.** A fade buy limit at a support P fills on the first bar from `live` whose low is at most P - 1 tick. A
  break sell stop at P - 1 tick fills on the first bar whose low reaches P - 1 tick. The condition is identical, so the
  bar is identical. The same holds at a resistance.
- **Same price, off gap bars.** The fade's trigger is P - 1 tick, so the symmetric mirror is 2 x (P - 1 tick) - P,
  which is P - 2 ticks. The break's fill is P - 1 tick less one tick of slippage, also P - 2 ticks. Identical.
- **The void boundary.** The fade is void at a reference of P or below; the break at P - 1 tick or below. On the tick
  grid the only difference is a reference exactly at P, as the text says.
- **A gap bar opening below P - 1 tick.** Both orders fill **on that same bar**. Only the price differs: the walked
  mirror is booked at 2 x open - P, the break at open - 1 tick.

**So the check is right in substance.** Its exception list mixes a bar exception with a price exception (note 1).

### 2.2 T-13 (b)'s two generators, their rate and their population (17 item 5; 6.1 line 584)

**Population.**
- 20 levels a day, at 4 to 400 ticks from the 08:29 close;
- the window walked but unworked;
- the void at the 08:59 close; live from 09:00; cutoff 14:00;
- 9,000 days per (side rule, stop).

This is RL3's own timing, as SF-4 asked. The rate of transient prints (0.01 a step, 2 or 3 ticks beyond the mid) is
declared and labelled the drafter's choice. The switch reads the stricter generator.

**The risk I tested.** A rate this high puts several transient prints in every minute. It could make even rung 2
favour the fade under the null. The ladder would then kill every fade cell by construction, before any bar is read.
That is the repository's memory point: a strict rule must show power, not only size.

**The synthetic check.** I ran a reduced copy of both generators through the walker's own `fill_order`, `mirror_price`
and `walk_pair` (`fill_bar="pair"`): live 09:00, levels at a fair-coin side, 50 and 150 ticks, R = 1, symmetric, rung 1
and rung 2. I simplified it: transients at 1.5 or 2.5 ticks beyond a half-tick mid to stay on the grid, distances 4 to
60 ticks, and no window walk.

- **It found no kill by construction.** Under the heavier generator rung 2 did not lean toward the fade. If anything,
  the rungs favoured the mirror.
- **Under the base generator,** symmetric booking leaned toward the fade, which is F-02's N1. So the ladder will likely
  move C1 to rung 1 at some stops. That is conservative and is the ladder working as designed.
- I give no figures (this repository types none). The size of any switch is the build's to report.

**The heavier generator's wording is ambiguous** (note 2).

### 2.3 The void at 09:00 reading the 08:59 close (4.2, 4.5; 3; 4.8 (b); 17 item 3 (b))

- `live` = `slot(9, 0)`, so `price_at(live)` is the close of the last bar with index at most `live`: the bar stamped
  08:59, closed at 09:00. Fills come only from the bar stamped 09:00 on (index `live` + 1).
- So the void uses only the price at submission, which any order placed at 09:00:00 meets. It is realisable and
  outcome-free.
- 4.8 (b) and 17 item 3 (b) hold it unchanged under replacing every bar from 09:00.
- It is the one read of a price inside the window, and section 3 discloses it next to the narrower sentence.
- Like any order's marketability check, it lets the opening half-hour select which tickets are live (4.3's N-4 is
  stated). It is not a rule defined on the opening range.

**No defect.**
