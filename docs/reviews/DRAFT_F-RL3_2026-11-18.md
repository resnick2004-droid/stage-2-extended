## Blockers

### B-1. The pre-pin power gate is not lenient. At the ceiling it is at its strictest, and its factor-3 drop has no source and no power shown

**Where:** 7.1 (lines 532-564), header condition 2 (lines 28-30), 6.1's `power gate` row (line 473), 13's NOT PINNED (line 918).

**Evidence.**
1. **Direction.** The gate puts both inputs at their ceiling: the supply (every ticket fills on every one of 657
   sessions) and the uses (u = the judged block's sessions a year, capped only by the records count). The text
   then says it is "lenient by construction" (lines 560-561). Only one of the two errs toward a pass:
   - more supply lowers MDL80 roughly as 1/sqrt(n);
   - more uses raise the value per point of lift, so λ_bar (the lift worth $500 a year) falls.

   Section 10 values a lift as λ × Σ u_p v(p) / 100, with u_p filled in priority order. So λ_bar = 50,000 / V(u),
   with V concave and increasing, and uses grow with fills. The ratio the gate tests is therefore
   MDL80 / λ_bar ∝ V(u) / sqrt(u). That ratio *rises* with supply wherever V is close to linear in u, which is
   wherever the highest-valued place's cap is not yet full. The vendored value map's `place_values` rows
   (`trades_per_year_records`) put the top places' records counts above the judged block's sessions a year for both
   bases, at least at the R those places ask for. So the uses bind before the caps, V is linear there, and **the
   ceiling is where the ratio is largest**.
2. **Inconsistent with the text's own stage A.** Take a `pd` fade cell whose realistic fill rate is a quarter of the
   ceiling. At its realistic supply its MDL80 roughly doubles, while its uses, and so its λ_bar, are roughly
   quartered. Its ratio is therefore about half its ceiling ratio. If its ceiling ratio lies between 3 and 6, the gate
   drops it at the pin, yet 7.2 item 8's own planning, at the cell's realistic supply, would not even call it a screen.
3. **Provenance of the 3.** The text calls it "R-16's factor" (line 555, 6.1). But R-16 (`docs/audits/phase0/D_protocol.md`
   lines 445-446, 522-523) names "a declared factor" with no value, and its remedy is "declared as a screen, or pooled",
   not dropped.
   - F-B uses 3 only as a **label**: a pass is still carried (F-B draft lines 488-493, 973-974). F-B also dropped a
     different factor 3 for "no derivation or source" (lines 662, 1094).
   - F-02's seventh text, which this text says it keeps, said "R-16 is not adopted, and is not applied" (F-02 line 649).
   - The precedent cited for dropping, E-086, dropped family R's cells on an **empty** MDL80, not on a factor.

   So the drop at 3 × λ_bar is a new choice, made by the drafter, with no outside source. Section 0's change table
   does not record it as a change.
4. **No power shown for the gate itself** (the memory `check-power-of-strict-rules`; E-051). A cell whose
   MDL80 = 4 λ_bar can still pass C1 and C2 if its true lift is about λ_bar + MDL80. Nothing shows that lifts of that
   size are implausible for these designs, or how often a dropped cell would have passed.
5. **rho is undefined at the gate.** For 50-tick cells, λ_bar needs rho (section 10), which needs 6.3's sizing over the
   records leaf-days. That is more than "the vendored value map" (line 537) supplies.
6. **The MDL does not vary by cell.** It is read from P-16's 150-tick 1R toys by size alone. So the gate sorts cells
   only through λ_bar (the stop's base and rho, R's places) and `pdc`'s lower ceiling. That is disclosed in part
   (line 563). At R = 3 the 1R toy overstates the pair term's variance, which makes the gate stricter again.

**Failure it allows.** The gate drops a profitable (cell, R), or closes the whole family before its pin ("takes no k,
nothing is read"), because of an inequality evaluated at the one point where it is hardest to meet. That is exactly
the case of a strict rule failing a profitable model.

**Remedy (either).**
- **(a)** At the pin, drop only an empty or infinite MDL80 at the ceiling (E-086's form). Keep the factor 3 as stage
  A's screen **label** (7.2 item 8), as F-B and R-16 use it.
- **(b)** If a factor-based drop is kept:
  - evaluate the minimum, over toy sizes z at or below the ceiling, of MDL80(z) / λ_bar(u(z)), where u(z) is the uses
    implied by z fills;
  - state rho at the gate (or use 1 and say which way that errs);
  - call the factor the drafter's choice;
  - show its cost before the pin on a light synthetic computation (no bar): the share of passes it removes at planted
    lifts from λ_bar to a few MDL80.

  Either way, add the change to section 0's table.

### B-2. The latency variants: nine cells are three cells counted three times, and elsewhere the variants are nested populations, not delays

**Where:** 4.2 (lines 179-194), 5.1 (lines 245-251), 5.9 and the counted-cells table (lines 354-390), 6.2 (lines 499-506),
8 item 7 (lines 645-649), 9.7 (lines 714-718), 16 item 3.

**Evidence.**
1. **`fade on` is the same tickets at every L in stages A to C:**
   - its levels come from bars ending 08:29 (5.1), and its side is fixed by construction (4.4, lines 204-206). The text
     itself says so at lines 250-251;
   - the void is read at the 08:59 close for every L (4.5, line 209);
   - the cutoff, K1, K2, K5, the side checks, K0's counts and the stage-B statistic all come from identical fills.

   Only the placebo (beside for fade) and stage D's re-writes ("live L minutes later") differ. So 9 of the 45 cells are
   3 cells counted three times. Six counted cells test nothing, and the operator's question gets no answer at `on`.
   Stage B's tie rule (equal value, then more fills, then table order with 5 minutes first) always resolves them to
   L = 5. Line 187 ("the variants differ only in what the ticket was written from: a later `P_ref`, and so possibly a
   different side or `rn100` multiple") is false for `on`.
2. **At `pd`, `pdc` and `rn100` the variants are nested.**
   - On a day when no level or multiple is crossed between the 08:54 close and the 08:57 close, all three tickets are
     identical.
   - On a crossing day, the 5-minute ticket at the crossed level is on the old side. At 09:00 it is marketable, and so
     void, unless the price re-crossed by 08:59. The 2-minute ticket is written after the crossing, on the other side,
     and lives: it is a limit at a level just broken inside the opening half-hour, waiting for a retest.
   - So the 2-minute cell is roughly the 5-minute cell's tickets plus retest tickets, and the 3-minute cell lies
     between them. "2 against 5" compares two populations of different trade types. It does not measure the cost of a
     delay.
3. **Stage B's pick leans toward the shorter latencies whatever the lift.** The pick is the largest valued lift at
   L_C2 - q·se (8 item 7). The 2-minute cell has more fills (fewer voids), so at equal per-fill lift it has more uses
   (more dollars) and a smaller se. A pick at 2 or 3 minutes is therefore not evidence that a shorter delay helps. 9.7
   reports the siblings' lifts but declares no paired difference.
4. **Which question the design answers.** The variants move the alert (`t_pub` = 09:00 - L) and keep the order time
   fixed at 09:00. E-202 (2)(c)'s question is whether the delay from alert to order costs results. 6.2 (lines 503-504)
   rejects a `t_live` that moves with L only in its form inside the window (`t_pub` 08:25). It does not consider the
   form wholly outside the window: information at the 08:59 bar's close, the order live at 09:00 + L (09:05, 09:03,
   09:02). That is the form in which a shorter delay can catch a touch, or avoid a void, that the longer one misses at
   the same information.
5. **Nothing chosen after a look.** The values 5, 3 and 2 are the operator's (E-202 (2)(c)). `t_live` is forced by the
   window. I found no choice made after a read.

**Failure it allows.**
- The pinned count claims 45 cells, each testing latency, when 6 are duplicates.
- A carried pick at 2 minutes would be reported as "a shorter delay helps" when the evidence is a supply difference,
  or a retest sub-population inside the closed opening-range branch (see S-2).

**Remedy.**
1. Declare `on` free of latency in stages A to C: one cell per stop, so 39 cells and 117 signed hypotheses. Recompute
   the table and T-0. Alternatively, keep the 45 with an explicit statement that 6 duplicate the 5-minute cells and are
   counted only.
2. Declare beside, deciding nothing:
   - the lift on the tickets the pick does not share with its 5-minute sibling, with their share;
   - the paired sibling difference with its one-sided bound on the judged calendar.
3. Record a lead's ruling under 19 that names which question the cells answer: fixed order time (as drafted), fixed
   information time (`t_live` = 09:00 + L), or both as counted arms. The choice belongs to the operator's question, so
   the lead records it before the pin. The text must say which one it answers.

### B-3. Positions open at a news window "walk on", against E-202 (2)(a)

**Where:** 3's news row (line 162: "a ticket that filled before a window walks on (the record's rule ...)"), 4.6
(lines 215-218), 4.7 (d) (lines 231-232), 11.2's EIA rule (line 815), T-4 (line 1028).

**Evidence.** E-202 (2)(a), the operator's ruling "for every family and protocol from now", reads: "no entry and no

verified release in every stage. That is contrary to the operator's ruling, and it is pinned.

- Keep "cancel an unfilled ticket at the start, re-write it at the end".
- Say that the EIA releases (not a verified window) are outside the ruling, or bring them under it explicitly.
- T-4 holds one toy case each way.

### B-4. An unscheduled FOMC statement cancels tickets five minutes before an unannounced release (look-ahead)

**Where:** 4.7 (d), lines 229-232; T-4 (line 1031).

**Evidence.**
- The list the draft cites (`atlas_a3.yaml` `news.fomc` → `config/sources/fomc_statements_2020_2024q1.csv`) holds the
  unscheduled statement dated 2020-03-03, "For release at 10:00 a.m. EST", which is 09:00 CT.
- The rule cancels every resting ticket at 08:55 CT. That is at or before every `t_pub` and before `t_live`, so the
  whole day leaves the choice block (stage A's cutoffs, K2 and K5, and stage B) on knowledge no one had at 08:55.
- F-02's third-text review (`docs/reviews/DRAFT_F-02_resting_levels_v3_2026-11-16.md`, N6, lines 102 and 426-428)
  named this date and its remedy: "unscheduled ones only from their release time". The remedy was not adopted.
- The shift test (4.8; 17 item 3) cannot catch it, because it is a calendar, not a bar.

**Failure it allows.** One of the most volatile sessions in the choice block (an emergency cut at the moment tickets go
live) is removed by a rule that uses the future. That shifts the cutoff and resolution reads, and stage B's statistics,
in a direction nobody can sign beforehand.

**Remedy.** An unscheduled statement acts only from its release minute:
- an unfilled ticket is cancelled at the release bar;
- a filled one is treated by B-3's rule (or walks on, if the lead rules that an unscheduled statement is not a
  verified window, and says so);
- T-4 includes a toy release at 09:00.

### S-2. The "differ" tickets of `P_ref` are close to ORB6's closed limit at the broken edge
